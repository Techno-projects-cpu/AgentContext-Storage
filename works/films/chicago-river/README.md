# The City That Reversed Its River

A 1:24 documentary short about Chicago, 1900: the city dumped its sewage into the river, the river
ran into the lake it drank from, so the city cut a canal 28 miles west and made the river run the
other way.

Made with the [`cinematic-skill`](../../../skills/cinematic-skill/README.md) engine: one continuous
camera over one board, every stamp, pin, counter and underline landing on the narrator's word.

## The script

Twenty-one lines, 226 words, written to `script.json` with a `fact_notes` list of every number and
claim. `beats.md` is the timed beat table; `make_beats.py` lints chronology, line length and digits
in spoken text and returns zero warnings.

The spine: the stinking river (1-2) → the canal west and the eight-foot divide (3-6) → the dig:
eight years, 42 million cubic yards, more earth than Panama (7-9) → the dam broken by hand at
Kedzie Avenue at dawn, 2 January 1900 (10-12) → Missouri v. Illinois, 1906, and the untreated
waste (13-17) → still reversed, still carrying the waste, now with electric carp barriers (18-21).

## The look

The board is one printed map sheet of the Chicago region, built from local atlas data: US states
(us-atlas), Canada and Mexico (world-atlas), the Great Lakes (Natural Earth 10m lakes) and the
rivers (Natural Earth 10m rivers), all projected to one Albers conic and printed in period ink.
Everything else is pinned onto that sheet where its subject is: the section through the divide at
Summit, the counters over the Illinois interior, the Sanitary District report near Kedzie, the
docket and the Holmes line across the Missouri border.

The map is generated by `tools/build_map.py` and `tools/lake.py` (both call the skill's projection
code); `map.json` documents the frame. Type is sized with `s2w()` so it stays phone-legible at every
zoom, and the spotlight is kept wider than the frame so the map never goes black.

## Rebuild

The checked-in `assets/prep/` already holds the projected sheet, so a rebuild can skip the two
builders; they need the atlas files unpacked from npm (`us-atlas`, `world-atlas`,
`@geo-maps/earth-rivers-10m`, `@geo-maps/earth-lakes-10m`) into `assets/data/`.

```bash
python3 tools/build_map.py                     # states, rivers, frame
python3 tools/lake.py                          # Great Lakes rings
python3 /path/to/cinematic-film/scripts/make_beats.py --project .
python3 /path/to/cinematic-film/scripts/split_takes.py --project .   # audio/takes/*.wav
python3 /path/to/cinematic-film/scripts/build_narration.py --project .
python3 /path/to/cinematic-film/scripts/prep_assets.py --project .
python3 /path/to/cinematic-film/scripts/render.py --project . --cues
python3 /path/to/cinematic-film/scripts/build_sfx.py --project .
python3 /path/to/cinematic-film/scripts/mix_soundtrack.py --project .
python3 /path/to/cinematic-film/scripts/render.py --project . --size 720x1280 \
        --out out/final.mp4 --audio audio/mix.m4a --workers 2 --crf 20
```

Sandbox notes: `LD_LIBRARY_PATH=/tmp:/home/user/.local/lib` for the GL stub, `PATH=/tmp/fbin` for
ffmpeg/ffprobe, `PYTHONPATH` or an absolute script path (the scripts resolve paths against their own
directory). Fonts: Oswald bold as `fonts/cond.ttf`, EB Garamond as `fonts/serif.ttf`.
