# The Loudest Sound — Krakatoa, 27 August 1883

A 112 second vertical short (1080x1920, 30 fps) made with the
[`cinematic-skill` engine](https://github.com/Moh4696/cinematic-skill), pushed past what the earlier
films asked of it: two printed hydrographic sheets on one board, a live eruption and shock rings
drawn in code, 1883 archive plates, and a finish graded for a phone screen.

## The film

At 10:02 on 27 August 1883 the third and last of Krakatoa's great explosions tore the island apart.
It is the loudest sound in recorded history: heard on Rodrigues, nearly 3,000 miles away, as "the
distant roar of heavy guns"; and its air wave was logged by barographs around the world, at many
stations seven times in five days, as it ran round the planet and back the other way. What killed
people was not the blast. It was the sea: waves up to a hundred feet high, and 36,417 dead on the
official Dutch count, most of them drowned.

## What is on the board

- **The strait sheet** — the Sunda Strait at Natural Earth 10 m coastlines (mercator), Krakatoa at
  the origin, with the 40 mile ring, the lithograph plate, the Batavia gasometer trace, a measured
  100 ft water column and the caldera drawn over the water the island left.
- **The world chart** — equirectangular, for the second half: Rodrigues, the London and Washington
  barographs, the seven passes of the air wave drawn as rings around the source, the column against
  the stratosphere, a Chelsea afterglow drawn in code (after William Ascroft's pastel sketches),
  New York's fire engines, the cooling, and the telegraph.

## The picture

The stock engine is built for paper on a board; this film extends it (see `tools/filmkit.py`):

- `Plume` — fire and ash as particles, each a pure function of `t`, so frames stay reproducible
- `RingWave` — expanding shock fronts and the nested passes of the air wave
- `Glow` — light that pulses at the vent
- `AtlasSheet` — a sheet drawn from scratch: toned paper, deep water, relief land, graticule,
  distance rings, compass, scale bar, corner ticks
- `ColumnCard` — a cut-away plate of the eruption column, keyed to the tropopause and 80 km
- `SunsetPlate` — the afterglow pillar and the Thames, drawn as bands and lobes in pastel colours
- `Dip` — a dip to near-black that hides the one camera move the board cannot make in a shot
- a `Renderer.post` replacement: a 3x3 grade, full-resolution grain, and a glow that still reads at
  1080 wide

Type: Oswald (condensed), Instrument Serif and Cormorant Garamond (serif), all open-licensed and in
`fonts/`.

## Sources

- Royal Society, *The Eruption of Krakatoa and Subsequent Phenomena* (1888) — the air wave, the
  station records, the sound at Rodrigues
- Smithsonian Global Volcanism Program, Krakatau; USGS
- The plate: the Royal Society chromolithograph of the eruption (1888), public domain.
- William Ascroft's pastel sketches of the Chelsea afterglow, 26 November 1883: the film's Chelsea
  plate is a drawn reconstruction in the same colours, credited on screen as "after William Ascroft".

No decibel figure is quoted in the film on purpose: no instrument in 1883 could measure one, the
nearest gauge was driven off its chart, and 194 dB is the ceiling for a sound wave in air. The
popular "310 dB" is not a measurement.

## Rebuild

```bash
pip install skia-python numpy pillow           # skia needs libGL/libEGL; stubs are enough headless
python3 tools/build_fine.py                    # sheet data from assets/data (Natural Earth)
python3 render.py --project . --probe 8,20,62  # stills
python3 tools/build_narration_local.py --project .   # only if audio/lines/*.mp3 are new
python3 render.py --project . --cues && python3 build_sfx.py --project .
python3 mix_soundtrack.py --project . --sfx-db -2
python3 render.py --project . --size 1080x1920 --out out/final.mp4 --audio audio/mix.m4a --crf 18
```
