# Mock B — Think like a researcher 🔬⚔️

30 original practice questions; **not an official CCMB test or a prediction of its format**. Suggested practice: 40–50 minutes, one point each, no penalty. Questions 1–24 are MCQs, 25–30 short answers. The goal is evidence-based reasoning, not speed alone. All examples are hypothetical; do not culture microbes or perform unsafe experiments at home. [Start page](README.md)

## Questions

1. A student asks whether light affects seedlings. Which is the best test? A) Compare similar groups given different light but otherwise similar conditions B) Guess from the colour of a pot. C) Give different species different light and water D) Use one plant with no comparison
2. In question 1, if seedling height is measured, height is the: A) hypothesis. B) dependent variable C) controlled variable D) independent variable
3. Why assign similar seedlings randomly to two groups? A) To prove the hypothesis true B) To avoid all error forever C) To reduce systematic selection bias D) To eliminate the need for comparison.
4. A study measures growth of one seedling per treatment. Its main limitation is: A) growth cannot be measured B) scientific tests never compare groups C) plants have no cells D) individual variation can strongly affect the result.
5. Group A grows 2, 3, 4 cm. Its mean growth is: A) 6 cm B) 9 cm. C) 4 cm D) 3 cm
6. Group B grows 1, 3, 5 cm. Which statement is correct relative to group A in Q5? A) Both have mean 3 cm, but B varies more B) B has mean 9 cm C) Both contain identical measurements. D) Both have mean 4 cm
7. In a class trial, 8 of 20 seeds germinate. Percentage germinated: A) 40% B) 80%. C) 20% D) 8%
8. A measured count rises from 40 to 60. Percentage increase is: A) 60% B) 40% C) 50%. D) 20%
9. A graph reports results for days 1–5 only. Which statement is supported? A) It reports the measured values during days 1–5 B) No units are needed. C) Day 6 must be higher D) The trend continues forever
10. Fertiliser-treated plants also receive more water than controls. The study cannot isolate the fertiliser effect because: A) plants cannot grow B) water is a confounding factor C) both groups must always have different species. D) water is not matter
11. A control expected to show an effect can help check that a method can detect that effect. This is a: A) conclusion. B) positive control C) dependent variable D) random guess
12. A baseline group in an experiment helps mainly by: A) removing all bias B) replacing measurements C) guaranteeing the hypothesis D) providing a comparison.
13. A person measures a sample without knowing which treatment it received. This can reduce: A) the amount of matter B) the need for an appropriate control. C) observer bias D) the need to record data
14. A scientist records only the results that fit a favourite theory. This is: A) random assignment B) replication C) calibration. D) cherry-picking
15. In a chart, ice-cream sales and swimming both increase in summer. A plausible third factor is: A) the alphabet B) warmer weather C) that ice cream necessarily causes all swimming. D) the number of planets
16. Two treatments have the same mean but different ranges. What follows? A) The mean tells you all individual values B) Every measurement must be identical C) Equal means do not imply identical variation. D) Neither dataset has variation
17. A drawing's stated scale is 1 cm = 10 µm. A 2 cm feature represents: A) 20 µm B) 10 µm C) 2 µm D) 200 µm.
18. Which is an observation rather than an explanation? A) The fertiliser caused growth B) The treatment must work in every species. C) The plant grew 3 cm over the week D) The plant grew because it was happy
19. An unexpected experiment result should first lead a scientist to: A) claim the hypothesis is always true. B) change the result to match the hypothesis C) hide the result D) check measurements, methods and alternative explanations
20. Which statement about enzymes is best? A) They are all used up permanently B) Many lose activity when high temperatures disrupt their structure. C) Heat always improves their function D) They create energy from nothing
21. A bacterial culture becomes resistant to an antibiotic. What has developed resistance? A) Bacteria in the population B) The person's body as a whole C) Every virus in the world D) The glass container.
22. If a solution changes from pH 5 to pH 4, hydrogen-ion activity: A) becomes zero. B) increases tenfold C) decreases tenfold D) remains unchanged
23. A fair coin is tossed twice independently. Probability of two heads: A) 1/3 B) 1/2 C) 1. D) 1/4
24. A treatment did not show an effect in one small trial. Best conclusion: A) No more evidence is needed B) It definitely works everywhere C) No effect was observed under these conditions; further investigation may be needed. D) It can never work under any conditions
25. A student tests whether a seed variety germinates better with more water. Name the independent variable, one dependent variable and one condition to keep comparable.
26. In an imagined enzyme trial, reaction rate rises with temperature then falls at high temperature. Give one plausible explanation for the fall.
27. A graph shows 12 cm, 14 cm and 16 cm plant heights on days 1, 2 and 3. What is the overall height increase between day 1 and day 3?
28. A post claims that a household remedy cures every infection. Give two reasons to be cautious.
29. A cell contains a nucleus and a cell membrane. Does that information alone establish it is a plant cell? Explain briefly.
30. In a no-treatment group 4 of 10 seeds germinate; in a treatment group 7 of 10 germinate. State one observation and one limitation before claiming the treatment causes the difference.

---

## Answer sheet and explanations

1. **A:** vary the main factor and keep other conditions similar.
2. **B:** height is the measured outcome.
3. **C:** random assignment can reduce systematic selection bias, not remove all chance differences.
4. **D:** one individual may be atypical; comparable replication helps.
5. **D:** (2 + 3 + 4)/3 = 3 cm.
6. **A:** (1 + 3 + 5)/3 = 3 cm; B ranges from 1 to 5 instead of 2 to 4.
7. **A:** 8/20 × 100 = 40%.
8. **C:** (60 − 40)/40 × 100 = 50%.
9. **A:** the graph provides data only for the stated period; do not invent later values.
10. **B:** water differs alongside fertiliser, making the causal effect unclear.
11. **B:** a positive control checks whether an expected effect is detectable under the method.
12. **D:** a baseline permits comparison, but cannot guarantee the study is unbiased.
13. **C:** blinded measurement can reduce observer bias where practical.
14. **D:** cherry-picking hides contrary evidence.
15. **B:** heat can influence both; association alone cannot settle causation.
16. **C:** equal averages can hide different spread and individual values.
17. **A:** 2 × 10 = 20 µm, assuming the stated scale applies to the displayed drawing.
18. **C:** the measured increase is an observation; causal claims require further evidence.
19. **D:** investigate data and methods and consider alternatives.
20. **B:** excessive heat may disrupt enzyme structure and activity.
21. **A:** bacteria can become resistant; this is not the same as the human body becoming resistant.
22. **B:** pH falls one unit, corresponding to tenfold greater hydrogen-ion activity.
23. **D:** 1/2 × 1/2 = 1/4, because the tosses are independent and fair.
24. **C:** a small trial has limits; avoid universal claims from it.
25. **Independent:** amount of water. **Dependent:** proportion germinating, or time to germination. **Controlled:** seed variety, light, temperature or duration, as appropriate. Several seeds per group help assess variation.
26. **Possible denaturation:** high temperature may disrupt the enzyme's functional structure. A specific experiment would need evidence and suitable controls.
27. **4 cm:** 16 − 12. This is a height difference, not necessarily total growth before day 1.
28. **Possible answers:** it lacks reliable evidence; infections have different causes; it could delay effective treatment or be unsafe. Consult qualified health professionals.
29. **No:** animal cells also have a nucleus and membrane. Other evidence, such as a cellulose cell wall or chloroplasts in appropriate cells, is needed.
30. **Observation:** 7/10 germinated in treatment vs 4/10 without treatment. **Limitation:** small samples, possible uncontrolled conditions or chance variation; a well-designed replicated study is needed for a stronger causal claim.

Award short-answer points for the central correct reasoning. Explain a wrong option aloud before moving on.
