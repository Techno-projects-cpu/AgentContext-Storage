# Chekumuki Extra: Science GK and Everyday Applications

This is a **bonus knowledge bank**, not a prediction of the official questions. It focuses on stable facts; current affairs and local rules must be checked close to the exam.

## India, scientific organisations and programmes

- **ISRO:** Indian Space Research Organisation. A launch vehicle carries a spacecraft; an orbiter, lander and rover perform different functions once at their destination.
- **Chandrayaan-1:** lunar orbiter; observations contributed evidence of water/hydroxyl on the Moon. **Chandrayaan-2:** orbiter plus lander/rover; the 2019 landing attempt did not succeed. **Chandrayaan-3:** successfully landed in the Moon's south polar region in 2023, not at the exact geographic pole.
- **Vikram** is Chandrayaan-3's lander; **Pragyan** is its rover. APXS and LIBS on the rover investigated elements in material near the landing site. [ISRO](https://www.isro.gov.in/Chandrayaan3_Details.html)
- **Aditya-L1:** Sun-observing mission around the Sun–Earth L1 region. **XPoSat:** studies polarisation of X-rays from bright astronomical sources. [ISRO report](https://www.isro.gov.in/media_isro/pdf/Publications/Space_Research_2022_2023_040625.pdf)
- **IMD:** India Meteorological Department; weather and climate services. **ICMR:** Indian Council of Medical Research. **CSIR:** Council of Scientific and Industrial Research. **DRDO:** Defence Research and Development Organisation.
- **National Science Day:** 28 February; associated with C. V. Raman's discovery of the Raman effect. **World Environment Day:** 5 June. **World Water Day:** 22 March. A day's date is stable, but its annual theme changes.

## Who contributed what?

- **C. V. Raman:** light scattering and the Raman effect. **S. N. Bose:** quantum statistics. **J. C. Bose:** radio/microwave work and plant responses.
- **Vikram Sarabhai:** a leader in establishing India's space programme. **Kalpana Chawla:** astronaut who flew on NASA space shuttle missions. **A. P. J. Abdul Kalam:** aerospace scientist and former President; major projects involve teams, not one person alone.
- **Marie Curie:** research on radioactivity. **Rosalind Franklin:** X-ray diffraction data crucial to understanding DNA structure. **Gregor Mendel:** inheritance studies in peas.
- **Louis Pasteur:** work on microorganisms and vaccination. **Edward Jenner:** early smallpox vaccination. **Alexander Fleming:** discovery of penicillin's antibacterial effect; later teams developed its practical use.
- **Ada Lovelace:** early work on algorithms for a proposed mechanical computer. **Alan Turing:** foundational work on computation and codebreaking.

## Geography and Earth: learn the logic

- **Latitude** measures angular distance north or south of the equator; **longitude** measures angular distance east or west from the prime meridian.
- **Earth rotates** about its axis, giving day and night; **revolves** around the Sun. Seasons are mainly due to axial tilt and revolution.
- The **equator** divides Earth into northern and southern hemispheres. The **Tropic of Cancer** passes through India. The **Indian Standard Time** meridian is 82°30′ E; IST is UTC+5:30.
- **Weather** refers to short-term atmospheric conditions; **climate** concerns long-term patterns. One unusually cold day does not disprove climate change.
- A **delta** forms where sediment accumulates at a river mouth under suitable conditions. A **watershed** separates drainage areas; a **catchment** is the area draining towards a water body.
- **Magma** is molten rock beneath Earth's surface; **lava** is molten rock at the surface. Earthquake magnitude describes event size; local intensity describes shaking effects.

## Everyday science: fast 'why?' facts

1. **Why do we see lightning before hearing thunder?** Light travels faster than sound.
2. **Why does a pressure cooker cook food faster?** Increased pressure raises water's boiling temperature.
3. **Why does a wet cloth cool a pot?** Evaporation takes energy from its surroundings, although cooling depends on humidity and airflow.
4. **Why is a fuse connected in series?** When excessive current melts it, the circuit opens.
5. **Why is a compass needle deflected near a current-carrying wire?** The current generates a magnetic field.
6. **Why does cut fruit sometimes turn brown?** Enzyme-driven reactions involving oxygen can produce brown pigments; the details vary with the fruit.
7. **Why is cooked food refrigerated?** Low temperature slows the growth of many microbes; it does not sterilise food.
8. **Why does a straw appear bent in water?** Light refracts as it passes between water and air.
9. **Why does a shaving mirror enlarge a nearby face?** A concave mirror can form an upright, magnified virtual image when the face is within its focal length.
10. **Why does a fire extinguisher work?** It interrupts one or more requirements for combustion: fuel, oxygen or sufficient heat. Different fires require different extinguishers; leave emergencies to trained adults.
11. **Why is water not safe just because it looks clean?** Microbes and dissolved chemicals can be invisible.
12. **Why do metal lids sometimes loosen after warming?** Thermal expansion can increase the lid's diameter; how well this works depends on materials and temperatures.
13. **Why does a plant lean towards light?** Phototropism involves unequal growth regulated by plant hormones, including auxin.
14. **Why is soap less effective in hard water?** Calcium and magnesium ions can form insoluble scum with soap.
15. **Why can birds sit on one power line without an electric shock?** If both feet are at nearly the same electrical potential, little current passes through the bird; touching another conductor at a different potential can be dangerous. Never approach power lines.

## Computer and cyber concepts

- **Bit:** a binary digit, 0 or 1. **Byte:** commonly 8 bits. Storage size and internet speed are different; bytes and bits are not interchangeable.
- **Hardware** is physical equipment; **software** is instructions. **RAM** is working memory; non-volatile storage retains data without power.
- **Internet** is a network of networks; the **Web** is a collection of linked resources accessed over the internet. A search engine is a tool for finding information, not the internet itself.
- AI output can be plausible and wrong. Check key claims against reliable sources. Avoid entering passwords, OTPs or other sensitive details into unknown sites.

## Spot the misconception

- “Weight and mass are identical.” → No: weight is gravitational force; mass is a measure of inertia.
- “All bacteria cause disease.” → No: many are harmless or useful, though some cause illness.
- “Plants only respire at night.” → No: they respire day and night.
- “Antibiotics cure all infections.” → No: they act on certain bacteria, not viruses.
- “Only deserts have ecosystems.” → Every region has ecosystems, from wetlands to forests and grasslands.
- “A satellite has escaped gravity.” → Gravity keeps an orbiting satellite in free fall around Earth.
- “Seasons happen because Earth is closer to the Sun in summer everywhere.” → No: opposite hemispheres have opposite seasons; tilt matters.

## Verify before memorising current affairs

For new missions, discoveries and officeholders, record the **event, date, status and official source**. A mission announced or planned is not the same as a launched or completed one. Useful starting points: [ISRO](https://www.isro.gov.in/), [PIB](https://pib.gov.in/), [WHO](https://www.who.int/) and the organiser's notice. Avoid copying unverified social-media facts.
