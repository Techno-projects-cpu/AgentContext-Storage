# Chekumuki: Fast-to-Read, Deep-to-Understand Knowledge Cards

**Goal:** Short, interesting explanations that go beyond memorising definitions. This is independent preparation, **not an official syllabus or a prediction of questions**. Your teacher and local organiser decide the actual format, date and topics.

**How to read:** Start with the ★ cards. Read 5–8 cards, cover them, and explain the “why” aloud. Do the short quiz at the end, then revisit mistakes tomorrow. Telugu glosses are included for selected terms. Maths is school-level and approachable.

## 1. ★ Physics: the world is full of hidden interactions

1. **Why is it easier to push a door at its handle?** The handle is farther from the hinge. Turning effect, or **torque (బలఘూర్ణం)**, depends on force and its perpendicular distance from the turning axis. Pushing in the wrong direction reduces the turning effect.
2. **Why do passengers lean outward on a turning bus?** Their bodies tend to continue in a straight line while the bus changes direction. A net inward force is needed to make an object follow a curved path; constant speed does not mean constant velocity.
3. **Why can a satellite keep falling without hitting Earth?** It has enough sideways speed that Earth curves away beneath it as it falls. Orbit is continuous free fall, not an absence of gravity. Atmospheric drag can eventually bring a low satellite down.
4. **Why do falling objects accelerate equally in a vacuum?** Near the same place, gravitational force is proportional to mass, so the mass cancels in force divided by mass. In air, drag can make a feather fall more slowly than a stone.
5. **Can friction make something move?** Yes. When you walk, your foot pushes backward on the ground and static friction pushes you forward. Friction can also oppose unwanted sliding and produce heat.
6. **Why is stopping a heavy vehicle difficult?** Momentum equals mass × velocity. For the same change in momentum, a longer stopping time reduces average force. Braking distance also depends on speed, tyres, road and reaction time.
7. **Why does an ice cube float?** Ice is less dense than liquid water under ordinary conditions. The upward **buoyant force (తేలే బలం)** equals the weight of displaced fluid; a floating cube displaces water whose weight equals the cube's weight.
8. **Why does water flow from a higher tank?** A height difference can create pressure and drive flow. Flow rate also depends on pipe diameter, resistance and other factors. Water pressure in a stationary liquid generally rises with depth.
9. **Why does a bicycle pump get warm?** Compressing air transfers energy to it and can raise its temperature. Rapid compression allows less time for heat to escape. The pump itself may warm through contact and friction too.
10. **Why does sweat cool you?** Evaporation requires energy, some of which is drawn from the skin. In humid air sweat evaporates less readily, making this cooling less effective.
11. **Why does a pressure cooker cook faster?** Higher pressure raises water's boiling temperature, so the food can be heated above the normal boiling point of water. Always use the cooker as instructed; do not tamper with its valve.
12. **Why is black clothing often warmer in strong sunlight?** Dark surfaces usually absorb more incoming visible light than light-coloured ones. Comfort also depends on wind, fabric, fit, surrounding temperature and other factors.
13. **Why does a spoon appear bent in water?** Light changes speed and direction at the water–air boundary: **refraction (వక్రీభవనం)**. The brain traces the emerging rays back in straight lines, making the underwater part appear displaced.
14. **Why is the sky blue but a sunset often red?** Air molecules scatter shorter visible wavelengths more strongly. Near sunset the Sun's light travels through more atmosphere, so more blue is scattered out of the direct beam; dust and clouds also affect the colour.
15. **Why does a prism separate colours?** Its refractive index varies with wavelength, so different colours bend by different amounts. A prism separates colours already present in white light; it does not create all of them from nothing.
16. **Why does lightning arrive before thunder?** Light travels far faster than sound. A delay of about 3 seconds corresponds to roughly 1 km away if sound travels near 340 m/s; conditions affect the estimate. Do not stand outdoors to measure a storm.
17. **Why do parallel household devices work independently?** Each branch has a path to the supply, so switching off one branch need not break another. Proper wiring and protective devices are essential; **never experiment with mains electricity**.
18. **Why can a transformer change alternating voltage?** Changing current in one coil produces a changing magnetic field that induces voltage in another coil. A conventional transformer needs changing magnetic flux; a steady DC input alone does not continuously provide it.

## 2. ★ Chemistry: tiny particles, large consequences

19. **Why do oil and water separate?** Water molecules interact strongly with one another, while oil is largely non-polar. Mixing is energetically unfavourable overall, so the liquids tend to form separate layers.
20. **How does soap help wash grease?** Soap molecules have a water-attracting part and an oil-attracting part. They help disperse grease into tiny aggregates that water can carry away.
21. **Why does saltwater conduct electricity?** Dissolved salt provides mobile ions that carry charge. Solid salt contains ions too, but they cannot move freely through its solid lattice.
22. **Why can graphite conduct if carbon is a non-metal?** In graphite, some electrons can move along layers of bonded carbon atoms. Diamond is also carbon, but its bonding structure is different and it does not conduct electricity well under ordinary conditions.
23. **Why does an iron nail gain mass when it rusts?** Iron combines with oxygen from the environment to form rust. In an open system, oxygen enters; in a closed system, total mass is conserved.
24. **Does melting ice create a new substance?** No. Its state changes, but it remains H₂O. Burning wax is different: melting is physical, while wax vapour reacting with oxygen is chemical.
25. **Why does crushing a tablet often make it dissolve faster?** Smaller pieces expose more surface area to the liquid. Temperature, stirring and the substance itself matter too. Never crush medication unless a qualified professional says it is safe.
26. **How do catalysts work?** They provide an alternative reaction pathway with lower activation energy and are regenerated overall. They can help a reaction reach equilibrium faster; they do not change its equilibrium position under the same conditions.
27. **Is every clear liquid pure?** No. Salt solution can look clear even though it is a mixture. Dissolved impurities and disease-causing organisms may be invisible.
28. **Can ordinary filtration remove dissolved salt from water?** No: dissolved ions pass through an ordinary filter. Distillation can separate and collect water from a salt solution, but practical drinking-water treatment depends on the actual contaminants.
29. **What is pH?** A measure related to hydrogen-ion activity. At 25°C, pH below 7 is acidic and above 7 basic. Each pH unit represents a tenfold change in hydrogen-ion activity, not a change of just one particle.
30. **What are isotopes?** Atoms of the same element with the same number of protons but different numbers of neutrons. Carbon-12 and carbon-14 are isotopes; not all isotopes are radioactive.
31. **Why do noble gases react relatively little?** Their outer electron shells are complete in the simple school-level model. “Relatively unreactive” does not mean no noble-gas compound can ever exist.
32. **Why does a copper vessel develop a green coating?** Its surface can react over time with substances in air and moisture, forming a patina. The exact compounds and rate depend on the environment; do not infer that every green coating has the same composition.

## 3. ★ Biology: structure, function and exceptions

33. **Why are red blood cells well suited to carrying oxygen?** Their shape and haemoglobin help transport oxygen. Mature mammalian red blood cells lack a nucleus, leaving more internal space; not every animal's red blood cells lack one.
34. **Do arteries always carry oxygen-rich blood?** No. Arteries carry blood **away from the heart**. Pulmonary arteries carry oxygen-poor blood from the heart to the lungs.
35. **Why is the small intestine long and folded?** Folds and tiny projections called villi increase surface area for absorbing digested nutrients. Absorption also depends on healthy cells and blood supply.
36. **What do kidneys actually do?** They filter blood and regulate water, salts and waste removal through urine. They do much more than simply “remove all water.”
37. **Why does breathing speed up during exercise?** Working muscles generally need more oxygen and produce more carbon dioxide. Breathing and circulation respond to changing demand; the exact response varies with intensity and fitness.
38. **Why are vaccines not the same as antibiotics?** Vaccines train immune responses against specific threats. Antibiotics treat certain bacterial infections; they do not kill influenza viruses. Follow qualified medical advice.
39. **What is antibiotic resistance?** Bacteria can survive drugs that would normally kill or inhibit them. Resistance spreads through reproduction and gene transfer; it is the microorganisms, not a person's determination, that are resistant.
40. **Why do plants need both photosynthesis and respiration?** Photosynthesis stores energy in chemical compounds using light. Cellular respiration releases usable energy from food; plants respire both day and night.
41. **How does water move up a tree?** Water moves through xylem; evaporation from leaves creates tension that helps pull a continuous water column upward. Adhesion and cohesion help, but transport is a system, not just one isolated force.
42. **Why do leaves have stomata?** These tiny pores allow gas exchange. Their opening also permits water loss, so plants balance carbon dioxide uptake with water conservation.
43. **What is an enzyme?** A biological catalyst, often a protein, that helps a reaction occur faster. Temperature and pH affect enzyme activity; very high temperatures can damage many enzymes.
44. **Is a gene a complete blueprint for a person?** Genes are segments of DNA associated with functions and traits. Many traits involve multiple genes and environmental influences; a single gene does not explain everything.
45. **Why are fungi not plants?** Fungi do not photosynthesise like green plants; they absorb nutrients from their surroundings. Many decompose material, though fungi also include parasites and partners in beneficial relationships.
46. **Why do antibiotics not help with every fever?** Fever has many possible causes, including viral infections and non-infectious conditions. The correct treatment depends on the cause and a health professional's advice.
47. **What happens when a predator disappears?** Prey populations may change, affecting plants and other species. Real food webs are complex; effects depend on the ecosystem, so one outcome is not guaranteed everywhere.
48. **Why are decomposers essential?** Bacteria and fungi break down dead matter, returning nutrients to the environment. Nutrients cycle through ecosystems; energy enters and flows through them, with some dissipated as heat.

## 4. Earth, space, environment and GK

49. **Why do seasons occur?** Earth's axis is tilted, so during its yearly revolution different hemispheres receive different sunlight angles and day lengths. Distance from the Sun is not the main cause.
50. **Why is the Moon sometimes visible during the day?** The Moon reflects sunlight and can be above the horizon while the Sun is also up. Its position and phase determine when it is visible.
51. **Why isn't there an eclipse every full moon?** The Moon's orbital plane is tilted relative to Earth's orbit, so the Sun, Earth and Moon usually do not align closely enough. A lunar eclipse requires Earth between Sun and Moon.
52. **Why does a geostationary satellite appear fixed?** It moves above the equator in the same direction as Earth's rotation, with a matching orbital period. Geostationary is a special case of geosynchronous orbit.
53. **What is the difference between weather and climate?** Weather describes short-term atmospheric conditions; climate describes long-term patterns. One cold day cannot by itself determine a long-term climate trend.
54. **Does the ozone hole cause all climate change?** No. Ozone depletion and greenhouse warming are different problems, though some substances and processes connect them. Stratospheric ozone helps shield life from much harmful ultraviolet radiation.
55. **Why is groundwater not automatically limitless?** Aquifers recharge at different rates; pumping faster than recharge can lower water levels. Local geology, rainfall, water quality and use all matter.
56. **Why do rivers matter after they reach the sea?** Freshwater and sediment support estuaries, coastal ecosystems and livelihoods. A river's flow is not automatically “wasted” when it reaches the ocean.
57. **What is biodiversity (జీవ వైవిధ్యం)?** Variety of life at genetic, species and ecosystem levels. Healthy ecosystems can support food, water and other benefits, but biodiversity alone does not guarantee immunity from every disturbance.
58. **What does ISRO do?** The Indian Space Research Organisation develops and operates space missions and technology. Chandrayaan-3 demonstrated a lunar soft landing and rover operations; see [ISRO's mission page](https://www.isro.gov.in/Chandrayaan3_Details.html).
59. **IMD, ICMR and CSIR:** IMD provides meteorological services; ICMR supports medical research; CSIR is a scientific and industrial research network. Learn the full names and match each organisation to its work.
60. **People and discoveries:** C. V. Raman is associated with light scattering and the Raman effect; S. N. Bose with quantum statistics; Janaki Ammal with botany and cytogenetics; Rosalind Franklin's X-ray work contributed crucial evidence to understanding DNA's structure.
61. **Latitude and longitude:** Latitude measures angular distance north or south of the equator; longitude measures angular distance east or west of the prime meridian. India's standard time is UTC+5:30, based on 82°30′ E.
62. **Why check current affairs separately?** Mission updates, officeholders and competition notices can change. Use dated official sources and ask your teacher rather than treating an old study guide as today's news.

## 5. ★ Easy maths: a little method beats a big formula

63. **Percentages:** 15% of 240 = 10% (24) + 5% (12) = **36**. A 15% discount on ₹240 leaves ₹204, not ₹36.
64. **Ratio:** Share 72 in the ratio 5:3. There are 8 parts; one part is 9. The shares are **45 and 27**.
65. **Average:** 6, 8, 10 and 12 add to 36; divide by 4 to get **9**. If each value increases by 3, the average increases to 12.
66. **Average speed trap:** Travel 60 km in 1 hour, then 60 km in 2 hours. Average speed = total distance / total time = 120/3 = **40 km/h**, not 45 km/h.
67. **Units:** A 100 W lamp on for 3 hours uses 300 Wh = **0.3 kWh**. Watts measure power; kilowatt-hours measure energy.
68. **Area vs perimeter:** A 6 cm × 4 cm rectangle has area **24 cm²** and perimeter **20 cm**. Always check whether the answer needs square units or ordinary length units.
69. **Probability:** A fair die has two outcomes greater than 4: 5 and 6. Probability = 2/6 = **1/3**. A probability describes likelihood, not an exact result in every set of trials.
70. **Simple graphs:** On a distance–time graph, a steeper upward straight line means greater speed. On a velocity–time graph, a horizontal line means constant velocity, which may be nonzero.

## 6. Quick self-test: cover the answers

1. Why is a door handle placed far from its hinge?
2. Does a satellite experience gravity while orbiting Earth?
3. Why can ice float on liquid water?
4. Which property determines the pitch of a simple sound: frequency or amplitude?
5. Why does dissolved salt conduct electricity better than solid salt?
6. Are isotopes identified by the same number of neutrons or protons?
7. Can clear water contain dissolved contaminants?
8. Do pulmonary arteries carry blood towards or away from the heart?
9. Do plants respire after sunset?
10. Is antibiotic resistance a property of bacteria or of a patient's willpower?
11. Why is the small intestine's inner surface folded?
12. What do decomposers return to the environment?
13. What is the main cause of Earth's seasons?
14. Why doesn't a lunar eclipse occur every full moon?
15. What does IMD primarily provide?
16. What is 15% of 240?
17. What are the shares when 72 is divided in the ratio 5:3?
18. A 100 W lamp runs for 3 hours. How many kWh does it use?
19. A velocity–time graph is horizontal above zero. Is the object necessarily at rest?
20. A plant experiment changes both light and water. Why is it hard to identify the cause of any difference?

### Answers: short but explained

1. **Greater turning effect:** distance from hinge increases torque for the same suitable force. 2. **Yes:** gravity keeps it in orbit as it continually falls. 3. **Lower density:** ice is less dense than liquid water under ordinary conditions. 4. **Frequency:** amplitude is associated with loudness, not pitch. 5. **Mobile ions:** they carry charge in solution but are fixed in the solid lattice. 6. **Protons:** different neutron numbers make isotopes. 7. **Yes:** many contaminants cannot be seen. 8. **Away:** arteries carry blood away from the heart. 9. **Yes:** cellular respiration continues day and night. 10. **Bacteria:** resistant bacteria survive antibiotics that would normally affect them.

11. **More surface area:** folds and villi aid absorption. 12. **Nutrients:** decomposition returns material to the environment. 13. **Axial tilt and revolution:** these change sunlight angle and day length. 14. **Orbital tilt:** the required alignment usually does not occur. 15. **Meteorological services:** including weather information. 16. **36:** 24 + 12. 17. **45 and 27:** eight parts, each worth 9. 18. **0.3 kWh:** 100 W × 3 h = 300 Wh. 19. **No:** it has constant nonzero velocity. 20. **Two variables changed:** either or both could affect the outcome.
