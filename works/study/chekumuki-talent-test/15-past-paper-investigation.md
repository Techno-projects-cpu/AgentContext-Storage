## 1. What the organiser and past reports say

### JVV Andhra Pradesh's own overview

[JVV AP — Science Samburalu](https://jvvap.org/activites/science-samburalu/) describes school, mandal, district and state stages. Its general description says school teams have one student each from Classes 8, 9 and 10 and participate as teams from mandal level. It mentions physics, chemistry, biology, maths and everyday applications; it also describes district projects, discussions and cultural activities.

**Important:** This is a general AP programme description, not a verified timetable or rule sheet for your particular district or year. The actual duration, activities and selection rules can differ. Do not assume you must bring a project unless your organiser asks.

### A real historical district example—not a 2026 rule

[The Hindu, 16 December 2013](https://www.thehindu.com/news/cities/Visakhapatnam/important-for-students-to-stay-focused-and-explore-beyond-textbooks-gsn-raju/article5465581.ece) reported that a Visakhapatnam district test was a **one-hour, group-discussion-based test of 60 MCQs**, covering physics, biology, chemistry and general knowledge, with teams of around three. This is evidence of **one 2013 event**, not proof that your district will use the same time, question count, discussion rules or marking.

A [third-party 2025 AP school-instructions page](https://teachernews.in/chekumuki-science-sambaralu-school-district-state-level-test/) describes 50 MCQs and an 80% science / 20% general-studies split. It is **school instructions for an older edition**, and must not be transferred to your district round without confirmation.

A [2026 Telangana report on the state-level festival](https://hindudayashankar.com/education/chekumuki-science-sambhuraalu-from-december-11-to-13-at-cmr-college-in-medchal/) mentions written, activity, quiz, visual, creative and experiment rounds at the **state level**. It is not evidence that every district uses those rounds, nor that this is your state or venue.

## 3. Actual question material: limited, transparent analysis

### A. School-level example from JVV AP's own overview

The organiser gives examples about **the gas used in a packaged snack**, the **formula of plaster of Paris**, a plant causing itching, and **why polished shoes shine**. These are sample questions in the programme overview—not a complete district paper. [JVV AP](https://jvvap.org/activites/science-samburalu/)

| Example theme | What it tests | Concept to learn |
|---|---|---|
| Packaging gas | Everyday chemistry | Nitrogen is often used in snack packaging to reduce oxidation and protect contents; check the particular package if asked about an actual product. |
| Plaster of Paris | Chemical formulas and water of crystallisation | POP is **CaSO₄·½H₂O**; gypsum is **CaSO₄·2H₂O**. Heating and setting involve changes in water content. |
| Shiny polished shoes | Light and surfaces | Smoother polished surfaces produce more regular/specular reflection; the surface does not create light. |
| Itching plant | Living organisms and chemistry | Do not memorise a single chemical from a vague description: species and wording matter. Check the exact plant and reliable source before choosing. |

**What this supports:** the organiser explicitly values science in familiar settings and distinguishes similar-looking options. It does **not** tell us how many district questions fall in each subject.

### B. A few OCR fragments from a user-uploaded 2022-labelled paper

[“Chekumuki Sr-2022” search-indexed upload](https://www.scribd.com/document/778377938/chekumuki-Sr-2022): OCR snippets show a prism/colour question, a question about recovering copper sulphate crystals from solution, a plant-response question, and a question describing use of animal serum against a toxin. Some wording and options are garbled; the original page images, full context and answer key were **not reliably readable**. Stage and authenticity are unverified.

**Carefully inferred concepts, not claimed official answer keys:**

1. **Prism:** In ordinary visible-light dispersion through glass, red generally deviates least and violet most because refractive index varies with wavelength. The fragment includes red, violet, green and blue. Verify the exact original wording and diagram before using any option letter.
2. **Copper sulphate solution:** Controlled **crystallisation** can recover crystals from an appropriate solution. Heating to dryness may alter hydrated crystals; the exact best answer depends on the original options. Read the complete paper before assigning an option.
3. **Plant response to touch:** **Thigmotropism** is directional growth in response to touch, such as a tendril winding around a support. A touch-me-not plant's rapid leaf folding is a different, non-directional response called thigmonasty; don't treat the examples as identical.
4. **Antitoxin serum:** The described process relates to using preformed antibodies in antiserum, often called **serotherapy/passive immunisation**. This differs from vaccination, which stimulates the recipient's own immune response. Medical antivenom is administered by trained professionals; never experiment with toxins or animals.

**What this supports:** at least these **fragments** combine textbook concepts with application and precise terminology. It does **not** prove that these topics recur across district papers, that any specific question will repeat, or that the visible OCR is error-free. No official key was checked.

## 4. What to prioritise this week

These are **recommendations** based on the organiser overview and limited material above, not measured percentages:

1. **Everyday physical science:** reflection/refraction, heat, pressure, force, density, electricity and safe circuit diagrams. Explain the reason, not only the definition. [Electricity from zero](12-electricity-from-zero.md)
2. **Chemistry in context:** mixtures, filtration and crystallisation, atoms and ions, pH, reactions, familiar formulas such as POP and gypsum. Distinguish a substance from a mixture.
3. **Biology and health:** transport in plants and humans, plant responses, immunity, microbes, food webs and how evidence is tested. Don't use health questions to self-medicate.
4. **Maths and experiment skills:** units, percentages, ratios, average speed, graphs and fair tests. Practise small calculations with clear steps.
5. **Science-related GK and recent events:** scientist–contribution matches, Indian science organisations and missions, environment, and current topics mentioned in your notice. Sports GK can be supplementary; do not let it replace core science. [Science GK](08-science-gk-daily-life.md) · [Sports snapshot](13-sports-current-affairs.md) (dated; update before using).
6. **Possible team/oral/practical skills:** take turns stating a claim and a reason, analyse an unfamiliar picture, and practise describing safe demonstrations. Only prepare a project if your organiser requires one.

## 5. Original mini-drill based on the *types* of material seen

These are **newly written practice questions**, not copied official past-paper questions or predictions.

1. A prism separates white light. In ordinary glass, which visible colour generally deviates least: red or violet?
2. Which is plaster of Paris: CaSO₄·½H₂O or CaSO₄·2H₂O?
3. A pea tendril coils around a support. Is this directional growth in response to touch or a response to gravity?
4. How can you separate insoluble sand from a salt solution? Will this step also remove the dissolved salt?
5. A 6 V source is connected to a 3 Ω ohmic resistor. Find the current.
6. A smooth polished surface appears shiny. Is regular reflection or refraction the main idea?
7. One group of seedlings is kept in different soil and given different light from another group. Why is the comparison weak?
8. Is receiving preformed antitoxin antibodies the same process as a vaccine stimulating your immune system to make its own response?

**Answers:** 1. **Red**, generally; dispersion depends on material and wavelength. 2. **CaSO₄·½H₂O**; the other formula is gypsum. 3. **Directional growth in response to touch**, called thigmotropism. 4. **Filter the sand**; dissolved salt passes through an ordinary filter. 5. **2 A**, from I = V/R = 6/3. 6. **Regular reflection.** 7. **More than one variable changed**, so the cause is difficult to isolate. 8. **No:** preformed antibodies provide passive immunity; vaccination stimulates an immune response. Medical treatment needs qualified professionals.

## 6. How we can analyse your actual paper properly

Please upload the **complete mandal paper you just took**, a district notice and/or a district paper as a PDF or sharp photos (all pages, straight and readable, including diagrams, options and answer key if you have it). Cover names, phone numbers and other personal details first.

For every accessible paper I can then record:

- Year, state/district if printed, stage, language, source and whether authenticity is confirmed.
- Instructions: number of questions, duration, individual/team rules and marking, *only if printed*.
- Question-by-question topic and skill (recall, calculation, diagram, experiment, everyday application); ambiguous questions noted rather than forced into a category.
- An independently reasoned answer with a brief explanation; compare it with the printed key and flag disputed, outdated or unclear items for a teacher.
- Topic counts **for that paper only**, followed by a separate cross-paper summary if multiple comparable papers are available. Small samples and different rounds do not predict exact future questions.

Use the existing [district “heavy sword” challenge](14-district-heavy-sword-challenge.md) while collecting papers. Sleep and breaks matter: harder practice should build confidence, not cause exhaustion.
