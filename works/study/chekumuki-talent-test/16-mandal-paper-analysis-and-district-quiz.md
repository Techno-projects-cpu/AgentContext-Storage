## 2. What this means for training

1. **Do not study science in isolation.** Practise applying physics, chemistry and biology to ordinary situations, alongside easy maths, scientific reasoning, safety and some civics/GK.
2. **Practise formats:** multiple choice, assertion–reason and short fill-ins. Read the exact words—especially “NOT”, “best”, and conditions such as equal resistance or same brightness.
3. **Look at diagrams:** be ready to identify functions of xylem/phloem, parts of a neuron and region overlaps in a set diagram. Text-only practice is useful but cannot replace interpreting pictures.
4. **Know current science news:** the Voyager question is about a dated mission milestone, not simply the name of a planet. Check updates from NASA and ISRO instead of memorising undated claims. [NASA Voyager](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/voyager/)
5. **Train discussion and oral speed:** use the practice rounds below if your local rules allow, but don't assume buzzers, time limits or scoring from this practice activity are official.

## 3. Two places to be careful when checking the paper

- **Question 5:** a knee-jerk is a spinal **somatic reflex**, not an autonomic reflex. The available option that calls it an automatic reflex also says “autonomous nervous system,” so it is imprecise if that means *autonomic*. Ask the teacher for the official key; don't confidently present the printed wording as scientifically exact.
- **Question 35:** the fuse melts because current heats its specially designed element until it melts. In a series path, the same current flows through the fuse and appliance, and heat in a component is I²Rt. It would be wrong to say lower resistance **alone** makes the fuse produce more total heat than a higher-resistance appliance. Shape, material, mass, temperature and heat loss matter. The missing word may be “current” based on the sentence, but no key is provided; confirm it with a teacher.

These cautions are about the question wording, **not a score**. Also, a handwritten mark on a photo is not an official answer key.

## 5. Original quiz practice: 3 rounds

These are **practice rounds**, not official rules or questions. Ask your teacher what the real quiz uses. For training, let each team discuss; one person answers, another gives the reason, and rotate roles. A suggested 15–20 seconds per question is for practice only—take longer when learning.

### Round A — quickfire

1. What is the SI unit of electric current?
2. Which plant tissue mainly carries water and minerals upwards?
3. What gas makes yeast-raised dough expand?
4. Why does a passenger tend to move forward when a bus brakes suddenly?
5. Which organelle carries out photosynthesis in plant cells?
6. What does a volt measure: energy per charge, or charge per second?
7. Which blood component mainly transports oxygen?
8. What is the pH of a neutral solution at 25°C?
9. Which Indian organisation is associated with space missions?
10. What is 25% of 200?

### Round B — explain the reason

11. Why does a pressure cooker cook food faster than an open pot?
12. Why can a steel ship float while a small solid steel nail sinks?
13. A leaf of touch-me-not folds after contact. Does that prove it has a human-like nervous system?
14. Why is water moving into a raisin an example of osmosis?
15. Why can an LED use less power than an incandescent lamp at similar brightness?
16. What should you do if a message asks for an upfront fee to claim an unexpected prize?
17. Why is one cold day not enough to disprove global warming?
18. Why does a fuse melt during a sufficiently large current?

### Round C — visual/oral descriptions (original practice)

19. A labelled circuit has two bulbs on separate branches across the same battery. Series or parallel?
20. A leaf diagram shows a vein carrying sugars away from leaves. Name the tissue.
21. A map shows a country directly south of Nepal. Which country is it?
22. A prism diagram shows white light spreading into colours. What is this splitting called?
23. A chart shows a line rising on a distance–time graph. What does its slope represent?
24. An image shows a person taking cover under a sturdy desk during shaking. What type of emergency is this response for?

### Answers and short explanations

1. **Ampere (A).** 2. **Xylem.** 3. **Carbon dioxide.** 4. **Inertia:** the body tends to keep moving as the bus slows. 5. **Chloroplast.** 6. **Energy per charge:** voltage, in joules per coulomb. 7. **Red blood cells**, using haemoglobin. 8. **7**, at 25°C. 9. **ISRO.** 10. **50.**

11. Higher pressure raises water's boiling temperature. 12. The ship's overall average density, including enclosed air, can be below water's; buoyancy can balance its weight. 13. **No:** plants respond to stimuli without a human-like nervous system. 14. Water passes through a selectively permeable membrane in response to a water-potential difference. 15. It converts a larger share of input electrical energy to visible light under the comparison; exact power depends on the products. 16. **Do not pay or share sensitive details; verify independently and tell a trusted adult.** In India, cybercrime can be reported at [cybercrime.gov.in](https://cybercrime.gov.in/) or 1930 for financial cyber fraud; do not delay contacting your bank. 17. Climate concerns long-term patterns; weather varies daily. 18. Electrical heating raises the element's temperature until it melts and breaks the circuit.

19. **Parallel.** 20. **Phloem.** 21. **India.** 22. **Dispersion.** 23. **Speed** when plotting increasing distance travelled against time. 24. **Earthquake:** drop, cover and hold on as directed by local authorities.
