# Science and Everyday Life: Concept Guide

Chekumuki favours curiosity and application. For every fact, ask: **What causes it? What changes if conditions change?**

## A. Physics: motion, forces and energy

### Motion and force

- **Distance** is total path length; **displacement** is change in position with direction. A person who walks around a closed track back to the start has nonzero distance but zero displacement.
- **Speed** = distance / time. **Acceleration** = change in velocity / time. Velocity changes if direction changes, even at constant speed.
- Newton's first law: an object stays at rest or moves uniformly in a straight line unless a net external force changes its motion. **Inertia (జడత్వం)** is resistance to change in motion.
- Newton's second law: F = ma for constant mass. Third law: forces in an action–reaction pair act on **different bodies**.
- Gravity attracts masses. Weight = mg near Earth's surface; mass remains the same when moving to the Moon, while weight changes.
- **Friction** can oppose sliding or impending relative motion. It is useful for walking, writing and brakes but can waste energy as heat. Lubricants can reduce friction.
- Momentum = mass × velocity. A heavier or faster-moving object generally has more momentum; a seat belt increases stopping time and reduces average force for the same change in momentum.

### Work, energy and power

- Work by a constant force in the direction of movement = force × displacement. Unit: joule (J). If force is perpendicular to displacement, work done by that force is zero.
- Kinetic energy is energy of motion; gravitational potential energy is associated with position in a gravitational field. Energy transfers and transforms; it is not created from nothing in an ordinary closed accounting.
- Power is energy transferred per unit time, measured in watts (W). A 100 W bulb running for 10 hours uses 1 kWh, assuming constant rated power.
- Density = mass/volume. Floating depends on buoyant force and average density, not simply on whether something is heavy. A steel ship can float because the ship including its air spaces has sufficiently low average density.
- Pressure = force/area. Sharp knives have a smaller contact area, increasing pressure for the same force. Fluids exert pressure; pressure in a stationary liquid increases with depth.

### Heat and temperature

- **Temperature** indicates how hot or cold a body is; **heat** is energy transferred because of a temperature difference.
- Conduction: transfer through material, important in metals. Convection: transfer by bulk motion of a fluid, important in boiling water. Radiation: electromagnetic transfer; sunlight reaches Earth through vacuum.
- Dark, dull surfaces generally absorb and emit thermal radiation better than shiny surfaces under comparable conditions.
- Water's boiling point depends on pressure. At lower pressure it boils at a lower temperature; in a pressure cooker, higher pressure raises the boiling point of water, aiding cooking.
- Evaporation occurs at the surface at many temperatures and can cause cooling. Boiling occurs throughout a liquid when its vapour pressure matches external pressure.

## B. Light, sound and electricity

### Light (కాంతి)

- Reflection: angle of incidence equals angle of reflection, measured from the normal. Smooth surfaces can give clearer, regular reflections; rough surfaces scatter reflected light.
- Plane mirrors form virtual, upright, same-sized images; lateral inversion is a common description of the reversal seen in a mirror.
- Refraction occurs because light changes speed between media. A straw may appear bent at the water surface. A lens refracts light at its surfaces.
- A convex lens generally converges parallel rays; a concave lens diverges them. The actual image formed by a convex lens depends on the object's position.
- Myopia (short-sightedness) is commonly corrected with a concave/diverging lens; hypermetropia (long-sightedness) with a convex/converging lens.
- A prism disperses white light into colours. The daytime sky appears blue largely because shorter visible wavelengths are scattered more strongly by air molecules; sunset colours also depend on the path through the atmosphere and particles.

### Sound

- Sound is a mechanical wave. It requires a medium; it does not travel through empty space. In air it is a longitudinal pressure wave.
- Frequency is vibrations per second (hertz, Hz) and relates to pitch. Amplitude relates to loudness under comparable conditions. Do not confuse pitch with loudness.
- Echo is reflected sound heard separately when the delay is sufficient. Ultrasound has frequencies above the usual human hearing range; medical scans use echoes.

### Electricity and magnetism

- Current I = charge/time. Voltage is potential difference. Resistance opposes current. For an ohmic conductor at constant temperature, **V = IR**.
- Series circuit: current same through components; resistances add. Parallel circuit: voltage same across branches; household appliances are generally connected in parallel so they can operate independently.
- P = VI. A 12 V circuit with 2 A current has power 24 W. Electrical energy = power × time.
- A fuse or circuit breaker protects against excess current. Never experiment with mains electricity or damaged wires.
- A current-carrying wire produces a magnetic field. An electromagnet uses current in a coil; an iron core can strengthen it. Electric motors transform electrical energy into mechanical motion; generators convert mechanical energy into electrical energy.

## C. Chemistry: matter and changes

### Atoms, mixtures and reactions

- Element: one kind of atom. Compound: elements chemically bonded in fixed ratios. Mixture: substances physically combined, often separable by physical methods.
- Protons have positive charge, electrons negative, neutrons no electric charge. Atomic number is proton count; mass number is protons + neutrons.
- Isotopes have equal proton counts but different neutron counts. Ionic bonds involve electrostatic attraction between oppositely charged ions; covalent bonds involve shared electron pairs.
- Physical changes need not create a new substance; melting ice is an example. Chemical reactions form new substances: rusting is an example.
- Balanced equations conserve atoms. For instance, **2H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O** has four hydrogen and two oxygen atoms on each side.
- Exothermic reactions release energy to surroundings; endothermic reactions absorb energy. A catalyst changes the reaction rate without being consumed overall.

### Acids, bases and salts

- Acids generally release H⁺ in aqueous solution; bases produce OH⁻ in water or accept protons depending on the definition. Litmus: acid blue→red, base red→blue.
- At 25°C, pH below 7 is acidic, 7 neutral, above 7 basic. pH depends on temperature; do not taste chemicals.
- Neutralisation often produces salt and water: HCl + NaOH → NaCl + H₂O.
- Acid + carbonate usually produces salt, water and CO₂. Passing CO₂ through limewater makes it milky due to calcium carbonate.
- Baking soda: sodium hydrogen carbonate, NaHCO₃. Washing soda: Na₂CO₃·10H₂O. Plaster of Paris: CaSO₄·½H₂O; gypsum: CaSO₄·2H₂O.
- Rusting requires oxygen and moisture; paint, oil or galvanisation can protect iron. Galvanisation coats iron or steel with zinc.
- Carbon forms many compounds because it has four valence electrons and can bond with itself. Diamond and graphite are different forms of carbon with different structures and properties.
- Soap molecules can form micelles that help remove oily dirt; soap works less effectively in hard water because it forms insoluble scum with calcium/magnesium ions.

## D. Biology: living systems

### Cells and nutrition

- The cell is the basic structural and functional unit of life. The nucleus contains most genetic material in human cells; mitochondria play a major role in aerobic respiration; ribosomes make proteins.
- Plant cells have a cell wall and often chloroplasts; animal cells lack cell walls. Both plant and animal cells have cell membranes.
- Photosynthesis uses light energy to make carbohydrates from carbon dioxide and water; oxygen is a by-product. Plants also respire, including at night.
- Stomata are pores involved in gas exchange and water loss; guard cells regulate their opening.
- Respiration in cells releases usable energy from nutrients. Breathing brings air in and out; the two terms are related, not identical.
- Digestion breaks complex food into simpler absorbable substances. The small intestine is a major site of digestion and absorption. The liver makes bile; bile helps emulsify fats but is not itself a digestive enzyme.

### Transport, control and health

- Heart pumps blood; arteries carry blood away from it, veins towards it. Pulmonary artery carries relatively deoxygenated blood to lungs, an exception to the shortcut 'arteries carry oxygenated blood'.
- Red blood cells carry oxygen using haemoglobin; white blood cells support defence; platelets help clotting. Xylem carries water/minerals, phloem transports sugars.
- Kidneys filter blood and regulate water and salts; nephrons are their functional units. Neurons transmit signals; brain and spinal cord form the central nervous system.
- Hormones are chemical messengers. The pancreas produces insulin, which helps regulate blood glucose. The thyroid produces hormones involved in metabolism.
- Genes are stretches of DNA that carry information for functional products. Traits can be influenced by genes and environment. Evolution occurs across populations and generations, not because an individual chooses a trait.
- A vaccine prepares the immune system to respond to a pathogen. Antibiotics act against bacteria, not viruses; misuse contributes to antimicrobial resistance. Use medicines under medical guidance.
- Deficiency examples: vitamin C—scurvy; vitamin D deficiency can contribute to rickets in children; iron deficiency can cause anaemia. Disease can have multiple causes.

## E. Earth, space and environment

- Rotation causes day and night; revolution plus Earth's tilted axis explains seasonal changes. The Moon's phases come from our changing view of its sunlit half, not Earth's shadow except during a lunar eclipse.
- Weather is short-term atmospheric conditions; climate describes patterns over longer periods.
- Greenhouse gases help retain heat in the atmosphere; increases in human-caused greenhouse gases drive current global warming. The ozone layer and the greenhouse effect are different topics.
- Food chain: producer → consumer(s) → decomposers acting on dead matter. Energy is lost as heat at each transfer; material is recycled through ecosystems.
- Biodiversity means variety of life. Conservation includes habitats, species and genetic diversity. Native ecosystems are not interchangeable with any tree plantation.
- Water treatment and testing are important: clear-looking water can contain invisible microbes or chemicals; boiling kills many microbes but does not remove dissolved arsenic.

## F. Daily-life 'why?' challenge

1. **Why do pressure cookers cook faster?** Higher pressure raises water's boiling point, allowing a higher cooking temperature.
2. **Why is a metal spoon cold to touch?** It usually conducts heat away from your skin faster than wood at the same room temperature.
3. **Why do food packets use nitrogen?** Nitrogen can displace oxygen, slowing oxidation of fats; it is not a substitute for safe packaging and storage.
4. **Why does an iron nail rust faster in damp air?** Oxygen and water enable rusting.
5. **Why does sweating cool you?** Evaporation carries energy away from the skin.
6. **Why does the Moon not have a solar eclipse every month?** The Moon's orbit is tilted relative to Earth's orbital plane, so alignment is not exact every new moon.
7. **Why does a fuse melt?** Excess current heats the fuse wire, interrupting the circuit.
8. **Why do stars twinkle more than planets?** Atmospheric fluctuations alter light from point-like stars more noticeably; planets generally appear as small extended discs.
