# Science GK, India, Space and Scientific Thinking

## Science institutions and missions

- **ISRO:** Indian Space Research Organisation. Its headquarters is in Bengaluru. Check current mission updates at [ISRO](https://www.isro.gov.in/).
- **Chandrayaan-1:** India's first lunar mission; its instruments helped identify evidence of water/hydroxyl on the lunar surface.
- **Chandrayaan-2:** an orbiter and attempted lander/rover mission; the 2019 soft-landing attempt did not succeed.
- **Chandrayaan-3:** Vikram lander and Pragyan rover; a successful 2023 soft landing near the Moon's south polar region. This was not at the exact geographic south pole.
- **Aditya-L1:** India's solar observatory mission studying the Sun from a halo orbit around the Sun–Earth L1 region; it is not on the Sun.
- **Gaganyaan:** India's human spaceflight programme. Verify latest flight status before the test; don't memorise unverified future launch dates.
- **PSLV and GSLV:** Indian satellite launch vehicle families. A launch vehicle carries payloads; a satellite or probe is the payload, not the rocket itself.
- **DRDO:** Defence Research and Development Organisation. **CSIR:** Council of Scientific and Industrial Research. **ICMR:** Indian Council of Medical Research.
- **IMD:** India Meteorological Department; weather and related services. **CPCB:** Central Pollution Control Board.

## Scientists: contributions, not “invented everything”

- **C. V. Raman:** Raman effect, change in wavelength of scattered light; Nobel Prize in Physics 1930.
- **S. N. Bose:** work on quantum statistics; the term boson honours him.
- **Jagadish Chandra Bose:** pioneering work in radio/microwave research and plant responses.
- **Vikram Sarabhai:** key founder and leader of India's space programme.
- **A. P. J. Abdul Kalam:** aerospace scientist and former President; associated with India's missile and space programmes, which involved large teams.
- **M. S. Swaminathan:** agricultural scientist associated with India's Green Revolution, alongside many farmers and researchers.
- **Marie Curie:** pioneering radioactivity research; shared the 1903 Physics Nobel and received the 1911 Chemistry Nobel.
- **Isaac Newton:** laws of motion and universal gravitation; **Albert Einstein:** relativity and explanation of the photoelectric effect.
- **Charles Darwin:** natural selection as a mechanism of evolution; **Gregor Mendel:** foundational inheritance experiments with pea plants.
- **Edward Jenner:** early smallpox vaccination; **Alexander Fleming:** discovery of penicillin's antibacterial effect. Many others developed antibiotics into treatments.
- **Rosalind Franklin:** X-ray diffraction work critical to understanding DNA structure; many scientists contributed to the discovery.

## Everyday science and technology

- **GPS** uses signals from satellites to determine location. Phone location may also use Wi-Fi and cellular signals.
- **Binary:** computers represent data using bits 0 and 1. CPU processes instructions; RAM is temporary working memory; storage retains files.
- **AI** describes techniques that perform tasks associated with intelligence. Machine-learning systems learn patterns from data and can make mistakes or reproduce bias.
- **Cyber safety:** phishing attempts to trick you into revealing information; use strong unique passwords, enable multi-factor authentication where possible, and never share OTPs.
- **Electricity:** renewable sources include sunlight and wind; they can still have manufacturing, land and disposal impacts.
- **Climate vs ozone:** greenhouse-gas warming and stratospheric ozone depletion are different problems; ozone high in the atmosphere protects from UV, while ground-level ozone is an air pollutant.
- **Earthquake magnitude** characterises the size of the earthquake; intensity describes effects and shaking at a place. Magnitude and intensity are not interchangeable.
- **First aid:** seek adult and professional assistance for emergencies. Never put yourself in danger, particularly around electricity or unknown chemicals.

## Scientific temper: test an idea

1. Ask a clear question.
2. Form a testable hypothesis.
3. Change one independent variable; measure the dependent variable.
4. Keep relevant conditions similar; use a comparison/control where appropriate.
5. Repeat, record results, look for errors and share findings.
6. A result may support a hypothesis but does not prove it universally. Correlation alone does not establish causation.

**Example:** To test whether light affects seedling growth, use similar seedlings, water, soil and time, varying light exposure; measure growth. Multiple seedlings help reduce effects of individual differences. Avoid changing both light and water if you want to isolate light.

## Science GK traps

- A theory in science is a well-supported explanation, not merely a casual guess.
- Mass and weight are not interchangeable; neither are speed and velocity.
- Antibiotics do not cure viral infections; vaccination is preventive immune training.
- Plants both photosynthesise and respire; they do not photosynthesise without light.
- A lunar eclipse occurs at some full moons, not every full moon.
- CO₂ and oxygen cycle in ecosystems; extra CO₂ does not make pollution or warming harmless.
- A result on social media is not reliable merely because it is popular. Compare the claim with trusted sources and check its date.

## Current science news — make your own dated notes

The world changes quickly. For each event, write **what happened, organisation, date, and source**. Check [ISRO](https://www.isro.gov.in/), [PIB](https://pib.gov.in/), [WHO](https://www.who.int/) and your textbook/teacher. Don't confuse planned missions with completed ones.
