# Chekumuki Deep Science Master Guide

**Level:** Beyond quick facts, with Class 8–10 concepts, tricky applications and a little curiosity-driven extension. This is **not an official syllabus**. Learn the ideas rather than trying to memorise every line. For a short session, read the ★ sections and test yourself.

**Recall sequence:** What is it? → Why does it happen? → What changes if a condition changes? → What is a common exception?

## 1. Physics: explain the world

### ★ Motion, graphs and forces

- **Distance** is the path length. **Displacement** is the change in position, including direction. One full lap of a circular track has nonzero distance but zero displacement.
- **Average speed** = total distance / total time, not necessarily the simple average of two speeds. Example: 60 km in 1 h, then 60 km in 2 h: 120 km / 3 h = **40 km/h**, not 45 km/h.
- On a **distance–time graph**, slope represents speed when distance increases with time. A horizontal section means distance travelled is unchanged during that interval. For velocity–time graphs, slope gives acceleration; the signed area gives displacement.
- **Acceleration** can occur at constant speed if direction changes, as in circular motion. A net inward force changes the direction of velocity. Do not say that velocity is constant merely because speed is constant.
- Newton's third-law pair acts on **different objects**: Earth pulls a falling stone and the stone pulls Earth with equal and opposite force. Their accelerations are different because their masses differ.
- **Momentum = mass × velocity**. A force changes momentum. For a given momentum change, increasing stopping time reduces the average force; this helps explain airbags and catching a ball by moving the hands back.
- **Friction is not always bad:** tyre–road friction helps a vehicle accelerate or brake; unwanted friction produces heat and wear. Ball bearings change much sliding to rolling, often reducing resistance.

### Pressure, fluids and floating

- **P = F/A**. A snowshoe spreads weight across a larger area and reduces average pressure on snow. Pressure in a stationary liquid generally increases with depth because of the weight of fluid above.
- **Buoyancy:** the upward buoyant force equals the weight of the fluid displaced (Archimedes' principle). Floating is a force balance, not simply 'heavy things sink'. A floating object displaces fluid whose weight equals its own weight.
- A sealed steel ship can float because its overall average density, including enclosed air, is less than that of water. An object whose average density exceeds that of water tends to sink.
- **Density = mass/volume.** Example: a 150 g object occupies 50 cm³ → 150/50 = **3 g/cm³**. Compare compatible units before deciding whether something floats.

### ★ Heat and energy

- **Temperature** tells how hot a body is; **heat** is energy transferred due to temperature difference. Two objects at equal temperature can contain different amounts of internal energy.
- A metal spoon feels cooler than a wooden spoon at the same room temperature because metal generally conducts energy away from the skin faster; it is not automatically at a lower temperature.
- **Latent heat** is energy absorbed or released during a change of state without a temperature change during the phase transition under fixed conditions. Evaporation removes higher-energy molecules on average and can cool the remaining liquid/skin.
- Heat reaches Earth from the Sun through **radiation**, which does not require a medium. Conduction and convection require matter; convection involves bulk motion of fluid.
- Power = energy/time. A 60 W lamp used for 5 h consumes 60 × 5 = 300 Wh = **0.3 kWh**. **kW is power; kWh is energy.** Actual consumption may vary if rated power is not constant.
- Renewable energy sources are replenished naturally on human timescales, but solar panels, dams and wind turbines still involve materials, land and environmental trade-offs.

### ★ Light and vision

- **Reflection:** incidence angle = reflection angle, measured from the normal, not from the surface. A ray hitting perpendicular to the surface has incidence angle 0°.
- **Refraction:** light changes speed when entering a different medium. From air into glass it bends towards the normal for a non-normal ray. Frequency stays the same at the boundary; wavelength changes because speed changes.
- **Total internal reflection** requires light to go from a higher refractive-index medium to a lower one and the incidence angle to exceed the critical angle. Optical fibres use this principle to guide light.
- A **convex lens** can form a real inverted image or a virtual upright magnified image depending on object position. A **concave lens** forms a virtual, upright, diminished image for a real object.
- **Myopia:** a distant object's image tends to form in front of the retina; a diverging (concave) lens helps. **Hypermetropia:** difficulty with nearby vision is often corrected with a converging (convex) lens.
- The blue sky is mainly explained by stronger scattering of shorter visible wavelengths by air molecules. At sunrise and sunset a longer atmospheric path removes more blue light from the direct beam, often making the Sun appear red/orange. Clouds contain droplets of many sizes and usually scatter the visible colours more evenly.

### Sound and electricity

- Sound frequency is set by the source and perceived as pitch. When sound moves between media, frequency stays the same, but speed and wavelength may change. **v = fλ**: at 340 m/s and 170 Hz, wavelength = **2 m**.
- An echo involves a round trip. If sound speed is 340 m/s and an echo returns after 0.2 s, reflector distance = 340 × 0.2 / 2 = **34 m**. Always divide by 2 for travel to the wall and back.
- **Ohm's law** V = IR applies to ohmic conductors under appropriate constant conditions. Two 6 Ω resistors in series give 12 Ω. Two 6 Ω resistors in parallel give 3 Ω. An open circuit breaks the current path.
- Household appliances are connected in parallel so they receive the supply voltage and can be operated independently. A fuse protects against excess current; it is placed in the live conductor in a properly designed circuit. **Never experiment with mains electricity.**
- An electric motor converts electrical energy to mechanical energy; a generator converts mechanical energy to electrical energy. In practice some energy also becomes heat and sound.

## 2. Chemistry: particles, reactions and everyday substances

### ★ Atom, ions and periodic patterns

- **Atomic number Z = protons.** Mass number A = protons + neutrons. A neutral sodium-23 atom has 11 protons, 12 neutrons and 11 electrons. A Na⁺ ion has 11 protons, 12 neutrons and **10 electrons**.
- **Isotopes:** same protons, different neutrons (carbon-12 and carbon-14). **Isobars:** same mass number but different atomic numbers (argon-40 and calcium-40).
- Elements in a periodic-table **group** share a column and often show similar valence patterns; a **period** is a row. Trends have exceptions and depend on context—do not assume every property changes perfectly.
- Metals usually lose electrons to form positive ions; non-metals often gain or share electrons. **Ionic** compounds contain oppositely charged ions; **covalent** bonds involve shared electron pairs.
- Solid sodium chloride does not conduct electricity well because ions are fixed in place; molten NaCl or an aqueous solution conducts because mobile ions carry charge. Do not test this with household mains electricity.
- **Graphite**, a form of carbon, conducts electricity; diamond is a poor electrical conductor. Both are carbon, but their arrangements and properties differ. Elemental **iodine** is a lustrous non-metal; most metals are solid at room temperature, while mercury is liquid.

### Reactions and observations

- Chemical equations conserve atoms. In **2Mg + O₂ → 2MgO**, there are two Mg and two O atoms on each side.
- **Combination:** A + B → AB. **Decomposition:** AB → A + B. **Displacement:** a more reactive metal can displace a less reactive one from a suitable salt solution. **Double displacement:** ions exchange partners; sometimes a precipitate forms.
- Example: Fe + CuSO₄ → FeSO₄ + Cu. Iron displaces copper because it is more reactive. The blue solution can become greenish and copper can deposit; conditions matter.
- Oxidation can mean **gain of oxygen** or **loss of electrons**; reduction can mean loss of oxygen or gain of electrons. Both occur together in a redox reaction.
- A **catalyst** changes reaction rate without being consumed overall; it is not a fuel and does not change the mass conservation law.
- A packet of crisps may be filled with nitrogen to reduce oxygen exposure and slow oxidation of fats. Nitrogen is not a promise that the food never spoils.

### ★ Acids, bases, salts and materials

- Acids produce H⁺ ions in water; alkaline solutions contain OH⁻. At **25°C**, pH < 7 acidic, pH 7 neutral, pH > 7 basic. Acidity and pH should be considered with temperature and concentration; do not taste chemicals.
- **Dilution rule:** add acid slowly to water, never water to concentrated acid; dilution releases heat. Only trained adults should handle concentrated chemicals.
- Some metal oxides, including **Al₂O₃ and ZnO**, are amphoteric: they react with acids and with bases under suitable conditions.
- Baking soda **NaHCO₃**; washing soda **Na₂CO₃·10H₂O**; gypsum **CaSO₄·2H₂O**; plaster of Paris **CaSO₄·½H₂O**. Baking powder often contains baking soda plus an edible acid.
- Temporary hard water can often be softened by boiling; dissolved calcium/magnesium salts reduce soap's effectiveness. Detergents can work better in hard water, but different detergents have different environmental impacts.
- **Alloy:** a material made by combining a metal with other element(s). Steel is primarily iron and carbon; stainless steel includes chromium. An alloy is not necessarily a chemical compound of fixed composition.

## 3. Biology: systems working together

### ★ Cells and transport

- **Diffusion** is net movement from higher to lower concentration due to random motion. **Osmosis** is net movement of water through a selectively permeable membrane from a region of higher water potential to lower water potential. Do not describe osmosis as movement of all solutes.
- **Plant cell wall** helps support and protect the cell; the cell membrane controls exchange. Mitochondria are major sites of aerobic respiration; chloroplasts perform photosynthesis. Mature mammalian red blood cells lack a nucleus—do not say every human cell has one.
- **Xylem** mainly carries water and minerals from roots; **phloem** transports sugars and other organic substances. Transpiration helps draw water upwards; root pressure can also contribute in some conditions.
- Photosynthesis stores energy in chemical products. Plants **respire day and night**; net gas exchange changes with light and conditions. Stomata regulate gas exchange and water loss through guard cells.

### Human body: why exceptions matter

- **Double circulation:** blood passes through the heart twice in one complete circuit: heart → lungs → heart, then heart → body → heart. The four-chambered human heart separates oxygen-rich and oxygen-poor streams.
- Arteries carry blood **away** from the heart; the pulmonary artery carries oxygen-poor blood to the lungs. Veins carry blood **towards** the heart; pulmonary veins carry oxygen-rich blood to the heart.
- Gas exchange occurs across thin walls of **alveoli** and nearby capillaries. Haemoglobin transports much of the oxygen in blood. Carbon monoxide is dangerous partly because it binds strongly to haemoglobin.
- Food moves through the gut by **peristalsis**, muscular waves. The liver produces bile, which helps emulsify fats; the pancreas produces digestive enzymes and hormones such as insulin. Kidneys filter blood; nephrons carry out filtration and reabsorption.
- **Reflex action** is a rapid, automatic response; a reflex arc often involves the spinal cord. The brain can still be informed about the event.
- **Homeostasis** means maintaining internal conditions within suitable ranges, not keeping every value perfectly constant. Sweating and changes in skin blood flow help regulate body temperature.

### ★ Heredity, health and microorganisms

- DNA carries hereditary information. A **gene** is a DNA sequence that produces a functional product; alternative forms are **alleles**. Traits can be affected by multiple genes and the environment.
- In a simple Mendelian model, crossing Tt × Tt gives TT, Tt, Tt and tt. If T is fully dominant, expected genotype ratio = **1:2:1**, and expected phenotype ratio = **3:1**. Individual families or tiny samples need not exactly match ratios.
- Dominant does **not** mean more common, better or stronger. Acquired body changes such as a haircut are not inherited as DNA changes by offspring.
- Viruses, bacteria, fungi and protozoa are different. Antibiotics treat some bacterial infections, not viral flu or common colds. Misuse contributes to antimicrobial resistance; use medicines only as advised by a qualified clinician. [WHO](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/antimicrobial-resistance)
- Vaccines stimulate immune responses; they do not guarantee that no vaccinated person will ever become infected. They can greatly reduce risk of serious illness for targeted diseases.
- **Vector:** an organism that transmits a pathogen. Female *Anopheles* mosquitoes can transmit malaria parasites; female *Aedes* mosquitoes can transmit dengue viruses. Malaria is caused by *Plasmodium* parasites, not by the mosquito itself.

## 4. Environment and Earth

- **Food web** connects multiple food chains. Energy flows through ecosystems with losses, including heat; matter such as carbon is cycled. A pesticide can become more concentrated at higher trophic levels in certain circumstances (**biomagnification**).
- Carbon dioxide is a greenhouse gas, but air pollution and climate change are not identical concepts. **PM2.5** are fine particles that can penetrate deep into the lungs; CO₂ is a gas. Pollution controls should target the actual pollutant.
- Stratospheric ozone absorbs much UV radiation; ground-level ozone can harm health and plants. Ozone depletion is distinct from greenhouse warming, although atmospheric processes interact.
- A river reaching the sea is not necessarily wasted water: downstream flows support fish, wetlands, sediment transport and communities. Safe water policy must consider both people and ecosystems.
- **Earthquake magnitude** describes earthquake size; **intensity** describes shaking/effects at a location. Earthquakes are associated with sudden movement along faults; they cannot be reliably predicted to an exact time and place by simple animal-behaviour claims.
- The seasons arise mainly from Earth's axial tilt and revolution, not from Earth being closest to the Sun in summer everywhere. Opposite hemispheres have opposite seasons.

## 5. Science & technology GK with substance

- **Raman effect:** light scattered by a material may change wavelength because energy is exchanged with the material. Used in Raman spectroscopy to investigate substances.
- **S. N. Bose:** work with Einstein on quantum statistics; particles called bosons are named in his honour. **J. C. Bose:** research in radio/microwaves and plant responses.
- **Chandrayaan-3:** ISRO's Vikram lander and Pragyan rover landed near the lunar south polar region in August 2023. The rover's APXS and LIBS were designed to investigate elemental composition. [ISRO mission details](https://www.isro.gov.in/Chandrayaan3_Details.html)
- **Aditya-L1:** a solar observatory placed in a halo orbit around the Sun–Earth L1 region, not landed on the Sun. **XPoSat:** an ISRO mission for X-ray polarimetry of bright X-ray sources. [ISRO report](https://www.isro.gov.in/media_isro/pdf/Publications/Space_Research_2022_2023_040625.pdf)
- **Artificial intelligence:** models identify patterns or produce outputs based on training and design; confident output is not guaranteed correct. Check primary sources and protect private data.
- **Semiconductor:** a material whose conductivity can be controlled, important in devices such as diodes and transistors. Silicon is widely used; a semiconductor is neither simply a perfect conductor nor a perfect insulator in all circumstances.
- **Satellites:** a satellite remains in orbit because gravity continuously changes its direction; it is not 'beyond Earth's gravity'. Astronauts in orbit experience apparent weightlessness because they and their spacecraft are in continuous free fall together.

## 6. Mini-lab: think like an investigator

**Question:** Does fertiliser X increase plant growth? Use similar plants, soil, pots, water and time; randomly assign multiple plants to fertiliser and comparison groups where possible. Measure height or biomass using the same method. If fertiliser and light change together, you cannot confidently attribute differences to fertiliser alone. Repetition reduces—but does not eliminate—uncertainty. Avoid unsafe chemicals.

- **Independent variable:** fertiliser treatment. **Dependent variable:** chosen growth measurement. **Controls:** other relevant conditions.
- A **control group** helps compare outcomes. A larger, representative sample can reduce random uncertainty, but does not automatically eliminate bias.
- **Correlation ≠ causation.** Ice-cream sales and swimming may both rise in hot weather; one does not necessarily cause the other.
- Check whether a chart uses a fair axis, whether a sample is large enough and whether the source is reliable. A scientific theory is a well-supported explanatory framework, not just a guess.

## 7. Twelve quick 'why' questions

1. Why do you move forward when a bus brakes? → Your body tends to maintain motion (inertia).
2. Why does a straw appear bent in water? → Refraction changes light's direction at the boundary.
3. Why can a boat float but a steel nail sink? → Shape and enclosed air affect the boat's overall average density and displaced-water weight.
4. Why is there no echo in space? → Sound requires a medium.
5. Why does sodium chloride conduct when molten but not as a solid? → Ions are mobile when molten.
6. Why does nitrogen help keep crisps fresh? → It reduces oxygen exposure and slows fat oxidation.
7. Why can a desert plant reduce water loss by closing stomata? → Less water vapour escapes, though gas exchange is also affected.
8. Why do arteries not always carry oxygen-rich blood? → They are classified by direction from the heart; the pulmonary artery is an example.
9. Why don't antibiotics cure influenza? → Influenza is caused by a virus; antibiotics target bacteria.
10. Why is a cloudy night often warmer than a clear night under otherwise similar conditions? → Clouds can absorb and re-emit outgoing infrared radiation; local conditions also matter.
11. Why can lightning be seen before thunder is heard? → Light travels much faster than sound.
12. Why is 1 kWh not a unit of power? → It measures energy: power multiplied by time.
