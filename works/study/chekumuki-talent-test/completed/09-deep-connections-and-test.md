## 1. ★ Think like a scientist

**Observation (పరిశీలన):** what you notice or measure. **Hypothesis (ఊహ):** a testable possible explanation. **Experiment (ప్రయోగం):** a controlled way to compare explanations. **Conclusion:** what the evidence supports, with uncertainty.

Example: Do plants grow better with more light? Use similar plants, soil, water, pots and time; change light exposure. Measure growth with the same method. Several plants in each group help reduce the effect of individual differences. Very strong light or heat may create additional effects. Correlation alone does not prove cause; even a controlled experiment has limits.

**Fair test:** independent variable = what is changed; dependent variable = what is measured; controlled variables = other conditions kept as similar as possible. A control group is a comparison baseline, not a guarantee of truth. Repeat and record unexpected results.

## 2. Physics: changes and causes

### ★ Motion and forces

Velocity includes speed and direction. If a cyclist turns at constant speed, velocity changes: acceleration is present. A balanced force means **net force zero**, not necessarily no motion: an object can move at constant velocity if other conditions permit. Seat belts increase the time over which a passenger's momentum changes, reducing average force in a collision.

**Graph reasoning:** a position–time graph's slope gives velocity; a velocity–time graph's slope gives acceleration and signed area gives displacement. A horizontal velocity–time line above zero means constant nonzero velocity, not rest.

### Energy and electricity

Energy is conserved in a closed-system accounting, but useful energy often spreads into less useful forms such as heat. Efficiency = useful output / total input × 100%. If 80 J of 100 J supplied becomes useful output, efficiency is **80%**; the other 20 J has not vanished.

In series the same current passes through components; a broken series path stops the circuit. Parallel branches have the same voltage across them, while current can divide between branches. With a fixed voltage and an ohmic resistor, V = IR: 12 V across 4 Ω gives **3 A**. Changing resistor temperature may change resistance. Never experiment with household mains.

**Power and bill:** 100 W for 2 h = 200 Wh = 0.2 kWh. A unit on an electricity bill ordinarily means one kWh; the bill also depends on tariff and other charges.

### Light and waves

Frequency (ఆవృత్తి) is cycles per second, in hertz. Wave speed = frequency × wavelength. A 100 Hz wave travelling at 300 m/s has wavelength 3 m. A wave transfers energy without necessarily carrying matter along with it over the full distance. Sound needs a medium; electromagnetic waves can travel through vacuum.

Light bends at a boundary because speed differs between media; frequency remains unchanged across the boundary. A straw in water appears bent because of refraction. Mirrors form images by reflection. In a plane mirror the image is virtual, upright and the same size; it appears as far behind the mirror as the object is in front.

## 3. Chemistry: particles and reactions

**Element:** atoms with the same proton number. **Compound:** elements chemically combined in definite proportions. **Mixture:** substances physically combined in variable proportions. Air is a mixture; pure water is a compound. Dissolving salt in water forms a mixture, even though the dissolved particles are tiny.

**Physical vs chemical:** melting ice changes state; rusting forms new substances. Burning a candle includes both: wax melts physically and wax vapour reacts chemically. Mass is conserved in a closed system; a gas escaping from an open container can make the remaining material appear lighter.

**Acids and bases:** pH below 7 is acidic, above 7 basic, and 7 neutral at 25°C. pH is logarithmic: pH 3 has ten times the hydrogen-ion activity of pH 4 under the definition, not merely one unit more. Never taste laboratory chemicals. Indicators show a change, not necessarily an exact pH.

**Metals:** many conduct electricity because electrons can move through them. Graphite, a carbon allotrope and non-metal, also conducts; avoid saying all non-metals are insulators. Corrosion needs suitable conditions; paint or galvanising can protect iron by limiting exposure, and zinc can also offer sacrificial protection.

## 4. Biology: systems and interdependence

**Cell (కణం):** basic structural and functional unit of life. A cell membrane controls movement of substances; plant cells usually have a cell wall and chloroplasts in photosynthetic tissues. Mature mammalian red blood cells lack a nucleus: exceptions matter.

**Photosynthesis and respiration:** photosynthesis stores energy in chemical compounds using light; cellular respiration releases usable energy from food. Plants respire day and night; photosynthesis requires light. Leaves exchange gases mainly through stomata, whose opening also affects water loss.

**Food webs:** energy flows from producers through consumers and decomposers; nutrients cycle through ecosystems. Removing one species can affect many others. Decomposers break down dead matter; they are not the same as all scavengers.

**Immunity:** vaccines train the immune system to respond to specific threats. Antibiotics act against bacteria, not viruses such as those causing influenza. Misuse encourages antimicrobial resistance; seek qualified medical advice rather than self-prescribing.

## 5. Earth, space and environment

Seasons mainly arise from Earth's tilted axis and its revolution, not simply changing Earth–Sun distance. Day and night result mainly from rotation. Moon phases arise from changing viewing geometry of the sunlit Moon; a lunar eclipse requires Earth to be aligned between the Sun and Moon and does not happen every full moon because the Moon's orbit is tilted.

**Weather** is the atmosphere's short-term state; **climate** describes patterns over longer periods. Greenhouse gases absorb and emit infrared radiation, affecting Earth's energy balance. The natural greenhouse effect makes Earth habitable; additional greenhouse gases from human activities contribute to warming. Weather on one day does not by itself establish a long-term climate trend.

**Water cycle:** evaporation/transpiration → condensation → precipitation → runoff and infiltration. Groundwater is not an unlimited underground lake; recharge and extraction vary by location.

## 6. ★ Maths without fear

Write the units and ask whether your answer makes sense. These are ordinary school-level examples, not Olympiad puzzles.

1. **Percentage:** 15% of 200 = 10% (20) + 5% (10) = **30**.
2. **Ratio:** Divide 60 in ratio 2:3. Total parts 5; one part 12; answers **24 and 36**.
3. **Average:** 4, 6, 8 → (4 + 6 + 8)/3 = **6**. Average speed is total distance / total time, not always the average of speeds.
4. **Speed:** 120 km in 3 h → **40 km/h**. At 40 km/h for 30 minutes, distance = 40 × 0.5 = **20 km**.
5. **Area:** a 6 cm by 4 cm rectangle has area **24 cm²** and perimeter **20 cm**. Area and perimeter are different.
6. **Probability:** a fair six-sided die has probability **1/6** of landing on 4. Each independent roll still has the same probability regardless of previous rolls.
7. **Simple interest:** ₹1,000 at 5% per year for 2 years gives interest 1,000 × 5 × 2 / 100 = **₹100**; total ₹1,100, assuming simple interest and no extra charges.
8. **Unit conversion:** 1 m = 100 cm; 1 m² = 10,000 cm² because both dimensions change.

## 8. Original self-test: answer first, then check

1. A car moves around a curve at constant speed. Is it accelerating?
2. What is the current through an ohmic 6 Ω resistor at 12 V?
3. How much energy in kWh does a 1 kW heater use in 30 minutes?
4. Can sound travel through a vacuum?
5. Is air an element, compound or mixture?
6. What is conserved during a chemical reaction in a closed system?
7. Do plants respire at night?
8. Are antibiotics a treatment for viral influenza?
9. What mainly causes seasons?
10. A 20 cm string is cut into pieces in ratio 1:3. What are their lengths?
11. A student changes both water and light for a plant experiment. Why is the result hard to interpret?
12. A lake's water looks clear. Does that prove it is safe to drink?
13. Why can a large metal ship float?
14. Does a 0.2 probability mean exactly two successes must occur in every ten trials?
15. Is a horizontal line on a velocity–time graph necessarily zero velocity?
16. What happens to light's frequency when it crosses from air into glass?

### Answers with reasons

1. **Yes**, its direction and thus velocity change. 2. **2 A**, using I = V/R. 3. **0.5 kWh**, since half an hour is 0.5 h. 4. **No**, sound needs a material medium. 5. **Mixture**, of gases in variable proportions. 6. **Total mass**, although substances change. 7. **Yes**, cellular respiration continues. 8. **No**, antibiotics target bacteria, not influenza viruses. 9. **Axial tilt and revolution**, changing sunlight angle and day length. 10. **5 cm and 15 cm**, four equal parts. 11. **Two variables changed**, so the cause cannot be isolated. 12. **No**, invisible microbes or chemicals may be present. 13. **Its overall average density including enclosed air can be less than water**, and buoyancy balances its weight when floating. 14. **No**, it describes likelihood, not a guaranteed count. 15. **No**, it shows constant velocity, possibly nonzero. 16. **It remains the same**; speed and wavelength change.
