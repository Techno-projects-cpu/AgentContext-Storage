## 1. ★ Physics: ask what changes and what stays the same

### Motion, force and energy

- **Inertia (జడత్వం)** is resistance to a change in velocity. When a bus starts suddenly, passengers may seem to fall backward because their bodies tend to maintain their previous state of motion; the floor moves forward beneath them.
- **Mass** measures inertia; **weight** is the gravitational force on an object. The same object has approximately the same mass on Earth and the Moon, but weighs less on the Moon because gravitational acceleration is smaller.
- **Action and reaction** are equal and opposite forces on different bodies. A book resting on a table experiences Earth's pull and the table's upward force; those two forces act on the same book and are **not** a third-law pair.
- **Work** in the elementary mechanical sense involves a force and displacement in the force's direction. Holding a heavy bag still may tire muscles, but the supporting force does no mechanical work on the stationary bag in that simplified sense.
- **Power (శక్తి వినియోగ రేటు)** is the rate of energy transfer: 1 watt = 1 joule per second. A more powerful motor can do the same work in less time, assuming comparable conditions; power alone does not tell you the total energy used.
- A pendulum's energy changes between gravitational potential and kinetic energy. Air resistance and friction gradually transfer some mechanical energy to the surroundings; total energy is still conserved when the whole system is considered.

### Pressure, heat and fluids

- A sharp knife needs less force than a blunt one for the same pressure because the force acts over a smaller area: **pressure = force / area**.
- Liquid pressure generally increases with depth. Two holes at different depths in a container can produce different water jets because the pressure differs; actual jet distance also depends on height and other conditions.
- A pressure cooker raises the boiling temperature of water by increasing pressure, so food can cook faster. It does not make boiling water infinitely hot. Use cookers only according to the manufacturer's safety instructions.
- **Specific heat capacity** describes how much energy is needed to change a given mass's temperature by a certain amount. Water's relatively high specific heat helps moderate temperature changes, though many factors affect a place's climate.
- Water expands on freezing under ordinary conditions. Ice is less dense than liquid water and normally floats; ice on a pond's surface can reduce heat loss from the water below.
- **Conduction** transfers thermal energy through interactions within matter; **convection** involves bulk movement of fluid; **radiation** can travel through a vacuum. A thermos reduces, but does not eliminate, heat transfer.

### Waves, light and electricity

- **Frequency (ఆవృత్తి)** determines perceived pitch for a simple sound; amplitude is associated with loudness, although perception depends on other factors too. Sound needs a medium; light can travel through vacuum.
- A plane mirror reverses the direction perpendicular to its surface, not literally left and right in every coordinate system. Its image is virtual, upright and the same size for an ordinary plane mirror.
- A prism disperses white light because refractive index depends on wavelength. A rainbow involves refraction, dispersion and internal reflection in water droplets; many droplets contribute to the light seen by one observer.
- **Current** is the rate of electric charge flow. **Voltage** is energy transferred per unit charge. A battery provides a potential difference; a complete conducting path is required for sustained current in a simple circuit.
- In a parallel circuit, branches have the same potential difference; the total current is the sum of branch currents. A short circuit can produce a dangerously large current. Never investigate this with mains power.

## 2. ★ Chemistry: look at particles, not just appearances

### Atoms and bonding

- Atomic number = number of **protons**. Isotopes have the same number of protons but different numbers of neutrons. A neutral atom has equal numbers of protons and electrons; an ion has a net charge because electrons have been lost or gained.
- **Valency (సంయోజకత)** is an elementary measure of combining capacity; it is not always the same as an atom's ionic charge in every compound. Sodium chloride is written NaCl; water is H₂O.
- **Ionic bonding** involves attraction between oppositely charged ions. **Covalent bonding** involves shared electron pairs. Solid sodium chloride does not conduct well, but its molten form or aqueous solution conducts because ions can move.
- A chemical equation must balance each type of atom: **2H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O**. The coefficients count molecules (or moles); do not change a formula's subscripts just to balance it.

### Changes, mixtures and reactions

- **Oxidation** can be described in school-level reactions as gaining oxygen or losing hydrogen; more generally it involves loss of electrons. Rusting is a form of oxidation, but not all oxidation is rusting.
- Rusting of iron usually needs both oxygen and water. Paint, oil and galvanisation can protect iron; zinc coating can also protect iron sacrificially when scratched under appropriate conditions.
- Filtration can separate insoluble sand from water, but ordinary filtration does not remove dissolved salt. Evaporation can leave dissolved salt behind; distillation can collect the condensed water.
- A **catalyst (ఉత్ప్రేరకం)** changes the rate of a reaction and is regenerated overall; it does not mean that a reaction must become faster in every possible definition, nor does it create products without reactants. Enzymes are biological catalysts.
- Acid plus base can produce salt and water in a neutralisation reaction. The final mixture is not automatically exactly pH 7: amounts, strengths and temperature matter.

## 3. ★ Biology: connect structures to jobs

### Cells and transport

- The **cell membrane** is selectively permeable. **Diffusion (వ్యాపనం)** is net movement of particles from higher to lower concentration under suitable conditions. **Osmosis (ద్రవాభిసరణం)** is net movement of water through a selectively permeable membrane from a region of higher water potential to lower water potential.
- Red blood cells carry oxygen using haemoglobin. Mature mammalian red blood cells lack a nucleus, an exception to the idea that every cell has one. White blood cells help defend the body; platelets help blood clot.
- Arteries carry blood **away from** the heart and veins carry it **towards** the heart. Oxygen content is not the definition: pulmonary arteries carry oxygen-poor blood from the heart towards the lungs.
- **Xylem** transports water and dissolved minerals mainly from roots upwards; **phloem** transports sugars and other organic substances to parts of the plant that need or store them. Transpiration is loss of water vapour, mainly through stomata in many plants.

### Reproduction, heredity and health

- **Genes** are segments of DNA associated with inherited functions or traits. A chromosome carries many genes. Traits can depend on multiple genes and the environment, not just one simple dominant/recessive pair.
- Sexual reproduction generally combines genetic material from two gametes; asexual reproduction involves one parent and can produce genetically similar offspring, though mutations can occur.
- Insulin helps regulate blood glucose. A hormone is a chemical messenger, not a substitute for a healthy lifestyle or medical care.
- A vaccine prepares the immune system for a particular threat. Antibiotics treat certain bacterial infections, not viral colds or influenza; resistance means microorganisms become less susceptible, not that a person's body becomes resistant to all antibiotics. Seek qualified medical advice.
- Digestion breaks large food molecules into smaller ones that can be absorbed. Most nutrient absorption occurs in the **small intestine**; the large intestine absorbs water and salts and forms faeces.

### Ecosystems

- A **food chain** is one path of feeding relationships; a **food web** connects many such paths. Energy available to organisms generally decreases between trophic levels because energy is used in life processes and dissipated as heat.
- Decomposers such as many bacteria and fungi break down dead material and return nutrients to the environment. Nutrients cycle; energy flows through ecosystems and is eventually dispersed as heat.
- Invasive species can disrupt ecosystems, but a species living outside its original range is not automatically invasive; invasiveness involves harmful spread or impact.

## 4. Earth, space and scientific organisations

- **Latitude** helps describe position north or south of the equator; **longitude** describes position east or west of the prime meridian. Indian Standard Time is UTC+5:30, based on 82°30′ E.
- The **monsoon** involves seasonal changes in winds and rainfall influenced by land, ocean and atmospheric circulation. It is not simply a daily sea breeze.
- **Weather** describes conditions over short periods; **climate** concerns patterns over longer periods. A single unusually hot or cold day is not enough to establish a climate trend.
- A solar eclipse occurs when the Moon lies between Earth and the Sun; a lunar eclipse occurs when Earth lies between the Sun and the Moon. Eclipses do not occur every month because the Moon's orbit is tilted. **Never look directly at the Sun** without appropriate certified solar viewing equipment and expert guidance.
- A geostationary satellite appears fixed above one location on Earth's equator, moving in the direction of Earth's rotation with a period matching Earth's rotation. Not every satellite is geostationary.
- **ISRO** works on India's space programme; **IMD** provides weather and climate services; **ICMR** supports medical research; **CSIR** is a network for scientific and industrial research. Check current missions and officeholders near the competition because they can change.
- **C. V. Raman** is associated with the Raman effect; **S. N. Bose** with quantum statistics; **Janaki Ammal** with botany and cytogenetics; **Asima Chatterjee** with research in organic chemistry. Discoveries involve evidence and often teams, not just names to memorise.

## 5. ★ Easy maths: six reusable tools

1. **Percentage:** 12% of 250 = 10% (25) + 2% (5) = **30**. A 12% increase on 250 gives 280; the increase is not the final amount.
2. **Ratio:** Divide 84 in the ratio 3:4. Seven parts total; 84 ÷ 7 = 12 per part; answers **36 and 48**.
3. **Average:** The average of 5, 7, 9 and 11 is 32 ÷ 4 = **8**. If all numbers increase by 2, the average also increases by 2.
4. **Speed:** 150 km in 3 h is **50 km/h**. A 30-minute journey at 50 km/h covers 50 × 0.5 = **25 km** if speed is constant.
5. **Area and perimeter:** A 5 cm by 3 cm rectangle has area **15 cm²** and perimeter **16 cm**. A square of side 4 cm has area 16 cm² and perimeter 16 cm; equal numerical values do not mean area and perimeter are the same quantity.
6. **Probability:** In a bag with 3 red and 2 blue balls, a randomly selected ball has probability **3/5** of being red. For a second draw without replacement, the probabilities change depending on the first result.

## 6. Everyday applications and common traps

- **Why do we see lightning before hearing thunder?** Light travels much faster than sound through air.
- **Why does a wet cloth cool?** Evaporation transfers energy from the surroundings; humidity and airflow affect the rate.
- **Why does a metal spoon feel cooler than a wooden spoon at the same temperature?** Metal generally transfers heat away from the skin faster.
- **Why does a fuse protect a circuit?** Excess current heats and melts its designed element, breaking the circuit. It is one part of electrical safety; never replace it with arbitrary wire.
- **Why can a ship made from steel float?** Its overall average density, including enclosed air, can be less than water; the buoyant force balances its weight when floating.
- **Why does an astronaut in orbit feel weightless?** The astronaut and spacecraft are in continuous free fall together; gravity at orbital height is not zero.
- **Why does soap help remove grease?** Soap molecules have parts that interact with water and oily material, helping form tiny aggregates that can be washed away.
- **Why is a blanket useful?** It slows heat transfer from the body, largely by trapping air; it does not create body heat by itself.

## 7. Original practice: 30 MCQs

Try first without looking at the answers. These are **practice questions, not past papers**.

1. When a moving bus stops suddenly, a passenger tends to move forward because of: A) inertia B) buoyancy C) magnetism D) refraction.
2. On the Moon, compared with Earth, an object's mass is approximately: A) zero B) unchanged C) one-sixth D) six times greater.
3. A 60 W lamp operates for 2 h. Energy used is: A) 0.12 kWh B) 1.2 kWh C) 120 kWh D) 30 kWh.
4. Pressure increases when the same force acts on: A) a larger area B) a smaller area C) the same area D) no surface.
5. Which can travel through a vacuum? A) Sound in air B) Light C) A water wave D) A wave on a rope.
6. In parallel branches connected to an ideal source, each branch has the same: A) current B) resistance C) voltage D) power.
7. A rainbow's formation involves: A) refraction and internal reflection B) only conduction C) only magnetism D) only diffraction by soil.
8. If a pendulum slows due to friction, its mechanical energy: A) vanishes B) partly transfers to surroundings C) becomes massless D) increases forever.
9. Isotopes of the same element have the same number of: A) neutrons B) protons C) neutrons and electrons in every ion D) nucleons.
10. A neutral atom has 11 protons. How many electrons does it have? A) 10 B) 11 C) 12 D) 22.
11. Which mixture is best separated initially by ordinary filtration? A) Salt dissolved in water B) Sand in water C) Sugar dissolved in water D) Air.
12. Solid sodium chloride conducts poorly mainly because: A) it contains no ions B) its ions are not free to move through the solid C) it contains no sodium D) electrons are flowing freely.
13. Which equation is balanced? A) H₂ + O₂ → H₂O B) 2H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O C) H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O D) 2H₂ + 2O₂ → H₂O.
14. Rusting of iron generally requires: A) oxygen and water B) nitrogen only C) pure gold D) sunlight only.
15. Which transports sugars in plants? A) Xylem B) Phloem C) Stomata D) Root cap only.
16. Arteries are defined as vessels that carry blood: A) always rich in oxygen B) away from the heart C) always towards the heart D) only to the lungs.
17. Most nutrient absorption during digestion occurs in the: A) stomach B) small intestine C) large intestine D) oesophagus.
18. Which cause of illness is not treated by antibiotics? A) Some susceptible bacteria B) The influenza virus C) Some bacterial infections D) Some susceptible streptococci.
19. In an ecosystem, decomposers help: A) recycle nutrients B) stop energy transfer C) produce sunlight D) prevent all decay.
20. Which is a gene? A) A segment of DNA associated with inherited functions or traits B) An entire ecosystem C) A blood vessel D) A species of bacterium.
21. Seasons are mainly caused by: A) Earth's axial tilt and revolution B) the Moon's phases C) Earth's daily rotation alone D) changing distance from the Sun alone.
22. A lunar eclipse requires: A) Moon between Sun and Earth B) Earth between Sun and Moon C) Sun between Earth and Moon D) a new Moon every day.
23. IMD is associated with: A) weather services B) manufacturing all medicines C) building all schools D) creating the Moon.
24. The Raman effect is associated with: A) C. V. Raman B) Gregor Mendel C) Isaac Newton's apple alone D) Charles Darwin.
25. 15% of 200 is: A) 15 B) 20 C) 30 D) 45.
26. Divide 50 in the ratio 2:3. The larger part is: A) 10 B) 20 C) 25 D) 30.
27. A person travels 90 km in 2 h. Average speed is: A) 45 km/h B) 90 km/h C) 180 km/h D) 92 km/h.
28. The perimeter of a rectangle 6 cm long and 2 cm wide is: A) 8 cm B) 12 cm C) 16 cm D) 24 cm².
29. A fair die is rolled. Probability of a number greater than 4 is: A) 1/6 B) 1/3 C) 1/2 D) 2/3.
30. To test how light affects plant growth fairly, which is best? A) Change light and water together B) Change light while keeping water and other conditions similar C) Compare different species and different soils D) Measure only the tallest plant once.

### Answers and explanations

1. **A:** inertia keeps the body moving as the bus slows. 2. **B:** mass stays approximately the same; weight changes. 3. **A:** 60 W × 2 h = 120 Wh = 0.12 kWh. 4. **B:** pressure = force/area. 5. **B:** electromagnetic light does not need a medium. 6. **C:** parallel branches share the same voltage; their currents can differ. 7. **A:** droplets refract and disperse light and internally reflect it. 8. **B:** mechanical energy is transferred, mainly as thermal energy.

9. **B:** an element is identified by proton number. 10. **B:** a neutral atom has equal numbers of protons and electrons. 11. **B:** insoluble sand can be trapped by a filter. 12. **B:** mobile ions carry charge in molten or dissolved salt, but are fixed in the solid lattice. 13. **B:** both sides contain four hydrogen atoms and two oxygen atoms. 14. **A:** ordinary rusting requires oxygen and moisture. 15. **B:** phloem transports sugars. 16. **B:** pulmonary arteries show why oxygen content is not the definition.

17. **B:** most absorption occurs in the small intestine. 18. **B:** influenza is viral. 19. **A:** decomposers return nutrients to ecosystems. 20. **A:** genes are DNA segments, though traits can involve several genes and environmental influences. 21. **A:** tilt changes the angle and duration of sunlight through the year. 22. **B:** Earth casts its shadow on the Moon. 23. **A:** IMD provides meteorological services. 24. **A:** Raman investigated light scattering.

25. **C:** 10% is 20 and 5% is 10, making 30. 26. **D:** five parts; one part is 10, so three parts are 30. 27. **A:** 90 ÷ 2 = 45. 28. **C:** 2 × (6 + 2) = 16 cm. 29. **B:** outcomes 5 and 6 give 2/6 = 1/3. 30. **B:** changing one main variable makes the effect easier to interpret; use multiple plants if possible.
