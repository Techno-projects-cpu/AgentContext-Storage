# The physical world: why things happen ⚡

## Motion, forces and energy

1. **Speed = distance/time.** A cyclist covering 45 km in 3 h averages 15 km/h; average speed uses total distance and total time.
2. **Velocity:** speed in a specified direction. A car can have constant speed yet changing velocity when it turns.
3. **Acceleration:** rate of change of velocity; slowing down or changing direction can also involve acceleration.
4. **Inertia (జడత్వం):** resistance to a change in motion. A passenger tends to continue moving forward when a bus brakes; a seat belt restrains them.
5. **Friction:** opposes relative sliding or tendency to slide. It helps us walk and also causes wear; oil can reduce friction in machinery.
6. **Mass and weight:** mass is measured in kg; weight is gravitational force in newtons. An object's weight changes on the Moon; its mass essentially does not.
7. **Pressure = force/area:** smaller area gives more pressure for the same force. A sharp pin pierces more easily than a blunt one.
8. **Liquid pressure:** increases with depth in the same liquid. Dam walls are commonly thicker near the base.
9. **Buoyancy:** an immersed object experiences an upward force. A ship floats when this balances its weight; its shape gives it a low enough overall average density.
10. **Work and power:** work involves force and displacement in its direction. Power is energy transferred per unit time, measured in watts.
11. **Potential / kinetic energy:** a raised object can have gravitational potential energy; a moving object has kinetic energy. Real systems also dissipate energy as heat and sound.
12. **Simple machines:** a lever, pulley or inclined plane can change the magnitude/direction of force. They do not create free energy.

## Heat, light and sound

13. **Conduction:** heat flows through a material, as through a spoon in hot soup. **Convection:** moving fluid transfers heat. **Radiation:** electromagnetic waves carry energy even through vacuum.
14. **Evaporation and cooling:** higher-energy molecules escape a liquid surface, cooling the remainder. Sweat cools the skin when it evaporates.
15. **Temperature vs heat:** temperature indicates how hot or cold something is; heat is energy transferred because of a temperature difference.
16. **Reflection:** angle of incidence equals angle of reflection, measured from the normal. A mirror can form a clear image because its surface is smooth.
17. **Refraction:** light changes speed and can change direction at a boundary. A straw can appear bent in water.
18. **Lenses:** a convex lens converges parallel light rays in air; a concave lens diverges them. A magnifying glass is normally a convex lens.
19. **White light and prism:** different colours refract by different amounts. A rainbow also involves internal reflection and refraction in droplets.
20. **Sound:** vibrations create sound waves in a medium; sound cannot travel in a vacuum. Higher frequency means higher pitch; greater amplitude generally means louder sound.
21. **Echo:** reflected sound returning after a delay. If sound travels at 340 m/s and returns in 2 s, total path is 680 m; wall distance is **340 m**, half the round trip.

## Electricity and chemistry

22. **Current, voltage, resistance:** amperes measure current, volts measure potential difference, ohms measure resistance. Voltage is energy transferred per unit charge.
23. **Ohm's law:** V = IR for an ohmic conductor at constant physical conditions. 6 V across 3 Ω → 2 A.
24. **Power:** P = VI. A 6 V device taking 2 A uses 12 W. A 100 W device used for 3 h consumes 300 Wh = 0.3 kWh.
25. **Series:** one path for current in a simple circuit. **Parallel:** multiple paths; a bulb on one branch may work when another branch is open.
26. **Safety:** fuses help protect circuits from excessive current. Never bypass a fuse, handle mains wiring or short a battery. Physical circuit experiments require teacher supervision and safe low-voltage kits.
27. **Element / compound / mixture:** element = one type of atom; compound = elements chemically combined in fixed proportion; mixture = variable proportions. Air is a mixture, water a compound.
28. **Physical / chemical change:** melting ice is physical; rusting produces new substances. A candle demonstrates melting wax and burning wax.
29. **Acids and bases:** blue litmus turns red in acid, red litmus turns blue in base. Do not taste unknown chemicals.
30. **Separation:** filtration removes insoluble sand from water, not dissolved salt. Evaporation can recover salt from saltwater.
31. **Rust:** iron exposed to oxygen and moisture can rust; paint reduces exposure. A protective coating's effectiveness depends on its condition.
32. **Fire:** fuel, oxygen and sufficient heat support many fires. Never use water on an electrical fire; alert an adult and follow emergency guidance.
