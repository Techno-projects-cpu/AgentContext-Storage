## Electricity from zero: voltage, current, watts

- Think of **charge** as a property of matter. Electric current is a **rate of charge flow**, not a store of energy.
- **Current I:** charge per time, I = Q/t. Unit **ampere (A)** = coulomb per second.
- **Potential difference / voltage V:** energy transferred per unit charge. Unit **volt (V)** = joule per coulomb.
- **Resistance R:** how a component impedes current under given conditions. Unit **ohm (Ω)**.
- **Ohm's law:** V = IR for an ohmic conductor at suitable constant physical conditions; not every component obeys a constant R at all voltages.
- Example: 12 V across 6 Ω → I = 12/6 = **2 A**.
- **Power P:** rate of energy transfer, measured in **watts (W)**. For DC, P = VI.
- Example: 12 V × 2 A = **24 W**. For 10 s, energy = 24 × 10 = **240 J**.
- **Energy:** P × time. A 100 W lamp for 5 h uses 500 Wh = **0.5 kWh**. A kWh is energy, not power.
- **Series:** one path; same steady current through components. Resistances in series add.
- **Parallel:** branches across the same two nodes share voltage; branch currents add at junctions.
- Identical bulbs in series are normally dimmer than one bulb on the same ideal source; brightness depends on actual circuit and bulb properties.
- **Ammeter:** connects in series to measure current. **Voltmeter:** connects in parallel across the component.
- **Fuse:** excessive current heats the fuse element until it melts; element design and heat loss matter. A lower resistance does not by itself mean greater total heating at the same current.
- **LED vs filament:** an LED can need less power for comparable brightness because it generally produces visible light more efficiently.
- Household electricity can kill: **never test circuits using mains power**. [Full beginner guide](../12-electricity-from-zero.md).

## Motion, force and fluids

- **Distance** is total path length; **displacement** describes change in position with direction.
- Walk 30 m east then 40 m west: distance **70 m**, displacement **10 m west**.
- **Speed = distance/time.** Average speed = total distance / total time—not always the simple average of two speeds.
- Equal distances at 60 km/h and 30 km/h: 60 km each takes 1 h + 2 h; average = 120/3 = **40 km/h**.
- **Velocity** includes direction; an object moving in a circle can accelerate even at constant speed because direction changes.
- **Newton's first law / inertia:** without net external force velocity remains constant; a bus passenger tends to continue moving when brakes slow the bus.
- **Force:** F = ma for constant mass in a simple inertial-frame model; force is measured in newtons (N).
- **Momentum:** p = mv. A rebound reverses direction, so change in momentum must use signed velocities.
- Example: 0.1 kg at +4 m/s rebounds at −6 m/s → Δp = 0.1(−6 − 4) = **−1 kg·m/s**, magnitude 1.
- **Average force:** Δp/Δt. A 1 kg·m/s momentum change in 0.02 s → **50 N** average magnitude.
- **Action and reaction:** forces act on different bodies; a balloon pushes air backward and air pushes balloon forward.
- **Pressure:** force / area. A sharp edge produces more pressure for the same force.
- **Density:** mass / volume. A 150 g object of volume 50 cm³ has density **3 g/cm³**.
- **Buoyant force:** an upward force due to fluid pressure; its magnitude equals the weight of displaced fluid in the usual Archimedes principle.
- A floating object at rest displaces fluid with weight equal to its own weight. The average density of a floating ship including empty spaces can be lower than water.
- **Gravity:** in a simplified spherical model, F = GMm/r². Fixed planet mass and double radius → surface weight becomes one-quarter.
- **Mass vs weight:** mass is measured in kg; gravitational weight is a force. In free fall apparent weight can be zero while gravity still acts.
- **Friction:** static friction can prevent slipping; rolling wheels often reduce resistance compared with sliding, depending on conditions.

## Heat, sound and light

- **Temperature** measures a thermal state; **heat** is energy transferred because of temperature difference.
- A steel spoon can feel colder than wood at the same temperature because metal generally transfers heat from skin faster.
- Higher pressure raises water's boiling point; this can help a pressure cooker cook food faster. Do not open a pressurised cooker.
- Water vapour on a cold lid **condenses** into liquid droplets. Evaporation is liquid → gas; freezing is liquid → solid.
- Sound is a mechanical wave and needs a medium. A bell cannot transmit sound through a perfect vacuum.
- Echo distance = speed × total return time / 2. At 340 m/s, an echo after 0.4 s implies a wall **68 m** away.
- **Frequency** is cycles per second (Hz); wavelength × frequency = wave speed.
- **Reflection:** light changes direction at a surface. **Refraction:** direction/speed changes at a boundary when it enters at an angle.
- White light splits into colours through a prism due to **dispersion**; different wavelengths generally refract by different amounts.
- A **pinhole camera** gives an inverted image; two holes can form two images that may overlap.
- A convex lens can form a real image when a real object is beyond its focal length; a concave mirror can also form a real image under suitable conditions.
- **Solar eclipse:** do not look directly at the Sun; obtain certified solar-viewing protection and expert guidance.

## Matter and chemistry

- **Matter** has mass and occupies space. Air is matter; light and sound are not matter in the usual school-level classification.
- **Atom:** protons and neutrons in the nucleus; electrons around it. Atomic number = number of protons.
- **Isotopes:** same protons, different neutrons. **Isobars:** same mass number, different proton number.
- An atom that gains an electron becomes a negatively charged **ion**; protons do not change in ordinary ion formation.
- **Element:** one type of atom. **Compound:** chemically combined elements in a definite ratio. **Mixture:** components physically combined.
- **Physical change:** melting or dissolving; **chemical change:** new substances form, as with rusting.
- **Rusting** involves iron, oxygen and moisture; paint can slow it by limiting contact with air and water.
- **Acids:** lemon contains citric acid; curd contains lactic acid; vinegar contains acetic acid; apple contains malic acid.
- **pH** decreases as hydrogen-ion activity increases. At 25°C neutral pure water has pH 7.
- An acid and a base can neutralise one another; the final pH depends on amounts and strengths.
- A **catalyst** offers an alternative reaction pathway and is regenerated overall; it does not create atoms or automatically shift equilibrium.
- **Separation:** filter sand from salt solution; crystallisation can recover salt; distillation can collect condensed water, but suitability depends on contaminants.
- **Graphite** conducts electricity. Salt solution conducts because its ions move; pure distilled water conducts poorly.
- **Baking soda:** sodium bicarbonate. **Common salt:** sodium chloride. **Magnesium hydroxide:** used as an antacid. **Bleaching powder:** used in disinfection when appropriately handled.
- Many **esters** have fruity smells, but not every fragrance is an ester.
- Sodium can react vigorously with cold water; white phosphorus and dichromate are hazardous. **Never reproduce these demonstrations.**

## Catch the trap

- “Volts used per hour” and “watts used up” are imprecise: ask whether the problem is about voltage, power or energy.
- Doubling voltage at fixed resistance makes current ×2 and power ×4, not ×2.
- A material being clear does not prove it is free of microbes or dissolved pollutants.
- Do not infer a unique metal reactivity order from final pH unless initial acid, amounts, surface area and other conditions are controlled.
