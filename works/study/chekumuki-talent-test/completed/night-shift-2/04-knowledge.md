# More knowledge: connected revision ⚡

## Physics from everyday life

1. **Bus brakes and inertia:** When a bus stops suddenly, passengers tend to keep moving forward. A seat belt helps restrain them. Inertia is resistance to changes in motion.
2. **Action and reaction:** When you push the ground backward while walking, the ground pushes you forward. The forces act on different objects.
3. **Work:** In physics, work involves force and displacement in the direction of that force. Holding a heavy bag motionless can be tiring, but its mechanical displacement is zero.
4. **Power:** Rate of doing work or transferring energy; watt (W). A 100 W appliance used for 2 hours uses 200 Wh = 0.2 kWh.
5. **Floating:** An object floats when upward buoyant force balances its weight. Shape and average density matter: a steel ship can float despite steel being denser than water.
6. **Pressure in water:** In the same liquid, pressure increases with depth. This is why a dam is usually thicker near the bottom.
7. **Echo:** reflected sound heard separately after a delay. Soft curtains absorb some sound; hard surfaces often reflect more.
8. **Lightning:** We see lightning before hearing thunder because light travels much faster than sound.
9. **Mirror:** angle of incidence equals angle of reflection; both measured from the normal (perpendicular to the surface).
10. **Blue sky:** shorter visible wavelengths are scattered more strongly by air molecules. Sunsets often look red because sunlight travels a longer atmospheric path.
11. **Magnet:** like poles repel, unlike poles attract. Not all metals are magnetic; copper is not attracted like iron.
12. **Circuit safety:** dry hands and safe insulation matter, but never handle household live wiring. A battery kit should be used only under teacher guidance.

## Biology connections

13. **Breathing vs respiration:** breathing moves air into and out of lungs; cellular respiration releases energy from food in cells. They are linked but not identical.
14. **Pulse:** rhythmic expansion of arteries caused by the heartbeat. Exercise often raises pulse because muscles need more oxygen and nutrients.
15. **Oxygen exception:** arteries carry blood away from the heart; the pulmonary artery carries oxygen-poor blood to the lungs.
16. **Plant respiration:** plants respire day and night. Photosynthesis requires light, so do not say plants “only respire at night.”
17. **Germination:** many seeds need water, oxygen and suitable temperature. Different seeds have different light requirements.
18. **Pollination vs fertilisation:** pollination is transfer of pollen to a stigma; fertilisation is fusion of reproductive cells later.
19. **Infectious disease:** pathogens include viruses, bacteria, fungi and parasites. Not every microbe causes disease; many are useful.
20. **Vaccination and hygiene:** vaccines help protect against specific diseases; handwashing can reduce spread of many infections. Neither replaces medical advice.
21. **Food web:** multiple linked food chains show that organisms can have more than one food source or predator.
22. **Adaptation:** a cactus has features such as reduced leaves/spines that help reduce water loss; an individual does not evolve a new adaptation just because it wants one.
23. **Biomagnification:** some persistent pollutants become more concentrated at higher food-chain levels. This differs from energy, which decreases at successive levels.
24. **Conservation:** reducing habitat loss helps biodiversity; planting a single species everywhere is not the same as restoring a diverse ecosystem.

## Chemistry and Earth

25. **Acids and bases:** lemon juice is acidic; soap solution is generally basic. Use indicators only under teacher guidance—never taste laboratory chemicals.
26. **Indicators:** litmus changes colour in acids and bases. Blue litmus turns red in acid; red litmus turns blue in base.
27. **Separation:** filtration can separate insoluble sand from water; evaporation can recover dissolved salt from saltwater. Filtering alone does not remove dissolved salt.
28. **Dissolving:** stirring generally speeds up dissolving by bringing fresh solvent to the solid; it does not automatically change equilibrium solubility.
29. **Combustion:** burning commonly requires fuel, oxygen and sufficient heat. Removing one helps stop a fire, but use the appropriate extinguisher and adult help.
30. **Climate vs weather:** weather describes short-term conditions; climate describes long-term patterns.
31. **Eclipse vs moon phase:** Earth's shadow causes a lunar eclipse when alignment is right, not ordinary monthly Moon phases.
32. **Drinking water:** clear water is not necessarily safe. Filtration can remove particles; boiling can kill many germs but does not remove all chemical contamination.

## Easy maths worked out

33. **Discount:** ₹200 item with 10% discount: 10% of 200 = ₹20; sale price = ₹180.
34. **Percentage of marks:** 34 out of 40 = (34 ÷ 40) × 100 = **85%**. Do not confuse a practice calculation with an official new score.
35. **Unit conversion:** 2.5 km = 2500 m; 750 mL = 0.75 L; 90 minutes = 1.5 hours.
36. **Average speed:** travel 60 km in 2 h and 30 km in 1 h: total 90 km / 3 h = 30 km/h. Use total distance and total time.
37. **Fraction:** 3/4 of 80 = 60. Divide 80 by 4, then multiply by 3.
38. **Ratio:** 3:2 in a group of 25 means 5 parts, each 5; groups are 15 and 10.
39. **Pattern:** 5, 10, 15, 20 → 25: add 5. State the assumed rule because more than one rule can fit a short sequence.
40. **Fair test:** comparing seeds in sunlight and darkness: same species, water, soil and temperature as far as possible; change light, measure growth, repeat. Some conditions cannot always be perfectly controlled.
