# New maths and reasoning: small data, strong conclusions 📊

## Three different “middle” ideas

1. **Mean (సగటు):** sum / count. For 2, 3, 3, 4, 18, mean = 30/5 = 6.
2. **Median (మధ్యగతం):** put values in order and choose the middle. For 2, 3, 3, 4, 18, median = 3. The unusually large 18 pulls the mean upward.
3. **Even number of values:** for 1, 3, 5, 9 the middle two are 3 and 5; median = (3 + 5)/2 = 4.
4. **Mode (బాహుళకం):** most frequent value. In 2, 3, 3, 4, 18, mode = 3. A dataset can have more than one mode or no unique mode.
5. **Range:** largest minus smallest. In 2, 3, 3, 4, 18, range = 18 − 2 = 16.

## Unitary method and rates

6. **Unitary method:** first find the value for one unit. If 4 notebooks cost ₹60 at the same price each, one costs ₹15; 7 cost ₹105.
7. **Rate:** if 3 taps together fill a tank in 2 hours at a fixed combined rate, their combined rate is 1/2 tank per hour. Do not assume identical taps or constant flow unless stated.
8. **Distance–time table:** 0 h → 0 km, 1 h → 30 km, 2 h → 60 km. The average speed between 1 h and 2 h is (60−30)/(2−1) = 30 km/h.
9. **Percent vs percentage points:** a pass rate rising from 40% to 50% increases by **10 percentage points**; relative increase is 10/40 = **25%**.
10. **Simple interest:** principal ₹1,000 at 5% per year for 2 years gives SI = 1000 × 5 × 2 / 100 = ₹100. It assumes simple interest and the stated annual rate; it is not compound interest.
11. **Combined groups:** 10 students average 60 and another 20 average 75. Combined average = (10×60 + 20×75)/30 = 70, not the unweighted 67.5.

## Tables and evidence

12. **Read the denominator:** 8 correct out of 10 = 80%; 16 out of 20 = 80%. More correct answers does not automatically mean a higher percentage.
13. **Sampling:** testing 3 leaves from one plant does not prove every plant behaves that way. More samples and varied conditions improve confidence, but cannot guarantee certainty.
14. **Control group:** in a seed test, compare with and without fertiliser while keeping seed type, light, water and other relevant conditions similar. Don't claim results before measurement.
15. **Measurement uncertainty:** a stopwatch can be limited by human reaction time. Repeating trials and reporting a mean helps but does not eliminate systematic errors.
16. **Misleading scale:** a bar chart starting at 90 instead of zero can make 95 and 100 look dramatically different. Read the numbers and units before interpreting.
17. **Conditional reasoning:** “If it rains, the road gets wet” does not prove “if the road is wet, it rained”; a hose could also wet it.
18. **Counterexample:** one valid counterexample disproves “all metals are attracted to a magnet”. Copper is a counterexample to attraction like iron.
19. **Pattern caution:** 2, 4, 6, 8 suggests adding 2, so 10 is a natural next answer; a finite list can fit other rules too. Say the rule.
20. **Probability:** a bag has 3 red and 2 blue balls; one randomly chosen ball has probability 3/5 of red if every ball is equally likely. Removing a ball changes later probabilities if not replaced.

## Quick worked check

Data 4, 4, 5, 7, 10 → mean 6; median 5; mode 4; range 6. A temperature increasing 20°C to 25°C is a 5°C rise; avoid converting Celsius temperature changes into a percent of Celsius as though its zero were absolute.
