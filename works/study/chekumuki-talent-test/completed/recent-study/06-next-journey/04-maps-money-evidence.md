# Maps, everyday money and evidence 🗺️

## Maps and coordinates

1. **Latitude (అక్షాంశం)** describes angular position north or south of the Equator. The Equator is 0° latitude; the poles are 90° north and south.
2. **Longitude (రేఖాంశం)** describes angular position east or west of the Prime Meridian, defined as 0° longitude. Longitude lines meet at the poles; latitude and longitude together can identify a location.
3. Read **latitude first, longitude second** when coordinates are presented in that order. Include N/S and E/W; 10° N is not 10° S.
4. Earth rotates roughly 360° in 24 hours, so 15° corresponds to roughly one hour of solar-time difference. Actual civil time zones follow legal boundaries and may not match local solar time exactly.
5. India's standard time uses the 82°30′ E reference meridian and is **UTC+5:30**. India uses one official standard time; do not infer a different civil time for every longitude.
6. **Scale:** on a map where 1 cm represents 5 km, 4 cm represents 20 km along the measured straight line. A road may be longer because it bends.
7. **Contour lines** join points of equal elevation. Closely spaced contours generally indicate a steeper slope than widely spaced contours on the same map; check the map's contour interval.
8. A **grid reference** identifies a location using a grid; read its labels and the specific map instructions. Compass north on a map is not necessarily the direction a person is facing.

## Personal finance and consumer sense

9. **Budget (బడ్జెట్):** a plan for income and spending over a period. If ₹500 is available and planned expenses are ₹320, ₹180 remains before any unplanned costs.
10. **Unit price:** compare the same quantity. A 500 g pack for ₹60 costs ₹120 per kg; a 1 kg pack for ₹110 has the lower listed price per kg. Check quality, expiry and whether you actually need it too.
11. A discount is calculated from its stated base. ₹200 with 10% off costs ₹180 before any additional charges. Two successive 10% discounts yield ₹162, not ₹160: the second 10% is applied to ₹180.
12. **Simple interest** is calculated on original principal under the stated terms; compound interest can add earlier interest to the base. A low advertised rate is not enough to judge a loan: check fees, repayment schedule and conditions with a trusted adult.
13. A bill or receipt is evidence of a purchase. Check product, quantity, price, total and applicable terms; keep it when a return or complaint might be needed.
14. **MRP** is the maximum retail price printed for the relevant packaged commodity in India; applicable rules have exceptions and details. Don't assume every service or every unlabelled product follows the same rule.
15. “Only today!”, a celebrity photo and a screenshot of a prize do not establish an offer is genuine. Check the official organiser, written terms, eligibility and whether any money or sensitive information is requested.

## Thinking about evidence

16. **Correlation is not necessarily causation:** ice-cream sales and cold-drink sales may both rise in hot weather; one does not necessarily cause the other.
17. **Fair comparison:** if one crop gets fertiliser and also twice as much water, the difference cannot confidently be attributed to fertiliser alone. Change one variable where possible and measure multiple samples.
18. **Absolute vs relative risk:** if a result rises from 2 in 100 to 3 in 100, the absolute increase is 1 percentage point; relative to 2%, it is a 50% increase. Neither tells the whole story alone.
19. **Headline vs study:** a headline may simplify. Check sample size, comparison group, dates, uncertainty and whether the underlying source supports the claim.
20. **Prediction vs observation:** “the plant will grow 5 cm” is a prediction; “it grew 4 cm in seven days” is an observation if actually measured. Record results even when they contradict your guess.
