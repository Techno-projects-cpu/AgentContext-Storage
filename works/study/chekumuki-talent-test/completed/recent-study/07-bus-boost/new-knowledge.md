## 1. Cells, inheritance and variation

1. **Chromosome (వర్ణసూత్రం):** a structure containing DNA and proteins. A gene is a segment of DNA that can contribute to a functional product; traits are not always determined by one gene alone.
2. **Alleles:** different versions of a gene. **Genotype** refers to genetic makeup at a specified gene or genes; **phenotype** is an observable characteristic, influenced by genes and often the environment.
3. A **dominant** allele can affect a trait when one copy is present; a recessive trait in a simple example generally appears when two recessive copies are present. Dominant does not mean common, stronger or better.
4. In a simplified cross `Tt × Tt`, where T is completely dominant, possible combinations are TT, Tt, Tt and tt: 3:1 phenotype ratio is an expectation, not a guarantee for every four offspring. Real inheritance can be more complex.
5. **Mitosis (సమవిభజన):** cell division that generally produces two genetically similar daughter cells; important in growth and repair. DNA is copied before the cell divides.
6. **Meiosis (క్షయకరణ విభజన):** specialised cell division that reduces chromosome number and contributes to genetic variation in sexual reproduction. In humans it produces cells with 23 chromosomes, rather than the usual 46 in body cells.
7. Fertilisation joins gametes to form a **zygote**; offspring inherit genetic material from both parents. Genetic variation also arises through recombination and mutation.
8. **Mutation:** a change in DNA sequence. Effects can be harmful, neutral or occasionally beneficial depending on context; mutation does not always cause disease.
9. **Stem cell:** a cell capable of self-renewal and producing one or more specialised cell types. Different stem cells have different capacities; do not treat advertising claims as medical evidence.
10. **Homeostasis:** regulation of internal conditions within ranges compatible with life; it does not mean every body measurement remains perfectly constant.

## 2. Sound, heat and electronics

11. **Frequency (ఆవృత్తి):** cycles per second, measured in hertz (Hz). A higher frequency generally corresponds to higher pitch for a simple tone.
12. **Amplitude:** size of a vibration or wave disturbance. Greater amplitude generally corresponds to a louder sound under otherwise comparable conditions, but loudness also depends on frequency and the listener.
13. **Resonance:** a system can vibrate strongly when driven near a natural frequency. A swing pushed with suitable timing is an everyday example; the size of the response also depends on damping.
14. **Echo:** reflected sound heard separately when the delay is sufficient. Sound needs a material medium; it cannot travel through a perfect vacuum.
15. **Latent heat:** energy transferred during a change of state without a temperature change during that phase change under the specified conditions. Melting ice can absorb energy while remaining near 0°C at normal atmospheric pressure, until it has melted.
16. **Conduction:** transfer through interactions within material; **convection:** energy transfer involving bulk fluid motion; **radiation:** energy transfer by electromagnetic waves, which can cross a vacuum. Several mechanisms may operate together.
17. **Semiconductor:** material whose electrical behaviour lies between that of a typical conductor and insulator in useful conditions. Silicon is widely used in electronic devices; its conductivity can be changed by controlled doping.
18. A **diode** generally conducts more readily in one direction than the other under suitable conditions; an LED emits light when operated correctly. A diode is not a perfect one-way valve in every circumstance.
19. A **transistor** can act as a switch or amplifier in a circuit. Modern integrated circuits contain many transistors; never experiment with household mains.
20. A **capacitor** stores electric charge and energy in an electric field. It is not the same as a battery; larger capacitors can retain hazardous charge even after power is disconnected.

## 3. Sky and time

21. **Parallax:** apparent shift in an object's position against a distant background when viewed from different positions. Hold a finger up and alternate eyes: the nearby finger appears to move relative to the background.
22. **Asteroid:** a generally rocky or metallic small body orbiting the Sun. **Comet:** a small body containing ices and dust; near the Sun it can develop a coma and tail. These are simplified categories.
23. **Meteoroid:** small object travelling through space. **Meteor:** streak of light when it passes through an atmosphere. **Meteorite:** material that survives to reach the ground.
24. The Moon's phases reflect changing viewing geometry of the sunlit part of the Moon; Earth's shadow causes a **lunar eclipse**, not the ordinary monthly phases.
25. A year divisible by 4 is generally a **leap year**; century years must also be divisible by 400. Thus 2000 was a leap year; 1900 and 2100 are not.
26. **Rotation** is turning around an axis; **revolution** is motion around another body. Earth's rotation produces the day–night cycle; seasons mainly arise from its axial tilt and orbit, not simply a changing distance from the Sun.

## 4. Public institutions and good citizenship

27. The **Constitution** is the framework for India's government and rights. The **Preamble** sets out guiding ideals including justice, liberty, equality and fraternity.
28. India's Parliament consists of the **President, Lok Sabha and Rajya Sabha**. Lok Sabha is the House of the People; Rajya Sabha is the Council of States. Their powers differ in some matters.
29. The **Gram Sabha** comprises registered voters in a village-level area; an elected **Gram Panchayat** is a local governing institution. Exact arrangements and functions are set by applicable law.
30. An **election** selects representatives; a secret ballot helps voters choose privately. Eligibility, dates and procedures depend on the relevant election and current law.
31. A **law**, a proposed bill and a court decision are not interchangeable. A bill is a proposal; whether and when it becomes law depends on the applicable constitutional process.
32. Emergency warnings and election details change: consult relevant official authorities and local teachers. These notes are not legal advice or a prediction of quiz questions.

## 5. Mini exercises

- Finger shifts against the far wall when switching eyes → **parallax**.
- Object from space reaches ground → **meteorite**; the bright atmospheric streak was a **meteor**.
- Vibration rate doubles → frequency doubles; pitch generally rises. Loudness cannot be determined from frequency alone.
- Four children from `Tt × Tt` need not include exactly three dominant phenotypes: probability is not a quota.
