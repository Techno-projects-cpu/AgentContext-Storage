## 1. Pressure, fluids and waves

1. **Buoyancy (ఉత్ప్లావక బలం)** is the upward force a fluid exerts on an immersed object. Archimedes' principle states that its magnitude equals the weight of displaced fluid. An object floats in equilibrium when its weight and buoyant force balance.
2. An object can float in water even if made from dense material: a hollow steel ship displaces enough water for its total weight. The average density of the entire ship, including its enclosed air, is relevant.
3. **Pascal's principle:** a pressure change applied to a confined fluid is transmitted throughout it. Hydraulic systems can multiply force through different piston areas, though energy conservation and practical losses still apply.
4. Example: in an ideal hydraulic system a 10 N force on a 1 cm² piston produces an additional pressure of 10 N/cm². At a 5 cm² piston, the corresponding force is 50 N. The larger piston moves a shorter distance for equal fluid volume, ignoring losses.
5. **Surface tension** makes a liquid surface behave somewhat like a stretched film due to molecular interactions. It can support a small water strider; not every insect can stand on water.
6. **Capillary action:** liquid can rise or fall in a narrow tube due to interactions among liquid molecules and the tube, along with surface tension. It contributes to water movement in porous materials; plant water transport involves additional mechanisms.
7. **Electromagnetic spectrum:** radio, microwave, infrared, visible, ultraviolet, X-ray and gamma rays are all electromagnetic radiation. In a vacuum they travel at the same speed, but differ in frequency and wavelength.
8. For electromagnetic waves in vacuum, speed = frequency × wavelength. Higher frequency means shorter wavelength. Not all ultraviolet radiation behaves the same, and UV exposure can damage skin and eyes.
9. **Ionising radiation** has sufficient energy per photon to remove electrons from atoms in suitable interactions. X-rays and gamma rays are examples; whether UV is ionising depends on photon energy. Radiation exposure is not automatically contamination.
10. **Radioactive half-life:** time for half the radioactive nuclei in a large sample, on average, to decay. After two half-lives approximately one quarter remains. It is not the time for all the material to disappear.

## 2. Reactions and biology in context

11. **Catalyst (ఉత్ప్రేరకం):** speeds up a reaction by providing an alternative pathway with lower activation energy. It is not consumed overall, though it can change during intermediate steps or become deactivated.
12. **Enzymes** are biological catalysts; most are proteins. Enzyme activity depends on factors including temperature and pH. Heating an enzyme too much can change its shape and reduce activity.
13. **Exothermic:** transfers energy to surroundings as heat; **endothermic:** absorbs energy as heat from surroundings. Whether a reaction feels hot or cold can be influenced by experimental conditions.
14. A reaction releasing energy can still need **activation energy** to start: a match can start a fuel burning, but the burning fuel subsequently releases energy.
15. **Scientific classification** groups organisms by shared features and evolutionary relationships. It is revised as new evidence appears.
16. **Binomial nomenclature:** a species name has genus and specific epithet, conventionally italicised, e.g. *Homo sapiens*. Genus is capitalised, specific epithet lowercase.
17. **Herbarium:** a reference collection of preserved plant specimens, commonly dried and labelled. A botanical garden maintains living plants; both can support research and education.
18. **Seed bank:** stores seeds under controlled conditions to conserve plant genetic diversity. Not every species' seeds tolerate conventional long-term drying/freezing; conservation requires different methods for some.
19. **Hydroponics:** growing plants with nutrients supplied in water, without soil. Roots still need appropriate nutrients, water and oxygen, and systems vary.
20. **Endemic species:** native and restricted to a particular region. This is different from an endemic disease, which is consistently present at an expected level in a region or population.
21. **Keystone species:** a species whose effect on an ecosystem is disproportionately large relative to its abundance. Removing one can cause substantial changes, depending on the ecosystem.
22. **Bioaccumulation** is build-up of a substance in an organism over time; **biomagnification** is increasing concentration at successive trophic levels for certain persistent substances. Not all pollutants biomagnify.

## 3. Earth, institutions and interpretation

23. A **monsoon** is a seasonal wind pattern associated with seasonal rainfall changes. It is not simply any heavy shower. Local rainfall varies greatly.
24. **El Niño** is an episodic warming of the central/eastern tropical Pacific associated with broader atmospheric changes. It can influence Indian monsoon patterns, but it does not determine every season by itself.
25. The **International Date Line** generally follows 180° longitude with deviations. Crossing it changes the calendar date, while local civil-time rules and direction matter.
26. **Legislature, executive, judiciary:** broadly, they make laws, administer laws and interpret/apply laws respectively. Real constitutional functions can overlap and are subject to checks and balances.
27. India has a constitutional democracy with a written Constitution. Fundamental Rights and Fundamental Duties are distinct categories in the Constitution; specific rights, duties and limits must be read in their legal context.
28. A chronology orders events by time. The date a historical object was made is not necessarily the date its later archaeological layer was formed.
29. A population average cannot tell us every individual's result; check how the data were collected, the denominator and whether groups are comparable.
30. A controlled experiment changes a variable and compares outcomes while managing other relevant variables. In living systems perfect control is hard; repeat measurements and report uncertainty.

## 4. Short worked examples

- **Half-life:** start 80 g of a radioactive isotope → about 40 g after one half-life → 20 g after two, assuming only decay changes its amount.
- **Hydraulics:** 10 N on 1 cm² → 10 N/cm² → 50 N on 5 cm² in the ideal case. A force gain is not free energy.
- **Scientific name:** *Mangifera indica*: genus *Mangifera*; specific epithet *indica*. The two words together identify the species.
- **Pollution:** a fish accumulating mercury over its life is bioaccumulation; higher concentrations across suitable food-chain levels illustrate biomagnification.
