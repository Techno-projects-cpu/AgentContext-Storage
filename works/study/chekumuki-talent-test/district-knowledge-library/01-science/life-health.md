# Life and health — connected cards 🌱

Read the bold term, cover its explanation, then ask: “How do I know?” Telugu words are quick bridges, not an extra syllabus.

## Cells and systems

1. **Cell (కణం):** basic unit of living organisms. Cell → tissue → organ → organ system → organism. Muscle cells form muscle tissue; the heart is an organ.
2. **Cell membrane:** controls movement of many substances into and out of a cell. A plant cell also has a cell wall that provides support.
3. **Nucleus (కేంద్రకం):** contains most DNA in many cells. Mature human red blood cells have no nucleus; don't say every cell has one.
4. **Mitochondria:** help release usable energy from food in cellular respiration. Plant cells have them as well as animal cells.
5. **Chloroplasts and chlorophyll:** chloroplasts in green plant parts capture light energy for photosynthesis; chlorophyll is a pigment, not the whole organelle.
6. **DNA and genes:** DNA carries genetic information; genes are segments with roles in making functional products. Traits usually depend on genes and environment, not always one gene.
7. **Diffusion:** particles spread from higher to lower concentration because of random motion. Oxygen diffuses across thin alveolar walls.
8. **Osmosis:** net movement of water through a selectively permeable membrane towards the side with higher solute concentration, under suitable conditions. Water uptake in roots involves membrane processes.

## Body systems

9. **Digestion:** food breaks into simpler molecules. Most absorption of digested nutrients occurs in the small intestine, not the stomach.
10. **Lungs:** alveoli are tiny air sacs with thin walls and a large area for exchange of oxygen and carbon dioxide with blood.
11. **Breathing vs respiration:** breathing moves air in and out; cellular respiration releases usable energy from food. Plants also respire, including at night.
12. **Heart:** pumps blood. An artery carries blood away from the heart and a vein towards it; the pulmonary artery is an important oxygen-content exception.
13. **Blood:** haemoglobin in red blood cells carries oxygen; white blood cells help defend against infection; platelets help clot blood.
14. **Kidneys:** filter blood and regulate water and dissolved salts; urine contains wastes and excess water. Do not confuse kidney filtering with lung gas exchange.
15. **Nervous system:** brain and spinal cord coordinate responses; nerves carry signals. The knee-jerk response involves a spinal reflex arc and somatic motor neurons, not a simple autonomic reflex.
16. **Hormones:** chemical messengers carried in the body. Insulin helps regulate blood glucose. Quiz notes are not a substitute for medical diagnosis.
17. **Bones and muscles:** muscles pull bones across joints; they do not push them. Vitamin D supports calcium absorption; several nutrients matter for healthy growth.
18. **Vaccines vs antibiotics:** vaccines prepare the immune system against specific diseases. Antibiotics treat some bacterial infections, not viral flu; follow medical advice.
19. **Balanced diet:** different foods supply carbohydrate, protein, fat, vitamins, minerals, fibre and water. Protein helps growth and repair.
20. **Safe water:** clear-looking water is not necessarily germ-free. Boiling can kill many disease-causing organisms but does not reliably remove chemical contamination; follow local health advice.

## Plants and ecosystems

21. **Photosynthesis (కిరణజన్య సంయోగక్రియ):** plants use light, water and carbon dioxide to make sugars, releasing oxygen. Respiration and photosynthesis are different processes.
22. **Xylem / phloem:** xylem moves water and minerals mainly upward from roots; phloem transports sugars between source and use or storage regions.
23. **Stomata (పత్రరంధ్రాలు):** leaf pores controlled by guard cells. They allow gas exchange and water-vapour loss called transpiration.
24. **Pollination:** pollen reaches a stigma. Fertilisation is the later fusion of reproductive cells. A bee can transfer pollen while visiting flowers.
25. **Germination:** seeds typically require water, oxygen and a suitable temperature. Some species also need particular light conditions.
26. **Food chain:** producer → herbivore → carnivore. Arrows show direction of energy transfer, for example grass → grasshopper → frog.
27. **Food web:** several connected food chains; removing one organism can affect more than one route.
28. **Decomposers:** many fungi and bacteria break down dead material and return nutrients. Matter cycles, but energy flows and dissipates as heat.
29. **Adaptation:** cactus spines help reduce water loss; fish gills allow gas exchange in water. Whales are mammals that use lungs to breathe air.
30. **Biodiversity:** variety at genetic, species and ecosystem levels. Habitat conservation helps preserve it; a plantation of one species isn't the same as a diverse forest.

### Rapid recall

Why is a whale not a fish? **It is a mammal that breathes air.** Why is a pulmonary artery still an artery despite carrying oxygen-poor blood? **Arteries carry blood away from the heart.** Why do plants need mitochondria? **They respire to release usable energy.**
