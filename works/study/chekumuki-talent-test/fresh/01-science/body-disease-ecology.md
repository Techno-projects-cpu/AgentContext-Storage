# New angles: body signals, disease and ecological cycles 🧬

## Blood and hormones

1. **ABO blood groups:** red blood cells may have A antigen, B antigen, both or neither. The common groups are A, B, AB and O. These markers are not nutrients or measures of fitness.
2. **RhD:** positive or negative usually refers to whether the D antigen is present on red cells. ABO and RhD are separate ways to describe blood type.
3. **Transfusion caution:** matching is a medical task. Compatibility depends on the blood component, ABO/RhD and other factors; trained staff test and cross-match. “Universal donor” shortcuts do not replace hospital procedures. [WHO](https://www.who.int/teams/health-product-policy-and-standards/standards-and-specifications/blood-product-standardization/quality-and-safety/donation-testing).
4. **Endocrine gland:** produces hormones (రసాయన దూతలు) released into blood; hormones help coordinate activities in various parts of the body.
5. **Thyroid:** a gland in the neck that makes hormones involved in regulating metabolism. Iodine is needed to make thyroid hormones.
6. **Pancreas:** makes insulin, which helps regulate blood glucose. It also produces digestive substances; its functions are not limited to one job.
7. **Adrenal glands:** produce hormones including adrenaline, involved in the body's response to stress.
8. **Negative feedback:** when a system's response reduces the original change, it can help maintain stability. Body temperature regulation offers examples, but it does not mean a body never varies.

## Infection and public health

9. **Pathogen (వ్యాధికారక జీవి):** an organism or agent that can cause disease. Bacteria, viruses, fungi and parasites include pathogens, but many microbes are harmless or useful.
10. **Vector (వాహక జీవి):** an organism that can transmit a pathogen between hosts. Some mosquitoes transmit malaria parasites; the disease is caused by a *Plasmodium* parasite, not by every mosquito.
11. **Dengue:** a viral disease transmitted mainly by infected Aedes mosquitoes. Mosquitoes transmit it; the disease itself is not caused by the insect's bite alone without infection. Seek qualified care for illness.
12. **Malaria vs dengue:** malaria is caused by parasites; dengue is caused by a virus. Similar symptoms do not establish a diagnosis—testing and medical advice matter.
13. **Incubation period:** time between exposure to an infection and the appearance of symptoms. This differs from how long symptoms last; exact times vary by disease and person.
14. **Prevention:** clean hands, safe drinking water, vaccination where recommended and preventing mosquito breeding can reduce specific risks. No single measure prevents every disease.
15. **Endemic:** consistently present at an expected level in a particular population or region. **Outbreak:** more cases than expected in a place/time. Endemic does not mean harmless.

## Ecology: follow the atoms

16. **Nitrogen cycle:** atmospheric nitrogen gas is abundant, but most plants cannot directly use it. Certain bacteria convert nitrogen into usable compounds; decomposition and other bacteria also return nitrogen through the cycle.
17. **Nitrogen-fixing bacteria:** some live in root nodules of legumes. Both plant and bacteria benefit in the usual relationship: a useful example of mutualism.
18. **Nitrification:** certain microorganisms convert ammonium into nitrite and then nitrate. **Denitrification:** other microbes convert nitrogen compounds into nitrogen gases under suitable conditions.
19. **Carbon cycle:** carbon moves among atmosphere, living things, water and rocks. Photosynthesis takes in carbon dioxide; respiration and decomposition release carbon-containing substances.
20. **Biomagnification:** some persistent pollutants can become more concentrated at higher levels of a food chain. This differs from energy, which is dissipated along transfers.
21. **Invasive species:** introduced species that spread and cause harm can disrupt ecosystems. Not every non-native species is invasive.
22. **Mutualism, parasitism, commensalism:** both benefit; one benefits while the other is harmed; one benefits while the other is not significantly affected, respectively. Real relationships can vary with conditions.
