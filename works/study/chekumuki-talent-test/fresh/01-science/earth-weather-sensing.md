# Earth's signals: weather, earthquakes and maps 🌦️

## Instruments and weather

1. **Barometer:** measures atmospheric pressure (వాయు పీడనం). A pressure change helps forecasters, but one reading alone cannot guarantee tomorrow's weather.
2. **Hygrometer:** measures humidity (ఆర్ద్రత), a measure of water vapour in air. **Relative humidity** compares the actual vapour amount/pressure with saturation at that temperature.
3. **Anemometer:** measures wind speed. A **wind vane** indicates wind direction. They answer different questions.
4. **Rain gauge:** measures rainfall depth, often in millimetres. It describes rain at its location; one gauge need not represent an entire district.
5. **Dew point:** temperature to which air must cool for saturation under the given conditions. Dew can form when a surface cools enough for water vapour to condense on it.
6. **Fog and cloud:** both contain tiny water droplets or ice crystals. Fog is a cloud-like condition near the ground; invisible water vapour alone is not a visible cloud.
7. **Sea breeze:** during the day land can heat faster than the sea, producing a circulation with near-surface air moving from sea toward land. Real local weather is more complicated.
8. **Cyclone:** a rotating weather system around low pressure, which can bring strong winds and heavy rain. Its impacts depend on the storm and location; follow official warnings, not social-media rumours.

## Earthquakes and waves

9. **Focus / hypocentre:** point inside Earth where an earthquake starts. **Epicentre:** point on Earth's surface vertically above it; they are not the same location.
10. **Seismometer (భూకంపమాపి):** instrument sensing ground motion; a seismograph records it. Scientists compare records from several stations to locate earthquakes.
11. **Magnitude vs intensity:** magnitude describes the size of an earthquake at its source; intensity describes observed shaking/effects at a place. One earthquake has different intensities at different locations. [USGS explanation](https://pubs.usgs.gov/of//1984/0964/report.pdf).
12. **Tsunami:** a series of waves often caused by sudden displacement of a large volume of water, for example by an undersea earthquake. Not every earthquake produces a tsunami, and the first wave is not always the largest.
13. **Tsunami safety:** if a strong or long earthquake is felt on a coast, move inland and to higher ground when safe, and follow official alerts; do not go to the shore to watch. Local disaster guidance takes priority.
14. **Volcano:** an opening through which molten material and gases can reach the surface. Molten rock underground is called magma; once it reaches the surface it is called lava.
15. **Weather vs climate:** one day's rain is weather; long-term patterns describe climate. A single observation does not establish a long-term trend.

## Sensing from a distance

16. **Remote sensing:** collecting information about a place without touching it—for example, satellite images used to monitor clouds or crop conditions. A sensor's result must be interpreted and checked.
17. **GPS / satellite navigation:** receivers estimate position from timed signals from multiple satellites. A phone may also use other signals to improve location; satellite navigation is not the same as taking a satellite photograph.
18. **Resolution:** in an image, spatial resolution concerns the size of details a sensor can distinguish; a higher-resolution image can reveal smaller features, subject to image quality and conditions.
19. **Legend and scale on maps:** a legend explains symbols; map scale relates map distance to real distance. A blue line might denote a river only if the map legend or context supports it.
20. **Time-series comparison:** comparing satellite images from different dates can reveal changes, but season, cloud cover and sensor differences may also affect appearance. Check before claiming a forest was cleared.
