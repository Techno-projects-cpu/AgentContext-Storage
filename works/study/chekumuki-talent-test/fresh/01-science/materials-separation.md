# Materials and separation: what can we actually observe? ⚗️

These were selected because they were absent or lightly represented in our earlier text scan. Read each as **property → use → limitation**.

1. **Paper chromatography (క్రోమాటోగ్రఫీ):** a solvent moves through paper and carries mixture components at different rates. A washable black-ink dot may separate into colours. The solvent must begin below the ink mark; results vary by ink and solvent.
2. **Centrifugation (అపకేంద్రణం):** spinning can accelerate separation of suspended components with different settling behaviour. A laboratory centrifuge can separate blood components; trained staff and equipment are necessary. Do not handle blood at home.
3. **Sieving:** separates solid particles mainly by size, as when coarse flour particles remain in a sieve. It won't reliably separate two solids with similar particle sizes.
4. **Magnetic separation:** a magnet can separate iron pieces from sand, but cannot pull out all metals: copper and aluminium are not attracted like iron.
5. **Distillation (స్వేదనం):** liquid is boiled and its vapour condensed into a receiving container. Simple distillation can separate water from dissolved non-volatile salt; a classroom diagram is safer than heating a sealed apparatus.
6. **Evaporation vs distillation:** evaporation can leave dissolved salt behind; distillation is used when we want to collect condensed liquid too. Boiling can kill many germs but does not reliably remove dissolved chemical contaminants.
7. **Solution, suspension, colloid:** salt dissolved in water is a solution. Muddy water can be a suspension whose particles may settle. Milk is a colloid: dispersed tiny droplets do not behave like a simple dissolved salt solution.
8. **Tyndall effect:** a visible light beam can be scattered by particles in some colloids. A true solution generally does not show the same visible beam; the size and concentration of particles matter.
9. **Alloy (మిశ్రమ లోహం):** a material combining a metal with other element(s). Brass is mainly copper and zinc; bronze is mainly copper and tin. Exact properties and compositions vary.
10. **Stainless steel:** an iron-based alloy containing chromium; its thin protective oxide layer helps resist corrosion. “Stainless” does not mean it can never corrode.
11. **Galvanising:** coating iron or steel with zinc helps protect against rust. Zinc can also provide sacrificial protection at small scratches, but effectiveness depends on conditions.
12. **Hard water:** dissolved calcium or magnesium salts make soap lather less readily. Some hardness can form scale in kettles. Not every kind of hardness is removed by boiling.
13. **Polymer:** a large molecule built from repeating units. Many plastics are polymers; cellulose and natural rubber are naturally occurring polymers too.
14. **Thermoplastic vs thermoset:** many thermoplastics soften on heating and can be reshaped; thermoset materials, once cured, generally do not simply melt and reshape. Never burn or heat unknown plastics to identify them.
15. **Biodegradable:** can be broken down by biological processes under suitable conditions. “Biodegradable” does not guarantee rapid breakdown in every environment or permission to litter.
16. **Carbon monoxide:** a colourless, odourless poisonous gas that can form when carbon-containing fuel burns incompletely. It is not the same as carbon dioxide. Avoid indoor use of fuel-burning equipment without proper ventilation; seek emergency help for suspected poisoning.
17. **Electrolysis:** electricity can drive certain chemical changes. At school, use a diagram or teacher-approved demonstration only—no household mains or unknown solutions.
18. **Density:** mass divided by volume. Two objects of different mass can have the same density if made of the same uniform material under the same conditions.

## Think, don't guess

- A mixture contains **iron filings, sand and salt**. One possible plan: use a magnet for iron; add water to dissolve salt; filter out sand; evaporate water to recover salt. In real life the procedure needs safe handling and the result depends on the mixture.
- The dark ink mark separates into blue and yellow bands. The conclusion is that **the tested ink contains components that travelled differently in that solvent**—not that all black inks contain exactly those colours.
