# Land, water and food: follow the process 🌱

## Soil and landscapes

1. **Soil (నేల)** contains mineral particles, organic matter, air, water and organisms in varying amounts. It is not simply powdered rock.
2. **Sand, silt, clay:** soil particles are classified by size; sand is coarser and clay finer. Texture influences water retention and drainage, but organic matter and soil structure also matter.
3. **Humus** is dark organic material formed through decomposition. It can improve soil structure and water-holding ability; it is not identical to fresh leaves.
4. **Soil erosion (నేల కోత):** removal and movement of soil by water, wind or other agents. Loss of fertile topsoil can affect farming and waterways.
5. **Vegetation** and contour planting can reduce erosion in suitable circumstances by slowing runoff and holding soil; they do not stop every landslide.
6. **Weathering** breaks down rock in place; **erosion** transports material. A rock cracking in place illustrates weathering; a stream carrying the pieces illustrates erosion.
7. A **watershed / drainage basin (పరీవాహక ప్రాంతం)** is an area from which surface water drains toward a common outlet. A watershed boundary separates drainage areas; pollution upstream can affect downstream water.
8. **Groundwater** fills spaces and cracks below ground. An **aquifer** is a water-bearing geological formation that can yield usable quantities of water. A well draws groundwater; it is not necessarily an underground river.
9. **Recharge:** water moves into the ground and replenishes groundwater under suitable conditions. Recharge depends on rainfall, surface cover, geology and extraction; a recharge pit is not a guarantee of safe drinking water.

## Water after use

10. **Sewage (మురుగునీరు):** wastewater containing human waste and other materials. Untreated sewage can spread pathogens and harm aquatic ecosystems.
11. In a simplified treatment sequence, screening removes large objects; sedimentation lets heavier suspended solids settle; biological treatment uses microorganisms to break down organic matter; disinfection may reduce pathogens. Actual plant designs and order vary.
12. **Drinking-water treatment** has different goals from sewage treatment. Filtration or boiling alone does not reliably remove every dissolved chemical; use locally approved, tested safe water.
13. Excess nutrient runoff can contribute to **eutrophication**: algal growth may increase, and decomposition can deplete dissolved oxygen, harming aquatic life. Not every green pond has the same cause.
14. **Acid rain:** emissions including sulfur dioxide and nitrogen oxides can react in the atmosphere to form acids that fall with precipitation. It can affect soil, water and buildings; normal rain is already mildly acidic from dissolved carbon dioxide.
15. **Microplastics** are very small plastic pieces, generally defined as less than 5 mm. They can arise from larger plastics breaking down or from small manufactured particles. Their effects vary and are still being studied.

## Food, microbes and observation

16. **Fermentation (కిణ్వనం):** microbes or their enzymes change food materials. Yeast can produce carbon dioxide and alcohol from sugars under suitable conditions; the gas helps bread dough rise.
17. In making curd, suitable bacteria convert lactose into lactic acid; acid helps milk proteins form a gel. A different microbe and process can produce a different food.
18. **Pasteurization (పాశ్చరైజేషన్):** controlled heating followed by cooling reduces harmful microbes in foods such as milk. It is not the same as sterilization, which aims to eliminate all forms of microbial life under specified conditions.
19. Food can spoil even after pasteurization, so follow storage instructions. Swollen containers, unusual smell or appearance can warn of spoilage, but dangerous food is not always detectable by smell.
20. **Food chain vs food web:** a chain is one pathway of feeding; a web shows interconnected feeding relationships. Arrows usually represent the direction of energy transfer, from food to consumer.

### Compare carefully

- Soil forming slowly from rock is **weathering**; soil washing into a river is **erosion**.
- Wastewater treatment can reduce pollutants, but its output is not automatically safe to drink.
- Algae grow → algae die → decomposers use oxygen → aquatic animals may suffer. The steps depend on conditions.
