## 1. Waves and devices

1. **SONAR** (Sound Navigation and Ranging) sends sound through water and uses echoes to estimate the position or distance of objects. If sound speed is known, distance ≈ speed × round-trip time / 2. Water conditions affect speed.
2. **RADAR** uses radio waves, not sound, to detect objects. Reflected signals can reveal distance and other properties depending on the system.
3. **Ultrasound** is sound above 20,000 Hz, the approximate upper limit of typical human hearing. Medical imaging uses ultrasound, not X-rays. A trained professional should interpret images.
4. **Infrasound** is sound below 20 Hz, approximately the lower limit of typical human hearing. Individual hearing ranges differ.
5. **Doppler effect:** frequency observed changes when there is relative motion between source and observer. A moving siren can sound higher as it approaches and lower as it recedes, compared with the stationary case.
6. **Periscope:** commonly uses mirrors or prisms to redirect light so an observer can see over or around an obstruction. It is not a telescope simply because both can help observation.
7. **Electroscope:** an instrument used to detect electric charge. Simple leaf electroscopes can show that charge is present, but identifying its sign requires a suitable known reference and procedure.
8. **Electroplating** uses electric current in an electrolyte to deposit a metal coating on an object. The coating can improve appearance or corrosion resistance, depending on material and conditions. Use teacher-supervised demonstrations only.
9. **Thermos flask:** a vacuum layer greatly reduces conduction and convection; reflective surfaces reduce heat transfer by radiation. It slows heat transfer, not stops it forever.
10. **Solar cell** converts light energy directly into electrical energy by the photovoltaic effect. A solar water heater instead uses sunlight mainly for heating.

## 2. Changing matter and growing plants

11. **Sublimation:** direct change from solid to gas; the reverse is deposition. Some solids, such as iodine, can sublime. Do not heat or inhale chemicals at home.
12. **Emulsion:** a mixture of droplets of one liquid dispersed in another liquid with which it does not normally mix. Milk contains an emulsion of fat droplets in water; its full composition is more complex.
13. **Diffusion:** net movement of particles from a region of higher concentration to lower concentration due to random motion. No membrane is required.
14. **Osmosis:** net movement of water across a selectively permeable membrane from a region of higher water potential to lower water potential. Concentration descriptions are simplified approximations.
15. **Turgor pressure:** pressure of a plant cell's contents against its cell wall, supporting many non-woody plant parts. Water loss can reduce turgor and cause wilting.
16. **Transpiration:** loss of water vapour from plant aerial parts, especially through stomata. It can help cool leaves and support water movement, but rates depend on conditions.
17. **Grafting:** joining part of one plant (scion) to the rooted part of another (rootstock) so that they grow together. It is used for suitable compatible plants; not every pairing succeeds.
18. **Budding:** in horticulture, a bud from one plant is inserted into a suitable rootstock. Budding also describes a different asexual reproduction process in organisms such as yeast or Hydra: context matters.
19. **Phototropism:** directional growth response to light. A shoot often bends toward light; not all plant parts behave identically.
20. **Lichen:** a close association of fungi with photosynthetic partners such as algae or cyanobacteria. Some lichens are useful indicators of air quality because species vary in pollution sensitivity.
21. **Vermicomposting:** earthworms help convert organic matter into a compost-like material under suitable conditions. Avoid adding unsafe or unsuitable waste and follow local guidance.
22. **Biogas:** gas produced when microorganisms break down organic matter without oxygen; it contains methane along with other gases. Gas systems require proper design and safe handling.

## 3. Looking at the past

23. **Archaeology:** study of human past through material remains such as tools, buildings and pottery; evidence must be interpreted in context.
24. **Epigraphy:** study of inscriptions, including writing on stone, metal or other durable surfaces. **Numismatics:** study of coins, medals and related objects.
25. A **manuscript** is a handwritten document, often on paper, palm leaf or other material. A printed book and an inscription on a stone are different forms of evidence.
26. A **primary source** is evidence produced in the period or by people directly involved, such as a dated letter. It can still contain errors or bias; compare it with other evidence.
27. **Carbon-14 dating** can estimate the age of once-living material within its useful range; it does not directly date a metal coin or every ancient object.
28. A map or monument alone may not establish an event's date. Archaeologists compare layers, objects, inscriptions and other evidence; evidence can change an interpretation.

## 4. Telugu forms and Telangana

29. **ద్విపద:** a verse form consisting of paired lines or couplets. **పాల్కురికి సోమనాథుడు** is associated with *బసవ పురాణం* in ద్విపద form. School definitions can describe the form in more technical detail.
30. **ప్రబంధం:** a literary composition/form; its usage and conventions vary by period and tradition. *ఆముక్తమాల్యద* is associated with శ్రీకృష్ణదేవరాయలు.
31. **చాటువు / చాటుపద్యం:** a short occasional verse, often created for a particular person or situation; not every example is satire.
32. **బతుకమ్మ:** Telangana's floral festival, celebrated with flower arrangements, songs and community participation; local practices vary.
33. **సమ్మక్క–సారలమ్మ జాతర:** a major tribal festival at Medaram in Telangana; its schedule and arrangements should be checked with official sources before travel.

## 5. Quick applications

- Echo returns in 2 s; sound speed 1500 m/s in water → one-way distance ≈ **1500 m**, not 3000 m.
- A dry plant droops as cells lose water → reduced **turgor** is one possible explanation; disease and other stresses can also cause wilting.
- A copper coin is evidence for **numismatics**; the date of the coin is not automatically the date every surrounding object was made.
