*1️⃣ WAVES, SOUND & SMART DEVICES*

1. SONAR uses sound waves and echoes, commonly underwater; RADAR uses radio waves, not sound.
2. Echo travels to an object AND back. Distance = wave speed × round-trip time ÷ 2.
3. Sonar echo in 2 s at 1500 m/s → distance = 1500 × 2 ÷ 2 = 1500 m.
4. Ultrasound is sound above about 20,000 Hz; infrasound is below about 20 Hz. Hearing ranges vary by individual.
5. Medical ultrasound uses sound waves, not X-rays. Images require trained interpretation.
6. Doppler effect: relative motion changes observed frequency. A siren generally sounds higher approaching, lower receding.
7. Pitch mainly relates to frequency; loudness generally relates to amplitude and also depends on frequency and the listener.
8. A periscope uses mirrors/prisms to redirect light around an obstruction.
9. A plane mirror forms a virtual, upright image. Lateral inversion is an apparent left-right reversal.
10. A magnifying glass commonly uses a convex lens; the observed image depends on the object's position.
11. Electroscope detects charge; identifying charge sign requires an appropriate known reference and method.
12. Electroplating deposits a metal coating using electricity and an electrolyte. Do not experiment with mains electricity.
13. A thermos reduces heat transfer: vacuum limits conduction/convection; reflective surfaces reduce radiation.
14. A solar cell converts light directly to electrical energy; a solar water heater mainly heats water.
15. Conduction, convection and radiation may operate together; insulation slows transfer, not destroys energy.

*2️⃣ CHEMISTRY: SMALL CHANGES, BIG CLUES*

16. Sublimation = solid → gas directly; deposition = gas → solid directly.
17. Evaporation happens at a liquid surface at temperatures below boiling; boiling occurs throughout a liquid at its boiling conditions.
18. Condensation = vapour → liquid; freezing = liquid → solid; melting = solid → liquid.
19. Physical change need not create a new substance; chemical change forms new substances. Burning is chemical.
20. Mass is conserved in an ordinary chemical reaction in a closed system; escaping gas can make an open container appear to lose mass.
21. Emulsion = droplets of one liquid dispersed in another. Milk contains fat droplets dispersed in water.
22. A solute dissolves in a solvent; salt is the solute and water the solvent in salt water.
23. A saturated solution holds as much solute as it can at specified conditions; solubility depends on substance and temperature.
24. Filtration removes insoluble particles; distillation can collect solvent from dissolved nonvolatile salt.
25. Rusting iron usually needs oxygen and water. Painting or galvanising helps reduce corrosion.
26. Acid + base can neutralise each other, producing salt and water in many simple examples; do not mix unknown chemicals.
27. Baking soda is sodium bicarbonate; washing soda is sodium carbonate in a common hydrated form. They are not identical.
28. LPG and CNG are fuels; carbon monoxide from incomplete combustion is poisonous and needs urgent attention.
29. CO₂ is carbon dioxide; CO is carbon monoxide. One extra oxygen atom makes a different substance.
30. Natural indicator: turmeric turns reddish-brown in a basic medium. Indicators show colour changes, not exact identity of every chemical.

*3️⃣ PLANTS & ECOLOGY: HOW THEY WORK*

31. Diffusion = net movement from higher to lower concentration through random motion; a membrane is not essential.
32. Osmosis = net movement of water across a selectively permeable membrane, toward lower water potential.
33. A plant cell's turgor pressure helps support it; water loss may reduce turgor and contribute to wilting.
34. Transpiration = water-vapour loss from aerial plant parts, especially through stomata.
35. Plant shoot bending toward light is an example of phototropism: a directional growth response.
36. Roots often grow downward in response to gravity; growth direction is influenced by multiple environmental signals.
37. Grafting joins a scion to a compatible rooted stock. Farmers use it to propagate selected plants.
38. In horticultural budding a bud is joined to a rootstock; yeast budding is a different asexual reproductive process.
39. Lichen is an association between a fungus and a photosynthetic partner, such as algae or cyanobacteria.
40. Lichens can help indicate air quality, because species differ in sensitivity to pollution.
41. Vermicomposting uses earthworms to help break down organic material under suitable conditions.
42. Biogas is produced by microbes breaking down organic matter without oxygen; it contains methane.
43. Habitat = place an organism lives; niche = its role and interactions in the ecosystem.
44. Mutualism: both partners benefit. Parasitism: one benefits while the other is harmed. Nature can be complex.
45. Biodiversity means variety of life at genetic, species and ecosystem levels.
46. An adaptation is an inherited feature that can help survival/reproduction in an environment; an individual does not choose to evolve.
47. A cactus stores water and often has spines; adaptations vary among species and are not guarantees against drought.
48. Decomposers break down dead material, returning nutrients to ecosystems. Not all microorganisms cause disease.
49. Rainwater harvesting collects rain for later use or recharge; storage and treatment determine whether water is safe.
50. Ozone high in the stratosphere absorbs much harmful ultraviolet radiation; ground-level ozone can harm health.

*4️⃣ HUMAN BODY & HEALTH: CONNECT THE STEPS*

51. Digestion breaks food into smaller absorbable substances; absorption and use by cells are later steps.
52. Saliva begins starch digestion; stomach acid and enzymes support digestion. Most absorption occurs in small intestine.
53. Villi increase the small intestine's surface area for absorption.
54. Diaphragm contracts and moves downward during normal inhalation; chest volume increases.
55. Alveoli provide a large surface for gas exchange; oxygen enters blood and carbon dioxide leaves it.
56. Heart's right side pumps blood to lungs; left side pumps blood to the body in normal circulation.
57. Pulse reflects pressure waves in arteries from heartbeats; pulse rate and breathing rate are different.
58. A reflex is a rapid automatic response. Pain is a warning: do not intentionally touch hot objects to test it.
59. Bone + muscle + joint work together in movement; muscles pull when contracting, not push like a rod.
60. Insulin helps regulate blood glucose; hormone levels and diabetes require qualified medical assessment.
61. Immunity involves several defences; vaccination can train the immune system against specific diseases.
62. Clean water, sanitation and handwashing reduce particular infections; none prevents all disease.
63. Dehydration can be dangerous. Oral rehydration solution replaces water and salts; follow health-worker guidance.
64. A balanced diet contains varied nutrients in appropriate amounts; no single food supplies every need in all situations.
65. Anaemia can have multiple causes, including iron deficiency. Diagnosis and treatment require appropriate advice.

*5️⃣ EARTH, SPACE & THE ENVIRONMENT*

66. Earth's crust, mantle and core are broad layers. The tectonic plates include crust and uppermost mantle.
67. Igneous rocks form from cooled molten material; sedimentary from accumulated material; metamorphic from changes under heat/pressure.
68. Fossils are preserved remains, traces or impressions of past life. Most organisms do not become fossils.
69. Weathering breaks rock down; erosion carries material away; deposition lays transported material down.
70. Groundwater is stored in pore spaces and fractures; an aquifer is a formation able to yield useful quantities of water.
71. Greenhouse gases absorb and re-emit infrared radiation. The natural greenhouse effect warms Earth; added gases increase warming.
72. Ozone depletion and climate change are different issues, though atmospheric processes can interact.
73. Acid rain can form from sulfur dioxide and nitrogen oxides reacting in the atmosphere; normal rain is mildly acidic.
74. Most ocean tides arise mainly from the Moon's gravitational pull, with the Sun also contributing; local coast shapes matter.
75. A geostationary satellite orbits above the equator with Earth's rotation period and direction, appearing fixed over one longitude.
76. An orbiting satellite is continuously falling around Earth while moving sideways; it is not beyond gravity.
77. A shooting star is a meteor, not a star falling from the sky. A meteorite reaches the ground.
78. Earth's shadow on the Moon causes a lunar eclipse. Normal Moon phases are NOT caused by that shadow.
79. Latitude tells position north/south of Equator; longitude east/west of Prime Meridian.
80. IST = UTC+5:30; 10:00 UTC = 15:30 IST on the same date.

*6️⃣ TELUGU: సాహిత్యం, పండుగలు & అర్థం*

81. ద్విపద = a verse form with paired lines. పాల్కురికి సోమనాథుడు → బసవ పురాణం, associated with ద్విపద.
82. ప్రబంధం = a literary composition/form; conventions vary. ఆముక్తమాల్యద → శ్రీకృష్ణదేవరాయలు.
83. చాటుపద్యం = an occasional short verse, often linked to a person or situation; it is not always satire.
84. జానపద గీతం = folk song, often passed through communities; words and tunes can vary by place.
85. తోలుబొమ్మలాట = leather shadow-puppet theatre. Light + figures + screen create shadows.
86. బుర్రకథ, ఒగ్గుకథ = storytelling performance traditions combining narration, song and music.
87. బతుకమ్మ = Telangana floral festival with flower arrangements, songs and community celebrations.
88. సమ్మక్క–సారలమ్మ జాతర = major tribal festival at Medaram, Telangana. Check official dates and local details.
89. సుమతీ శతకం → బద్దెన. దాశరథీ శతకం → కంచర్ల గోపన్న / రామదాసు.
90. మహాప్రస్థానం → శ్రీశ్రీ. కన్యాశుల్కం → గురజాడ అప్పారావు. గబ్బిలం → గుర్రం జాషువా.
91. ఆంధ్ర మహాభాగవతం → పోతన. నా గొడవ → కాళోజీ నారాయణరావు.
92. నన్నయ, తిక్కన, ఎర్రన → కవిత్రయం, associated with Telugu Mahabharata.
93. మకుటం = recurring word/phrase in verses. ఉపమాలంకారం = explicit comparison, often with వలె/వంటి.
94. రూపకం describes one thing as another figuratively; compare “ముఖం చంద్రుని వలె” (simile).
95. కర్త–కర్మ–క్రియ: “సీత పుస్తకం చదివింది” → సీత / పుస్తకం / చదివింది.
96. ఆత్మకథ = one's own life written by oneself; జీవిత చరిత్ర = someone's life written by another.
97. సామెత = proverb; జాతీయము = idiom; meaning depends on actual usage.
98. వాస్తవం = checkable factual claim; అభిప్రాయం = opinion. Evidence is needed for a claim.
99. పర్యాయపదం = similar meaning; వ్యతిరేకపదం = opposite meaning: వెలుగు ↔ చీకటి.
100. పూర్ణవిరామం = full stop; ప్రశ్నార్థకం = question mark; అల్పవిరామం = comma.

*7️⃣ HISTORY, CIVICS & EVERYDAY EVIDENCE*

101. Archaeology studies human past through material remains: tools, pottery, buildings and more.
102. Epigraphy studies inscriptions; numismatics studies coins, medals and related objects.
103. Manuscript = handwritten document. An inscription may be carved on stone or written on another durable surface.
104. Primary sources are evidence from the period or direct participants; they may still be biased or mistaken.
105. Carbon-14 can help date once-living material within its useful range; it does not directly date every metal object.
106. India's Parliament = President + Lok Sabha + Rajya Sabha. They have distinct roles in the lawmaking process.
107. Gram Sabha includes registered voters in a village-level area; elected Gram Panchayat is a local governing body.
108. A bill is a proposal; it does not become law merely because someone introduces it.
109. Secret ballot protects privacy of choice; election rules and dates must be checked with the relevant authority.
110. A graph with a truncated vertical axis can exaggerate apparent differences; check scale, units and source.
111. A single observation is not always representative. Compare more samples and repeat measurements where possible.
112. Correlation alone does not prove causation; consider other possible explanations.
113. A claim that “all metals are magnetic” fails: copper is not attracted like iron. One counterexample disproves an absolute claim.
114. A trustworthy-looking message can still be phishing. Never give an OTP to claim an unexpected prize.
115. Verify competition eligibility, closing date and prize on the organiser's official page; screenshots can be outdated.

*8️⃣ EASY MATHS — THINK IN STEPS*

116. Ratio 2:3 means 2 parts to 3 parts. For a total of 25, 5 parts = 25, so shares are 10 and 15.
117. 3/4 of 80 = 60. 25% of 80 = 20. 3/4 = 75%.
118. If 5 pens cost ₹75 at equal price, 1 costs ₹15; 8 cost ₹120.
119. 120 km in 3 h → average speed 40 km/h. Actual speed can change during the trip.
120. Rectangle 8 cm × 5 cm → area 40 cm²; perimeter 26 cm. Units matter.
121. Triangle area = ½ × base × perpendicular height. Base 10 cm, height 4 cm → 20 cm².
122. Mean of 3, 5, 7 = 5. Median of 1, 2, 9 = 2; extreme values affect mean differently.
123. Mode of 2, 2, 3, 4 is 2; range = 4 − 2 = 2.
124. Probability of drawing a red ball from 2 red and 3 blue, equally likely: 2/5.
125. 200 increased by 10% = 220; then reduced by 10% = 198, not 200.
126. 40% → 50% = 10 percentage points; the relative increase is 25%.
127. 20 → 40 is a 100% increase, while 40 is 200% of 20.
128. 2 hours 15 minutes = 135 minutes. 1 km = 1000 m; 1 litre = 1000 mL.
129. If a number is divisible by 2 and 3, it is divisible by 6. Divisibility by 3 depends on digit sum.
130. Pattern 4, 7, 10, 13 suggests +3 → 16; more than one rule may fit a short sequence.
