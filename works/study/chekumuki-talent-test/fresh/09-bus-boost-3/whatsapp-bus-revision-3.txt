*1️⃣ FORCES, FLUIDS & MACHINES*

1. Force changes motion or shape; SI unit = newton (N). F = mass × acceleration when mass is constant.
2. Pressure = force ÷ area; same force on less area produces more pressure.
3. Liquid pressure at a given depth increases with fluid density and depth, assuming a stationary fluid.
4. Buoyancy (ఉత్ప్లావక బలం) = upward force exerted by a fluid on an immersed object.
5. Archimedes' principle: buoyant force equals the weight of the displaced fluid.
6. A floating ship displaces enough water to balance its weight. Shape and overall average density matter.
7. Pascal's principle: a pressure change in a confined fluid is transmitted throughout it.
8. Ideal hydraulic example: 10 N on 1 cm² → 10 N/cm² → 50 N on 5 cm². Larger output moves less distance for equal displaced volume.
9. A hydraulic brake uses fluid pressure; force multiplication is not free energy.
10. Surface tension results from interactions between liquid molecules; some small insects can stand on water.
11. Capillary action can draw liquid through a narrow tube or porous material. Plant water transport also involves other mechanisms.
12. Friction helps tyres grip the road, but also causes wear and heating.
13. A lever turns around a fulcrum. Changing distances from the fulcrum can change the effort needed.
14. Mechanical advantage = output force ÷ input force. Real machines have losses; force gain can require greater input distance.
15. Power = energy transferred ÷ time; 1 watt = 1 joule per second.

*2️⃣ LIGHT, RADIATION & ELECTRICITY*

16. Electromagnetic spectrum: radio → microwave → infrared → visible → ultraviolet → X-ray → gamma.
17. In that order frequency generally increases and wavelength decreases. All travel at the same speed in vacuum.
18. Radio and microwave are electromagnetic waves. SONAR uses sound, not electromagnetic waves.
19. Infrared is associated with thermal radiation; objects at ordinary temperatures emit infrared.
20. Ultraviolet can damage skin and eyes. Never look directly at the Sun without proper protection.
21. X-rays can help produce medical images; professionals control their use because ionising radiation can damage tissue.
22. Radiation is energy travelling through space or matter; radioactive contamination is unwanted radioactive material in a place or on a person.
23. Half-life = time for about half the radioactive nuclei in a large sample to decay. 80 → 40 → 20 after two half-lives.
24. Half-life does NOT mean all radioactive atoms disappear after that time.
25. A mirror reflects light; refraction changes light's direction when its speed changes between media, except in some directions.
26. White light contains many visible wavelengths; a prism can spread colours by dispersion.
27. Electric current is charge flow, measured in amperes. Voltage is energy transferred per unit charge, measured in volts.
28. For an ohmic conductor under fixed conditions, V = IR. At 12 V and 3 Ω, I = 4 A.
29. Power P = VI. At 12 V and 2 A, power = 24 W. kWh is a unit of energy, not power.
30. A solar cell converts light to electrical energy; a battery stores chemical energy that can provide electrical energy.

*3️⃣ CHEMISTRY: REACTIONS & MATERIALS*

31. Atom's proton number identifies the element. Isotopes have the same proton number but different neutron numbers.
32. A compound contains elements chemically combined in fixed proportions; a mixture can have varying proportions.
33. Chemical reaction forms new substances; physical changes do not necessarily form them.
34. Catalyst = substance that speeds a reaction via a lower-activation-energy pathway without being consumed overall.
35. Enzymes are biological catalysts; most are proteins. Temperature and pH can affect their activity.
36. High heat can alter an enzyme's shape and reduce its activity; more heat does not improve activity indefinitely.
37. Exothermic reaction transfers heat to surroundings; endothermic reaction absorbs heat from surroundings.
38. An exothermic reaction can still need activation energy to start; a match starts fuel burning.
39. Acid turns blue litmus red; base turns red litmus blue. Never taste chemicals to identify them.
40. Neutralisation often produces salt and water, e.g. hydrochloric acid + sodium hydroxide. Conditions and chemicals matter.
41. A solution has solute dissolved in solvent. In salt water, salt is solute; water is solvent.
42. Filtration removes insoluble solids; chromatography separates components moving differently with a solvent.
43. A saturated solution holds the maximum amount of solute under specified conditions; solubility can change with temperature.
44. Alloy is a material containing a metal and other element(s). Brass: copper + zinc; bronze: copper + tin.
45. Galvanising uses zinc coating to help protect iron/steel; rusting needs oxygen and water in ordinary conditions.

*4️⃣ BIOLOGY: CELLS, CLASSIFICATION & BODY*

46. Cell is the basic structural and functional unit of life; a cell membrane controls movement across its boundary.
47. Plant cells generally have a cell wall and chloroplasts; animal cells lack these structures.
48. Gene = DNA segment. Alleles are different forms of a gene. Phenotype depends on genes and often environment.
49. Mitosis usually produces two genetically similar cells; meiosis reduces chromosome number in sexual reproduction.
50. Human body cells normally have 46 chromosomes; human sperm and egg usually have 23 each.
51. Classification groups organisms based on shared features and evolutionary relationships; classifications can change with evidence.
52. Binomial scientific name has genus + specific epithet. In Homo sapiens, Homo is the genus, sapiens the epithet.
53. Mango's scientific name is Mangifera indica: genus starts with a capital, specific epithet lowercase.
54. Bacteria are single-celled organisms without a nucleus enclosed in a membrane; viruses need host cells to reproduce.
55. Fungi absorb nutrients from their surroundings. Yeast is a fungus; mushrooms are fruiting structures of some fungi.
56. Herbarium = collection of preserved, usually dried and labelled plant specimens. Botanical garden maintains living plants.
57. Seed banks preserve suitable seeds under controlled conditions; not all seeds tolerate the same storage methods.
58. Hydroponics grows plants without soil using nutrient solutions; roots still require oxygen, water and nutrients.
59. A vaccine trains immune responses for specific diseases; antibiotics do not cure viral infections.
60. Red blood cells carry oxygen using haemoglobin; platelets help clotting; white blood cells contribute to defence.
61. Arteries carry blood away from the heart; veins carry it towards the heart. This is about direction, not always oxygen level.
62. Alveoli in lungs exchange gases; villi in the small intestine help absorb nutrients.
63. Kidneys filter blood and regulate water and salts. Nephrons are functional units of the kidney.
64. Insulin helps regulate blood glucose; medical conditions should be assessed by qualified professionals.
65. Reflex = rapid automatic response. Nervous and hormonal signalling are related but different systems.

*5️⃣ ECOLOGY, EARTH & WEATHER*

66. Habitat = place an organism lives. Niche = its role and interactions in an ecosystem.
67. Endemic species is native to and restricted to a particular area; endemic disease means consistently present at expected levels in an area.
68. Keystone species has an ecosystem effect disproportionately large compared with its abundance.
69. Bioaccumulation = substance builds up in one organism over time; biomagnification = increasing concentration across food-chain levels for certain persistent substances.
70. Not every chemical biomagnifies; properties of the substance and food web matter.
71. Biodiversity includes genetic, species and ecosystem variety. Conservation protects habitats as well as individual species.
72. Producer makes organic food using energy; consumer obtains food from organisms; decomposers break down dead material.
73. Plants and animals both respire; plants also photosynthesise when suitable light and conditions are available.
74. Nitrogen-fixing bacteria in some legume root nodules help convert nitrogen into usable compounds.
75. Groundwater occupies spaces and cracks below ground; an aquifer can yield useful quantities of water.
76. Weathering breaks rock in place; erosion transports it; deposition lays it down elsewhere.
77. A watershed is land draining towards a common outlet. Upstream pollution can affect downstream water.
78. A monsoon is a seasonal wind system linked to seasonal rainfall, not simply any heavy shower.
79. El Niño involves episodic warming of parts of the tropical Pacific and can affect weather worldwide; it alone does not decide every Indian monsoon.
80. Weather is short-term conditions; climate is a long-term pattern. One rainy day does not define climate.
81. Greenhouse gases absorb and re-emit infrared radiation; adding them can strengthen Earth's warming effect.
82. Stratospheric ozone absorbs much harmful UV; ground-level ozone can harm health. Ozone depletion and climate change are distinct issues.
83. A tsunami is a series of waves often caused by sudden water displacement. Not every undersea earthquake generates one.
84. Earthquake magnitude describes source size; intensity describes local shaking/effects. Epicentre is above the focus at the surface.
85. The International Date Line generally follows 180° longitude with deviations; crossing it changes the calendar date.

*6️⃣ TELUGU: LITERATURE, LANGUAGE & CULTURE*

86. సుమతీ శతకం → బద్దెన. దాశరథీ శతకం → కంచర్ల గోపన్న / రామదాసు.
87. దాశరథీ శతకం author is not the modern poet దాశరథి కృష్ణమాచార్య; similar names can mislead.
88. ఆంధ్ర మహాభాగవతం → పోతన. ఆముక్తమాల్యద → శ్రీకృష్ణదేవరాయలు.
89. మహాప్రస్థానం → శ్రీశ్రీ. కన్యాశుల్కం → గురజాడ అప్పారావు. గబ్బిలం → గుర్రం జాషువా.
90. నా గొడవ → కాళోజీ నారాయణరావు. బసవ పురాణం → పాల్కురికి సోమనాథుడు.
91. నన్నయ, తిక్కన, ఎర్రన → కవిత్రయం, associated with Telugu Mahabharata.
92. మకుటం = recurring word or phrase in verses; “సుమతీ” is a clue to సుమతీ శతకం.
93. ఉపమాలంకారం: explicit comparison, as in “ముఖం చంద్రుని వలె ఉంది”. వలె indicates “like”.
94. రూపకాలంకారం describes one thing as another figuratively; don't classify by a single word without context.
95. అనుప్రాస involves repetition of similar sounds/consonants for a sound effect; textbook rules determine tricky cases.
96. ద్విపద is a verse form of paired lines. ఛందస్సు concerns verse metre and rhythmic structure.
97. కర్త–కర్మ–క్రియ: “సీత పుస్తకం చదివింది” → సీత / పుస్తకం / చదివింది.
98. సామెత = proverb; జాతీయము = idiom. Meaning depends on usage, not literal translation alone.
99. ఆత్మకథ = one's own life written by oneself; జీవిత చరిత్ర = a life account written by another.
100. తోలుబొమ్మలాట = leather shadow-puppet theatre. బతుకమ్మ = Telangana floral festival with community songs.

*7️⃣ CIVICS, EVIDENCE & EASY MATHS*

101. Indian Parliament consists of the President, Lok Sabha and Rajya Sabha.
102. Broadly, legislature makes laws, executive administers them and judiciary interprets/applies them. Their actual powers follow the Constitution.
103. Fundamental Rights and Fundamental Duties are distinct parts of India's Constitution; specific details and limits matter.
104. Gram Sabha includes registered village-area voters; Gram Panchayat is an elected local governing institution.
105. Archaeology studies the human past through material remains; epigraphy studies inscriptions; numismatics studies coins and related objects.
106. A manuscript is handwritten. A primary source may still be mistaken or biased; compare independent evidence.
107. A graph's axis scale and units matter; a truncated scale can exaggerate apparent differences.
108. Correlation does not by itself prove causation. A third factor may influence both observations.
109. If 4 notebooks cost ₹60 at equal prices, 1 costs ₹15 and 7 cost ₹105.
110. 3/4 of 80 = 60; 25% of 80 = 20. 3/4 = 75%.
111. ₹200 increased 10% becomes ₹220; reducing ₹220 by 10% gives ₹198, not ₹200.
112. 40% to 50% is a rise of 10 percentage points; relative increase is 25%.
113. 20 → 40 is 100% increase. The final 40 is 200% OF the original 20.
114. Mean of 2, 3, 3, 4, 18 = 6; median = 3; mode = 3; range = 16.
115. A bag has 3 red, 2 blue balls. One equally likely draw: P(red) = 3/5.
116. Distance = speed × time. At constant 30 km/h for 2 h, distance = 60 km.
117. Rectangle 8 cm × 5 cm: area 40 cm²; perimeter 26 cm.
118. Triangle area = ½ × base × perpendicular height; 10 cm and 4 cm give 20 cm².
119. Shares in ratio 2:3 with total 25 → 5 parts = 25 → shares 10 and 15.
120. Binary 1011 = decimal 8 + 2 + 1 = 11. Eight bits make one byte.
