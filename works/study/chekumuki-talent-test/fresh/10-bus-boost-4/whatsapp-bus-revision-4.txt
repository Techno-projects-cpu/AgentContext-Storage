*1️⃣ SCIENTIFIC THINKING & MEASUREMENT*

1. Observation is what you notice or measure; inference is an interpretation. Wet ground is an observation; “it rained” is one possible inference.
2. Hypothesis = a testable proposed explanation. A prediction states what you expect to observe if the hypothesis applies.
3. Independent variable = the factor changed; dependent variable = the outcome measured. Keep other relevant conditions similar.
4. Comparing seeds with/without fertiliser? Keep seed type, soil, light and water similar; measure growth for several plants.
5. Control group offers a comparison; it does not automatically prove every difference has one cause.
6. Repeated trials help reveal variability. Average = sum ÷ number of measurements; report unusual results honestly.
7. Random error can make repeated readings vary; a systematic error shifts measurements in a consistent direction.
8. Accuracy = closeness to a true value; precision = closeness of repeated measurements to one another. Precise readings can still be inaccurate.
9. A measuring cylinder shows volume; read a water meniscus at eye level at the bottom of the curve.
10. A balance compares mass; a spring balance measures force. Neither directly measures temperature.
11. SI base units include metre (length), kilogram (mass), second (time), kelvin (temperature), ampere (current).
12. 1 m = 100 cm; 1 km = 1000 m; 1 L = 1000 mL; 1 hour = 3600 s.
13. 1 m/s = 3.6 km/h. Example: 10 m/s = 36 km/h.
14. Resolution is the smallest change an instrument can distinguish; more digits on a display do not automatically mean more accuracy.
15. A claim with correlation alone does not prove cause. Search for alternative explanations and better evidence.

*2️⃣ PHYSICS: EVERYDAY CONNECTIONS*

16. Pressure = force ÷ area. A sharp blade can apply more pressure with the same force over a smaller area.
17. Liquid pressure generally increases with depth in a stationary liquid of uniform density.
18. Drinking through a straw: lowering pressure inside allows atmospheric pressure to push liquid upward.
19. Buoyancy is upward force from fluid; its magnitude equals the weight of fluid displaced (Archimedes' principle).
20. A floating ship displaces enough water to balance its weight; hollow shape and overall average density matter.
21. Work = force × displacement in force's direction. Holding a stationary bag still requires body energy, but mechanical work on it is zero if displacement is zero.
22. A longer spanner can make turning easier: torque depends on force and perpendicular distance from the pivot.
23. A screw is an inclined plane wrapped around a cylinder; it trades more turning distance for force advantage, subject to friction.
24. A pulley can change force direction; some pulley arrangements reduce effort while requiring more rope movement.
25. When light hits a mirror, angles of incidence and reflection are measured from the normal and are equal.
26. Refraction occurs when light changes speed across media; a pencil partly in water can appear bent.
27. Total internal reflection can occur when light travels from a higher to lower refractive-index medium above a critical angle; optical fibres use it.
28. A convex lens can converge parallel light rays in air; a concave lens diverges them.
29. An echo is reflected sound. Distance = speed × echo travel time ÷ 2 because sound travels out and back.
30. Resonance: a system can respond strongly when driven near a natural frequency; damping affects the response.
31. A transformer changes alternating voltage using electromagnetic induction. Ordinary transformers do not work as intended with steady DC.
32. An electromagnet uses current in a coil; a soft iron core can strengthen the magnetic field. Its strength depends on construction and current.
33. Electric power P = VI; 5 V and 2 A → 10 W. Electrical energy = power × time.
34. An LED is a light-emitting diode; it must be used with a suitable circuit and current limit.
35. Electric shock can kill. Never connect improvised experiments to household mains.

*3️⃣ CHEMISTRY & MATERIALS*

36. Atomic number = number of protons. Mass number = protons + neutrons in a nucleus.
37. Isotopes of one element have equal proton numbers but different neutron numbers.
38. Chemical symbol starts with a capital: Co = cobalt; CO = carbon monoxide, a compound.
39. A molecule of O₂ contains two oxygen atoms; ozone is O₃. Different arrangements can give different properties.
40. Conservation of mass: in an ordinary chemical reaction in a closed system, total mass remains the same.
41. A catalyst speeds a reaction through an alternative pathway with lower activation energy; it is not consumed overall.
42. Enzymes are biological catalysts, mostly proteins; their activity depends on factors including temperature and pH.
43. Oxidation is not always simply “adding oxygen”: at a broader level it involves electron loss. Reduction involves electron gain.
44. Rusting of iron usually involves oxygen and water; galvanising uses zinc to protect iron or steel.
45. A solute dissolves in a solvent. A solution may contain dissolved solids invisible to ordinary filtration.
46. Solubility varies with substance and conditions. A saturated solution holds the maximum under its specified conditions.
47. Paper chromatography can separate ink components that move differently with the solvent.
48. Distillation boils liquid and collects condensed vapour; evaporation can leave dissolved nonvolatile solids behind.
49. An emulsion contains droplets of one liquid dispersed in another; milk includes dispersed fat droplets.
50. Biodegradable means capable of biological breakdown under suitable conditions; it does not justify littering.

*4️⃣ LIFE: MICROSCOPIC TO ECOSYSTEM*

51. A microscope increases the apparent size of small objects. Magnification alone does not guarantee better resolution.
52. Bacteria are cells without a membrane-bound nucleus; viruses require host cells to reproduce.
53. Fungi absorb nutrients from surroundings; yeast is a fungus used in some fermentations.
54. Enzymes assist digestion. Digestion breaks food down; absorption moves usable molecules into blood or lymph.
55. Small-intestine villi increase absorptive surface area; alveoli in lungs provide a surface for gas exchange.
56. Red blood cells use haemoglobin to carry oxygen. Platelets help blood clot; white blood cells contribute to defence.
57. A reflex is a rapid automatic response. A reflex arc involves receptors, nerve pathways and an effector.
58. Vaccines train responses against specific diseases; antibiotics do not treat viral infections.
59. A disease vector can transmit a pathogen. Malaria is caused by Plasmodium parasites carried by infected mosquitoes.
60. Blood groups involve antigens; actual transfusions require professional compatibility testing and crossmatching.
61. DNA contains genetic information. Genes are DNA segments; different versions of a gene are alleles.
62. Mitosis generally produces two genetically similar cells. Meiosis reduces chromosome number in sexual reproduction.
63. Pollination is pollen transfer to a flower's stigma; fertilisation is fusion of reproductive cells.
64. Seed germination generally needs water, oxygen and suitable temperature; light requirements vary by species.
65. Plants transport water mainly in xylem and sugars in phloem. Water loss through stomata is transpiration.
66. Nitrogen-fixing bacteria in some legume root nodules help convert atmospheric nitrogen into usable forms.
67. Lichen is a close association of a fungus and a photosynthetic partner such as an alga or cyanobacterium.
68. A habitat is where an organism lives; its niche includes its role and interactions.
69. Bioaccumulation is build-up in an organism over time; biomagnification can increase concentration across food-chain levels for certain persistent substances.
70. Eutrophication can follow excess nutrient runoff; decomposers may use up dissolved oxygen, harming aquatic life.

*5️⃣ EARTH, SPACE & PRACTICAL SAFETY*

71. Earth's crust, mantle and core are broad layers. Plates consist of crust and uppermost mantle.
72. Igneous rock forms from cooling molten material; sedimentary rock from accumulated material; metamorphic rock changes under heat/pressure.
73. Weathering breaks rock down in place; erosion transports material; deposition lays it down.
74. An aquifer is a water-bearing geological formation that can yield useful water; groundwater is not necessarily an underground river.
75. Cyclones bring changing hazards. Follow current official warnings and evacuation instructions, not forwarded rumours.
76. Barometer measures atmospheric pressure; hygrometer humidity; anemometer wind speed; rain gauge rainfall depth.
77. Earthquake focus is the starting point inside Earth; epicentre is the surface point vertically above it.
78. Magnitude describes earthquake source size; intensity describes effects and shaking at a particular location.
79. During an earthquake, follow locally issued guidance. Indoors, “Drop, Cover and Hold On” is widely recommended where appropriate; do not rush into danger.
80. A tsunami is a series of waves from sudden water displacement. If strong/long shaking is felt on a coast, move to higher ground when safe and follow alerts.
81. Rotation of Earth causes day and night; axial tilt and revolution are the principal reasons for seasons.
82. Moon phases show changing views of its sunlit side. Earth's shadow causes a lunar eclipse, not ordinary phases.
83. Meteoroid = object in space; meteor = atmospheric light streak; meteorite = material that reaches the ground.
84. Remote sensing gathers information without contact, e.g. satellite observations of crops; interpretation needs checks.
85. Latitude is N/S of Equator; longitude E/W of Prime Meridian. IST = UTC+5:30.

*6️⃣ TELUGU: WORDS, WORKS & CULTURE*

86. సుమతీ శతకం → బద్దెన; దాశరథీ శతకం → కంచర్ల గోపన్న / రామదాసు.
87. మకుటం = recurring word or phrase in verses. “సుమతీ” is a clue to సుమతీ శతకం.
88. పోతన → ఆంధ్ర మహాభాగవతం. శ్రీకృష్ణదేవరాయలు → ఆముక్తమాల్యద.
89. గురజాడ అప్పారావు → కన్యాశుల్కం. గుర్రం జాషువా → గబ్బిలం. శ్రీశ్రీ → మహాప్రస్థానం.
90. కాళోజీ నారాయణరావు → నా గొడవ. పాల్కురికి సోమనాథుడు → బసవ పురాణం.
91. నన్నయ, తిక్కన, ఎర్రన → కవిత్రయం, associated with Telugu Mahabharata.
92. ఉపమాలంకారం = explicit comparison: “ముఖం చంద్రుని వలె ఉంది.” రూపకం describes one thing as another figuratively.
93. అనుప్రాస uses repetition of similar sounds; context and textbook definitions matter in tricky examples.
94. కర్త–కర్మ–క్రియ: “రాము బంతిని విసిరాడు” → రాము / బంతిని / విసిరాడు.
95. సామెత = proverb; జాతీయము = idiomatic expression. Understand intended meaning, not just literal words.
96. పర్యాయపదం = similar meaning; వ్యతిరేకపదం = opposite meaning. వెలుగు ↔ చీకటి.
97. ఆత్మకథ = one's life written by oneself; జీవిత చరిత్ర = a life story written by another.
98. ద్విపద = paired-line verse form; ఛందస్సు concerns poetic metre and rhythm.
99. తోలుబొమ్మలాట = leather shadow-puppet theatre; బుర్రకథ and ఒగ్గుకథ are storytelling performances.
100. బతుకమ్మ is a Telangana floral festival; local songs and practices can vary.

*7️⃣ MATHS & LOGIC — SHORT WORKED EXAMPLES*

101. Ratio 2:3, total 25 → 5 parts = 25 → values 10 and 15.
102. 3/4 of 80 = 60. 0.25 = 25% = 1/4.
103. 20 → 40 is a 100% increase; 40 is 200% OF 20. These are different statements.
104. 40% → 50% = 10 percentage points; relative increase = 10/40 = 25%.
105. ₹200 with 10% off → ₹180; a second 10% off → ₹162.
106. If 4 notebooks cost ₹60 at equal price, 7 cost ₹105.
107. 120 km in 3 h → average speed 40 km/h. Average does not prove speed was constant.
108. Mean of 2, 3, 3, 4, 18 = 6; median = 3; mode = 3; range = 16.
109. Rectangle 8 cm × 5 cm: area 40 cm²; perimeter 26 cm.
110. Triangle area = ½ × base × perpendicular height; 10 cm × 4 cm gives 20 cm².
111. Two red and three blue balls, equal chance: P(red) = 2/5. Without replacement, later chances can change.
112. If it rains the road may be wet; a wet road does not prove it rained. A hose is another explanation.
113. A sequence 4, 7, 10, 13 suggests +3 → 16, but a short list can fit more than one rule.
114. A bar chart with a shortened axis can exaggerate differences. Check scale, units and denominator.
115. A sample of three leaves from one tree cannot establish the condition of every tree in the district.

119. Coil + iron core + battery (proper safe circuit) → ELECTROMAGNET.
120. Enzyme + substrate + faster reaction → BIOLOGICAL CATALYST.
121. Stigma + pollen + flower → POLLINATION, not necessarily fertilisation.
122. Fungus + alga + rock surface → LICHEN ASSOCIATION.

124. Stone inscription + copied script + historian → EPIGRAPHY.
125. Leather figures + lamp + screen shadow → తోలుబొమ్మలాట.
