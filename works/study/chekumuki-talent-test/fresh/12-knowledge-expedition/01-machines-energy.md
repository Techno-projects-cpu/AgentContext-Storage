# Machines and energy: follow the change ⚙️

## Turning, gears and friction

1. **Torque / moment of force (బల భ్రమణ ప్రభావం):** the turning effect of a force. Its magnitude is force × perpendicular distance from pivot. A longer spanner makes the same perpendicular force more effective.
2. **Door example:** pushing far from the hinge turns the door more easily than pushing close to it. Force directed along the door toward the hinge has little turning effect.
3. **Balance:** a lever is in rotational balance when clockwise and anticlockwise moments balance. A 10 N force 2 m from a pivot balances a 20 N force 1 m away, ignoring the lever's own weight.
4. **Gears:** toothed wheels transfer rotation. Two externally meshing gears turn in opposite directions; an added intermediate gear reverses direction again.
5. **Gear teeth and speed:** in a simple pair, if a 10-tooth driver turns a 20-tooth driven gear, the driven gear rotates once for every two turns of the driver. No slipping or losses are assumed.
6. **Speed vs torque:** an ideal gear reduction gives lower output speed and greater output torque; actual machines have friction and other losses. It does not create energy.
7. **Bicycle example:** pedalling effort also depends on terrain, wheel size and speed. Gear selection changes the relation between pedal turns and wheel turns; no one setting is easiest for every situation.
8. **Friction (ఘర్షణ):** opposes relative motion or attempted motion at contact surfaces. It can be useful for walking and braking, while also causing heating and wear.
9. **Lubrication:** oil or grease can reduce friction and wear between appropriate moving surfaces. Lubricating a bicycle brake's braking surface would be unsafe because brakes need friction.
10. **Bearings:** parts that support relative movement between components; ball bearings can reduce sliding friction by using rolling elements. They are not friction-free.
11. **Mechanical advantage:** output force ÷ input force. If output force is 60 N and input is 20 N, advantage = 3. The input may move a greater distance; work and energy are not created.
12. **Efficiency:** useful output energy ÷ total input energy × 100%. With 60 J useful output from 100 J input, efficiency = 60%. The rest is transferred elsewhere, often as heat or sound.

## Heating and cooling

13. **Temperature** measures a thermal state; **heat** is energy transferred because of a temperature difference. A larger warm bucket can contain more thermal energy than a small hotter cup.
14. **Specific heat capacity:** energy needed per unit mass for each degree of temperature rise, when no phase change occurs. Different materials need different energy inputs for the same temperature change.
15. **Latent heat:** energy transferred during a phase change without a temperature change during that phase change under specified conditions. Ice melting near 0°C at normal pressure absorbs energy without immediately getting warmer.
16. **Evaporative cooling:** faster particles leaving a liquid carry energy away; sweating helps cool the body when sweat evaporates. High humidity can make evaporation less effective.
17. **Pressure cooker:** higher internal pressure raises water's boiling temperature above its value at normal atmospheric pressure, helping food cook faster. Follow equipment instructions and never obstruct the safety valve.
18. **Refrigerator:** a powered system transfers heat from inside the cabinet to the room. The room receives heat removed from the food space plus energy supplied to the device; an open fridge is not a free room cooler.
19. **Thermal expansion:** many substances expand when warmed. Expansion joints in bridges allow for movement; how much they expand depends on material and temperature change.
20. **Insulation:** slows unwanted energy transfer. A thermos reduces conduction/convection with a vacuum layer and radiation with reflective surfaces; no real container prevents heat transfer forever.
21. **Solar heating vs solar cell:** a solar water heater uses sunlight mainly to heat water. A photovoltaic cell converts light directly to electrical energy. Both use sunlight, but their outputs differ.
22. **Efficiency trap:** a larger watt rating means a greater rate of energy transfer when operating at that rating, not automatically greater total energy use. Time matters: energy = power × time.

## Quick checks

- 5-tooth driver → 15-tooth driven gear: **three driver revolutions per driven revolution** in an ideal pair.
- 2 kg material heated 3°C with specific heat capacity 100 J/(kg·°C): energy = 2 × 100 × 3 = **600 J**, assuming no losses or phase change.
- A fridge warms the room rather than cooling it when its door is left open in a closed room, assuming normal operation.
