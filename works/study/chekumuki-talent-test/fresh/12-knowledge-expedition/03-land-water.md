# Land, rivers and coasts: one connected system 🌏

## Moving Earth

1. **Lithosphere:** the rigid outer portion of Earth, including crust and uppermost mantle, broken into moving plates. The plates move slowly relative to one another.
2. **Divergent boundary:** plates move apart; new crust can form as molten material rises. A mid-ocean ridge is an example of a divergent setting.
3. **Convergent boundary:** plates move towards each other. One plate may descend beneath another (subduction), or continental collision may help build mountains.
4. **Transform boundary:** plates slide past one another. Earthquakes can occur at these boundaries, but the type and effect of any event vary.
5. **Earthquakes:** rupture inside Earth releases energy and causes seismic waves. The epicentre is above the focus; magnitude and local intensity are different measurements.
6. **Rock cycle:** igneous rock forms from cooled molten material; sedimentary rock from sediments and other processes; metamorphic rock changes under heat/pressure. A rock need not pass through the types in one fixed order.
7. **Fossil:** preserved remains, traces or impressions of past life. A footprint can be a trace fossil; most living things do not become fossils.
8. **Relative dating:** one layer can be older than another in an undisturbed sequence. Disturbance, folding and erosion complicate interpretation. Radiometric methods may provide numerical age estimates when suitable material is available.

## Mountains, rivers and water use

9. **Rain shadow:** moist air rises over a mountain, cools and may produce rain on the windward side. Descending air on the leeward side can be drier; actual rainfall depends on local conditions.
10. **Tributary:** smaller river or stream joining a larger one. **Confluence:** place where streams or rivers meet.
11. **Drainage basin:** area whose surface water drains to a common outlet. A watershed boundary separates neighbouring basins; upstream actions can affect downstream communities.
12. **Meander:** a bend in a river channel. Erosion and deposition at different places can change river shape over time; floods and geology complicate the simple model.
13. **Floodplain:** relatively flat land beside a river that can flood. Fertile sediment can accumulate there, but settlement in flood-prone areas needs risk planning.
14. **Delta:** deposits at a river mouth can form a delta where sediment accumulates faster than it is removed. **Estuary:** a coastal area where freshwater and seawater mix, influenced by tides. Real river mouths can have features of both.
15. **Salinity:** concentration of dissolved salts in water. Estuary salinity can vary with tide, rainfall and river flow; brackish water is neither simply fresh nor ocean-strength everywhere.
16. **Reservoir:** water stored behind a dam or in a constructed/natural basin. Reservoirs can supply irrigation, water or electricity, but construction and operation affect ecosystems and communities.
17. **Irrigation:** supplying water to crops. Drip irrigation can deliver water near roots and reduce losses under suitable management, but its effectiveness depends on design and maintenance.
18. **Groundwater recharge:** water entering the ground can replenish an aquifer; extraction, geology, rainfall and land cover affect groundwater levels. A recharge structure does not automatically make water drinkable.
19. **Water quality:** clear-looking water may still contain harmful microbes or dissolved chemicals. Use locally tested and approved safe sources.
20. **River example:** the Godavari and Krishna are major Indian rivers flowing eastwards to the Bay of Bengal. Their basins and tributaries span multiple regions; do not assume every Telangana river reaches the sea independently.

## Coastal ecosystems

21. **Mangroves:** salt-tolerant trees and shrubs found in suitable tropical/subtropical coastal conditions. Their roots support habitats and can slow water movement and stabilise sediment; protection from storms is not absolute.
22. **Coral reefs:** many corals are animals living with photosynthetic partners. Reefs support diverse life and can be damaged by heat stress, pollution and other pressures.
23. **Coral bleaching:** stress can cause corals to lose their colourful symbiotic partners or pigments. Bleached corals are stressed, not necessarily immediately dead; recovery depends on conditions.
24. **Wetlands:** areas where water influences soil and life for part or all of the time. They can support biodiversity, store floodwater and filter some pollutants, but they are not unlimited treatment plants.
25. **Ramsar Convention:** international agreement on wetlands. A site's designation recognises importance; protection still depends on management and local action.
26. **Biodiversity:** variation in genes, species and ecosystems. Conserving connected habitats can help organisms move and maintain populations.
27. **Ecological succession:** change in a community over time after new habitat forms or disturbance occurs. It is not a fixed inevitable ladder in every ecosystem.
28. **Carrying capacity:** population size an environment can support under specified conditions. Resources and conditions change, so it is not a permanent universal number.
