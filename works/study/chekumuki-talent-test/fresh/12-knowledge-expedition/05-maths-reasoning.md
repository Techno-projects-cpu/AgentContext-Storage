# Maths and reasoning workshop — small steps, clear thinking 🔢

## Fractions, ratios and rates

1. **Equivalent fractions:** 2/3 = 4/6 because numerator and denominator are multiplied by the same nonzero number.
2. **Add fractions:** 1/3 + 1/6 = 2/6 + 1/6 = 3/6 = 1/2. Do not add denominators directly.
3. **Fraction of an amount:** 3/5 of 40 = 40 × 3/5 = 24. The remaining 2/5 is 16.
4. **Ratio sharing:** divide ₹350 in ratio 2:5. Seven parts = ₹350; one part = ₹50. Shares are ₹100 and ₹250.
5. **Unit price:** 750 g costs ₹90. Price per kg = 90 ÷ 0.75 = ₹120. Compare equal weights before deciding.
6. **Rate:** a tap fills 1/4 of a tank in an hour at a constant rate; it takes 4 hours for an empty tank. Real flow may vary.
7. **Two workers, stated rates:** A fills 1/6 of a tank/hour and B 1/3/hour. Together: 1/6 + 2/6 = 1/2 tank/hour, so 2 hours if rates stay constant and no water is lost.
8. **Average speed:** a bus travels 90 km in 2 h and 30 km in 1 h. Total distance / total time = 120/3 = 40 km/h, not the unweighted mean of the two segment speeds.

## Percent, area and averages

9. **Percent of a quantity:** 15% of 200 = 0.15 × 200 = 30.
10. **Reverse percentage:** price after a 20% discount is ₹240. That is 80% of original; original = 240 ÷ 0.8 = ₹300.
11. **Percentage-point difference:** 35% to 42% is +7 percentage points; relative change = 7/35 = 20%.
12. **Simple interest:** ₹2,000 at 5% per year for 3 years gives 2000 × 5 × 3/100 = ₹300; total = ₹2,300 if no other charges.
13. **Rectangle:** 12 m × 5 m → area 60 m², perimeter 34 m. A border length and a surface area have different units.
14. **Triangle:** base 12 cm, perpendicular height 5 cm → area 1/2 × 12 × 5 = 30 cm².
15. **Cuboid:** 4 cm × 3 cm × 2 cm → volume 24 cm³. One cubic centimetre equals one millilitre.
16. **Mean and median:** 3, 4, 5, 6, 22 → mean 8, median 5. The large value affects the mean more strongly.
17. **Weighted mean:** 10 pupils average 40; 20 average 70. Combined average = (10×40 + 20×70)/30 = 60, not the simple average 55.

## Patterns, diagrams and evidence

18. **Number pattern:** 1, 4, 9, 16 → squares 1², 2², 3², 4², so 25 is a plausible next term. A finite pattern may fit other rules too.
19. **Direction:** face north, turn right → east; turn right again → south. Draw arrows if there are several turns.
20. **Order:** A is taller than B and B is taller than C; A is taller than C. This depends on the statements being true and on the same comparison.
21. **Conditional trap:** “If a figure is a square, it is a rectangle.” The converse is false: a 3×5 rectangle is not a square.
22. **Counterexample:** “All birds can fly” is false; an ostrich is a bird that cannot fly.
23. **Probability:** bag has 2 red, 3 blue. If balls are equally likely, first red chance = 2/5. If one red is removed and not replaced, next red chance = 1/4.
24. **Reading tables:** 12 correct out of 15 = 80%; 18 correct out of 30 = 60%. Larger raw score need not mean a larger percentage.
25. **Graph scale:** if an axis starts at 90 rather than zero, bars showing 95 and 100 can visually exaggerate the difference. Read labels and values.
26. **Test design:** if fertiliser and water both differ between two plants, you cannot confidently attribute the entire difference to fertiliser alone.

## Try before looking

- A 20% discount leaves ₹400. Original price? **₹500**, because ₹400 is 80%.
- A car goes 60 km in 1 h and 40 km in 1 h. Average speed? **50 km/h**.
- A tap fills 1/5 of a tank per hour. Time for a full empty tank? **5 hours**, under the stated constant-rate assumptions.
