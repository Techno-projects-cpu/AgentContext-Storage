# Knowledge upgrade: understand, don't just memorise 🧠

**How to use:** Cover the explanation and give an example aloud. Telugu words are quick glosses; terminology may vary by textbook.

## Biology and health (జీవశాస్త్రం)

1. **Cell (కణం):** basic structural and functional unit of life. A tissue contains similar cells working together; organs contain multiple tissues.
2. **Nucleus (కేంద్రకం):** contains most DNA in human cells. Mature mammalian red blood cells lack a nucleus—do not assume all cells have one.
3. **Mitochondria:** release usable energy from food during cellular respiration; plants have mitochondria too.
4. **Chloroplast (హరిత రేణువు):** photosynthesis converts light energy to chemical energy; carbon dioxide and water help make sugars, releasing oxygen. Respiration also occurs in plants, day and night.
5. **Stomata (పత్రరంధ్రాలు):** pores mainly on leaves allow gas exchange and water-vapour loss; guard cells regulate opening.
6. **Xylem:** transports water and minerals mainly upwards from roots. **Phloem:** transports sugars between sources and growing or storage areas.
7. **Transpiration (భాష్పోత్సేకం):** loss of water vapour from aerial plant parts; supports water movement and can cool leaves.
8. **Arteries** carry blood away from the heart; **veins** carry it toward the heart. Oxygen content has exceptions: pulmonary artery carries deoxygenated blood to lungs.
9. **Alveoli (వాయుకోశాలు):** tiny lung air sacs with large surface area and thin walls for oxygen and carbon dioxide exchange.
10. **Red blood cells:** haemoglobin helps carry oxygen. White blood cells help defend against infection; platelets help clotting.
11. **Vaccines:** train immune response before a future encounter. **Antibiotics** act against some bacteria, not viruses like those causing flu; use only as medically advised.
12. **Balanced diet:** diverse food provides nutrients. Iron deficiency can cause anaemia; iodine deficiency can impair thyroid function; vitamin D supports calcium absorption and bones.
13. **Food chains:** producers capture energy; herbivores eat plants; carnivores eat animals. Energy transfers are inefficient and energy does not cycle like matter.
14. **Decomposers (విఘటకాలు):** fungi and bacteria break down dead matter and return nutrients to ecosystems.
15. **Biodiversity:** variety of life at genetic, species and ecosystem levels. Protect habitats as well as individual organisms.

## Physical science (భౌతిక శాస్త్రం)

16. **Speed = distance/time.** A 120 km journey in 3 h averages 40 km/h. Average speed uses total distance divided by total time.
17. **Force (బలం)** can change motion or shape. Inertia is resistance to change in velocity; a seat belt restrains you when a vehicle stops.
18. **Mass and weight:** mass is measured in kg; weight is gravitational force, measured in newtons. On the Moon weight changes; mass remains essentially unchanged.
19. **Pressure = force/area:** a sharp knife concentrates force on a smaller area. Liquid pressure increases with depth in the same liquid.
20. **Heat (ఉష్ణం)** transfers because of temperature difference. Conduction dominates in a metal spoon, convection in circulating fluids, radiation can travel through a vacuum.
21. **Evaporation cools:** higher-energy molecules preferentially escape. Sweat cools the body when it evaporates; humidity can slow evaporation.
22. **Reflection:** angle of incidence equals angle of reflection, measured from the normal (a perpendicular), not from the mirror surface.
23. **Refraction (వక్రీభవనం):** light changes direction when its speed changes on entering another medium at an angle; a straw in water looks bent.
24. **Convex lens:** converges parallel rays in air; used in magnifiers. **Concave lens:** diverges parallel rays; commonly used for short-sightedness correction.
25. **Sound:** needs a material medium, so it cannot travel through a vacuum. Pitch relates to frequency; loudness generally increases with amplitude.
26. **Current (విద్యుత్ ప్రవాహం):** rate of charge flow in amperes. **Voltage:** energy transferred per unit charge, in volts. **Resistance:** opposition to current, in ohms.
27. **Ohm's law:** for an ohmic conductor under constant physical conditions, V = IR. 6 V across 3 Ω gives 2 A. Power P = VI: 6 V × 2 A = 12 W.
28. **Electrical energy:** 100 W for 5 h = 500 Wh = 0.5 kWh. Household electricity bills generally use kWh, not kW.
29. **Series:** one current path; a break stops the whole simple circuit. **Parallel:** branches share the supply voltage; one lamp can work when another branch breaks.
30. **Fuse:** melts when excessive current heats it; place correctly in the live path, using an appropriate rating. Never bypass it or experiment with mains.

## Chemistry, Earth and environment

31. **Elements** contain one type of atom; compounds contain chemically combined elements in fixed proportions; mixtures can have variable composition.
32. **Physical change:** ice melting changes state; **chemical change:** iron rusting makes new substances. Signs alone, such as colour change, are clues rather than infallible proof.
33. **Acid/base:** acidic solutions have pH below 7, basic above 7, neutral 7 at 25°C. Never taste an unknown substance to identify it.
34. **Rusting:** iron reacts in the presence of oxygen and water; paint or galvanising can protect it by reducing exposure.
35. **Water cycle:** evaporation and transpiration supply vapour; cooling may cause condensation, followed by precipitation.
36. **Ozone layer:** stratospheric ozone absorbs much harmful ultraviolet radiation. Ground-level ozone, however, is an air pollutant.
37. **Greenhouse effect:** gases absorb and re-emit infrared radiation, warming Earth. Enhanced greenhouse gas concentrations contribute to climate change; do not confuse this with the ozone hole.
38. **Solar and lunar eclipses:** a solar eclipse involves the Moon between Earth and Sun; a lunar eclipse involves Earth between Sun and Moon. Never view the Sun directly or through unfiltered optics.
39. **Moon phases:** changing visible sunlit portion from Earth; they are not normally caused by Earth's shadow.
40. **Safe drinking water:** filtration can remove particles; boiling can kill many disease-causing organisms, but does not reliably remove chemical contamination. Follow local health guidance.

## Easy maths and reasoning

41. **Percent:** 25% of 200 = 50. A rise from 20 to 40 is a **100% increase**; 40 is **200% of** 20. Always identify the base.
42. **Ratio:** 2:3 shared among 25 means 5 parts, 5 each, giving 10 and 15.
43. **Mean:** (4 + 6 + 8)/3 = 6. Averages can hide an unusually high or low value.
44. **Probability:** a fair die has **three** even results: 2, 4 and 6. Probability = 3/6 = 1/2. Assumes six equally likely outcomes.
45. **Scientific method:** compare fairly, control relevant factors, measure and repeat. A correlation by itself does not establish causation.
46. **Graph trap:** read axis labels, units and scale before comparing bars. A truncated axis can exaggerate visual differences.
47. **Classification:** all squares are rectangles, but not all rectangles are squares. One example cannot prove a general claim.
48. **Units:** 1 km = 1000 m; 1 L = 1000 mL; 1 hour = 60 min. Convert before calculating.
