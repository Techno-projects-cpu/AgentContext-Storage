# Team activity and experiment lab ⚗️

**Practice ideas only:** the actual tasks, scoring, materials and timing are unknown. Work with a teacher; do not use mains electricity, flames, sharp tools, unknown chemicals, bodily fluids or cultured microbes. Never taste a test material or look directly at the Sun.

## Answer any experiment question in six steps

1. **Question:** what are we testing? 2. **Prediction:** what do we expect, and why? 3. **Independent variable:** what one factor do we deliberately change? 4. **Dependent variable:** what do we measure? 5. **Controls:** what must stay the same? 6. **Conclusion:** report what the measurements show, uncertainty and a possible improvement.

### 1. Paper helicopter: does blade length affect fall time?

- Teacher helps make two similar paper helicopters, changing only blade length; same paper, approximate mass and drop height. If changing blade length also changes mass or blade area, acknowledge the confound and try to control it.
- Drop indoors from a safe reachable height; time several drops of each. Record individual times and mean. Avoid standing on furniture.
- Longer or shorter time is a **result**, not something to assume in advance. Air resistance, release technique and reaction time matter.

### 2. Dissolving sugar: does stirring matter?

- Two clear cups of same volume and temperature of clean water; equal sugar, same cup shape and grain size. Stir one consistently and leave the other still. Record time until visible sugar disappears, repeating if possible.
- Independent variable: stirring. Dependent variable: time to dissolve. Don't taste; explain this tests **rate**, not necessarily the final amount that can dissolve at equilibrium.

### 3. Paper towel absorption: a fair comparison

- Cut equal-sized pieces of two towel brands. Dip them for the same time in equal volumes of water, let drip for the same interval, and compare mass gained with a scale if available. Repeat with fresh pieces.
- Keep area and method constant; thickness and composition differ between brands, so the test compares products, not which single material property caused the difference. Do not infer a result before testing.

### 4. Shadow changes: observation, not a home eclipse experiment

- With a teacher, place a stick vertically outdoors and mark the tip of its shadow at two times. Keep the stick fixed and record time. Never look directly at the Sun. Describe change in length and direction; Earth's rotation changes the Sun's apparent position.
- If weather or location changes, discuss uncertainty. Different dates and latitudes change the pattern.

### 5. Closed-loop circuit: teacher-supervised low-voltage kit only

- Use a commercial low-voltage battery, holder, switch and suitable miniature lamp with insulated leads; follow kit instructions. A closed loop lights a working lamp; an open switch interrupts current.
- Never short battery terminals, connect to household sockets, use damaged/hot batteries, or handle exposed wires. Disconnect after the demonstration. For series vs parallel, explain from a diagram if no safe kit exists.

## Sample quiz/activity rounds

**One-minute explanation relay:** Each teammate explains one step: (1) plants make sugars using light, (2) organisms use food in respiration, (3) energy transfers through a food chain. Correct each other respectfully. Plants both photosynthesise and respire.

**Sort and justify:** Assign each item to living, once-living or non-living: a living leaf on a plant, dry wood, rock, living ant. Borderline cases need definitions; don't treat an isolated leaf as necessarily dead immediately.

**Data task:** Three times to dissolve sugar are 12, 10 and 11 seconds: mean = 11 seconds. A second method averages 8 seconds. The second was faster **in this small sample**, not proven universally better. Ask about sample size and conditions.

**Visual relay:** Sketch a stoma, lens, circuit and food chain; another person identifies each and gives one function. Diagrams are practice, not official images.

**Team protocol:** listen to the whole clue, agree on key term and evidence, avoid interrupting, and choose one spokesperson if organisers permit. Rotate knowledge areas for practice; don't assume roles are official.

## Before leaving: ask organisers

- How many students per team? Is the preliminary individual or team-based and how is its score calculated?
- Exact duration, languages, question formats, negative marking, ties and category selection?
- Allowed materials, nature/safety rules of activities, how much each round counts and reporting time?
