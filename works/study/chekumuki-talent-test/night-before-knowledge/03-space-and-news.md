## ★ Indian missions: match the purpose

- **Chandrayaan-1:** Indian lunar mission launched in 2008; instruments contributed evidence of water/hydroxyl on the Moon.
- **Chandrayaan-2:** lunar orbiter plus attempted lander/rover; the lander's 2019 landing attempt did not achieve the planned soft landing. Do not claim the whole mission simply vanished; the orbiter continued its work.
- **Chandrayaan-3:** lander **Vikram** and rover **Pragyan**; successful lunar soft landing on **23 August 2023** near the Moon's south polar region.
- **Mangalyaan / Mars Orbiter Mission:** studied Mars from orbit; not a Mars landing.
- **Aditya-L1:** solar observatory studying the Sun from an orbit around the Sun–Earth L1 region; **it does not land on the Sun**.
- **AstroSat:** astronomy observatory that studies celestial objects in multiple wavelength bands.
- **Gaganyaan:** India's human-spaceflight programme; confirm current mission status before stating crewed launch dates.
- [ISRO: Chandrayaan-3](https://www.isro.gov.in/Chandrayaan3.html) · [Aditya-L1](https://www.isro.gov.in/Aditya_L1.html) · [AstroSat](https://www.isro.gov.in/AstroSat.html).

## ★ Recent space achievements

- **SpaDeX:** ISRO's space docking experiment; two satellites docked on **16 January 2025** and undocked on **13 March 2025**. Docking helps future spacecraft join in orbit; it is not a Moon landing.
- **NISAR:** joint **NASA–ISRO** Earth-observation mission, launched **30 July 2025**. Uses radar bands including NASA's L-band and ISRO's S-band to study changes on Earth.
- **Shubhanshu Shukla:** flew to the International Space Station on **Axiom-4 in 2025**, according to ISRO. Distinguish this from a Gaganyaan mission.
- **Rakesh Sharma:** travelled to space in **1984**; do not confuse him with the first Indian to visit the ISS.
- [ISRO: 2025 achievements](https://www.isro.gov.in/Achievements_Department_of_Space_2025.html) · [ISRO: SpaDeX undocking](https://www.isro.gov.in/spadex_undocking_successful.html) · [ISRO: 2025 launch report](https://www.isro.gov.in/Indian_Space_Situational_Awareness_Report_2025.html).

## The sky without confusion

- A **star** produces energy through nuclear fusion; the Sun is a star.
- A **planet** orbits a star and is normally seen by reflected sunlight; planets also emit thermal radiation.
- A **moon / natural satellite** orbits a planet; Earth's Moon reflects sunlight.
- A **comet nucleus** contains ice, dust and other material; its tail forms as it approaches the Sun and generally points away from the Sun due to solar radiation/wind.
- An **asteroid** is a smaller rocky or metallic body orbiting the Sun; not every small body fits one simple composition.
- A **meteor** is the visible streak when a space particle enters an atmosphere; a surviving fragment on the ground is a **meteorite**.
- A **light-year** is a distance, not a time: distance light travels in vacuum in a year. A light-day is also a distance.
- **Gravity** acts in space too. Astronauts in orbit feel weightless because they and the spacecraft are in continuous free fall around Earth—not because gravity is absent.

## Eclipses, phases and seasons

- **Solar eclipse:** Moon comes between Sun and Earth. It occurs only with a suitable alignment near a new moon.
- **Lunar eclipse:** Earth lies between Sun and Moon. It occurs only with a suitable alignment near a full moon.
- We do not see an eclipse every month because the Moon's orbit is tilted relative to Earth's orbital plane and alignment is not always exact.
- **Moon phases:** the Sun always illuminates half the Moon approximately; our viewing angle changes how much of that lit half we see.
- **Seasons:** Earth's axial tilt and orbital motion change sunlight angle and day length. Northern summer is not simply caused by Earth being closer to the Sun.
- **Never look directly at the Sun.** Use only appropriate certified solar-viewing equipment with expert guidance; ordinary sunglasses are not safe solar filters.

## Orbits and signals

- A **satellite** can be natural (Moon) or artificial (communication spacecraft).
- An orbit involves continuous motion while gravity changes the direction of that motion.
- A **geostationary** satellite is in a circular equatorial orbit matching Earth's rotation and appears fixed over a point; not every satellite is geostationary.
- A **rocket** works by pushing exhaust one way; the exhaust pushes the rocket the other way. It does not need air to push against.
- Radio signals travel at light speed in vacuum; greater distance means longer one-way communication time.

## Quick connections

- A solar satellite studies the **Sun**, not solar-system planets exclusively.
- A Mars **orbiter** orbits; a Mars **lander** reaches the surface. Name the mission and its actual achievement.
- **Neutrino ≠ neutron:** neutrinos are elementary particles; neutrons are composite particles found in atomic nuclei.
- An astronaut's floating is **apparent weightlessness**, not proof that gravitational force is zero.
