## Cells and microscopic life

- **Cell (కణం):** basic living unit. Unicellular organisms have one cell; multicellular organisms have many.
- **Cell membrane:** selective boundary controlling movement; present in plant and animal cells.
- **Cell wall:** external support; plant walls contain cellulose. Animal cells lack a cell wall.
- **Cytoplasm:** region where many reactions occur; not an empty bag of water.
- **Nucleus:** contains most DNA in plant/animal cells; bacteria have DNA but no membrane-bound nucleus.
- **Mitochondria:** help produce usable cellular energy through respiration. Plants have mitochondria too.
- **Ribosomes:** sites of protein synthesis; bacteria have them too.
- **Chloroplasts:** sites of photosynthesis in plant cells that contain them; many root cells do not have them.
- **Vacuole:** storage compartment; many mature plant cells have a large central vacuole.
- **Cell → tissue → organ → organ system → organism.** Similar cells can work together as a tissue.
- **Microscope:** increases apparent size; a scale bar can help estimate actual size. A resized image can invalidate an unadjusted magnification label.

## DNA and reproduction

- **DNA (డీఎన్‌ఏ)** stores genetic information; a **gene (జన్యువు)** is a DNA segment with information for a functional product.
- DNA → RNA → protein is a useful basic pathway; some genes produce functional RNA instead of a protein.
- A **chromosome** contains DNA and associated proteins; chromosome numbers differ among species.
- **Mutation** is a change in genetic material. Effects vary: harmful, beneficial or neutral depending on context.
- **Inherited traits** are influenced by genes; environment also affects many characteristics.
- **Sexual reproduction** involves fusion of gametes; it often generates variation.
- **Asexual reproduction** can produce offspring from one parent without gamete fusion; examples include budding in yeast and vegetative propagation from potato tubers.
- A **flower** may have male and female structures in one flower or separate flowers depending on species; watermelon bears separate male and female flowers.
- **Pollination** is transfer of pollen; **fertilisation** is fusion of gametes. These are not the same event.

## Plants: food, transport and response

- **Photosynthesis:** light energy helps make sugars using CO₂ and water; oxygen released is derived from water.
- Photosynthesis needs light; **respiration occurs day and night** in plants.
- **Stomata** are openings involved in gas exchange and water-vapour loss, mostly in leaves.
- **Xylem:** mainly water and dissolved minerals. **Phloem:** sugars and other organic substances.
- **Root hairs** increase area for absorption; osmosis and other transport processes are involved.
- **Transpiration:** water loss as vapour, mainly through stomata; wind, humidity, temperature and light affect rate.
- **Phototropism:** directional plant growth response to light. **Gravitropism:** growth response to gravity.
- Touch-me-not leaves fold after touch; this shows **stimulus response**, not a human-like nervous system.
- **Osmosis:** net water movement through a selectively permeable membrane, often towards a more concentrated solution.
- **Diffusion:** net movement from higher to lower concentration due to particle motion; not all transport is diffusion.
- **Legume rotation:** bacteria associated with legumes can fix nitrogen, supporting soil fertility; crop management still matters.

## Human body

- **Digestion** breaks food into absorbable molecules; digestion differs from absorption.
- **Carbohydrates** and fats can provide energy; proteins have structural and functional roles. A balanced diet needs variety.
- **Enzyme:** biological catalyst; shape, temperature and pH affect activity. Very high heat can denature many enzymes.
- **Lungs:** oxygen and carbon dioxide exchange at alveoli; the heart circulates blood.
- **Haemoglobin in red blood cells** carries most blood oxygen; white blood cells have immune roles; platelets help clotting.
- **Arteries** carry blood away from heart; **veins** towards it. Pulmonary arteries carry oxygen-poor blood to lungs; pulmonary veins carry oxygen-rich blood to heart.
- **Heart rate and breathing often rise during exercise** as active muscles' demands and CO₂ production increase.
- **Neurons:** dendrites typically receive inputs; an axon carries signals away from the cell body.
- **Reflex:** rapid automatic response through a neural pathway. Knee jerk is a spinal **somatic** reflex, not an autonomic reflex.
- **Cerebellum:** coordination and balance; **hypothalamus:** important in temperature regulation.
- **Kidneys** filter blood and help regulate water, salts and waste; a nephron is a functional unit.
- **Hormones** are chemical messengers; the pituitary influences several glands but not every gland is simply controlled by it.

## Microbes, immunity and safe decisions

- **Bacteria** are cells; some useful, some harmful. Curd and nutrient cycling involve useful bacteria.
- **Viruses** reproduce using host cells. Influenza is viral; antibiotics do **not** treat the virus itself.
- **Fungi:** yeast and mushrooms are examples; yeast can produce CO₂ during fermentation.
- **Antibiotic resistance:** microorganisms survive medicines that would otherwise work; it is not the human body becoming resistant.
- **Vaccine:** stimulates the immune system. **Antiserum:** provides preformed antibodies; it gives passive protection.
- Handwashing with soap and safe water can reduce infection risk; clear water is not automatically safe.
- Menstruation is normal, not contagious, and does not justify exclusion from school.
- Do not diagnose or change medication based on a quiz or viral post; consult a qualified professional.
- [WHO: antimicrobial resistance](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/antimicrobial-resistance).

## Environment and ecology

- **Producer:** makes organic food using an energy source, usually sunlight. **Consumer:** obtains food from other organisms. **Decomposer:** breaks down dead matter and recycles nutrients.
- In grass → grasshopper → frog → snake, a sharp frog decline **may** reduce predation on grasshoppers and food for snakes. Ecosystems have other interactions too.
- Energy transfer between feeding levels is inefficient; organisms use energy and disperse some as heat.
- **Food chain** is one route; a **food web** connects many routes.
- Untreated sewage can deplete dissolved oxygen because microorganisms decompose organic material. Industrial waste can also contain harmful chemicals.
- **Biodegradable:** can be broken down by biological processes under suitable conditions; it does not mean instant or harmless in every setting.
- Suitable food scraps can be composted; keep glass, batteries and hazardous material out.
- **Biodiversity** includes variety within species, between species and of ecosystems.
- **Greenhouse effect:** certain gases absorb and re-emit infrared radiation; increased greenhouse-gas concentrations contribute to long-term warming.
- One hot or cold day is **weather**, not by itself a measure of long-term **climate**.
- Renewable energy helps reduce some emissions, but every technology has environmental impacts to manage responsibly.

## Rapid questions

- Why are leaves green? **Chlorophyll absorbs some wavelengths and reflects/transmits more green light.**
- Why can a steel ship float? **Its overall shape displaces enough water for buoyant force to balance weight.**
- Why does a raisin swell? **Net water enters by osmosis.**
- What gas makes yeast dough rise? **Carbon dioxide.**
- Do plants respire at night? **Yes.**
- Are all bacteria harmful? **No.**
