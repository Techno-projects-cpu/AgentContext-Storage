# ★ Easy maths, reasoning and scientific temper 🧩

[Start](README.md) · [Physics/chemistry](05-physics-chemistry.md) · [40-question recall](07-quiz-and-traps.md)

You do **not** need to be a maths Olympiad champion. Read carefully, write units, take one step at a time, and check whether your result makes sense.

## Percentage traps

- **Percentage of a quantity:** 25% of 240 = 0.25 × 240 = **60**.
- **Increase %:** (new − original)/original × 100.
- Example: 20 → 40: increase = 20; 20/20 × 100 = **100% increase**. The final value is **200% of the original**.
- **Decrease %:** (original − new)/original × 100. 80 → 60: decrease 20/80 × 100 = **25%**.
- **Successive discounts:** ₹1,000 less 50% = ₹500; then 60% of ₹500 is ₹300 off; final **₹200**. Don't add discount percentages.
- **Order of operations:** √(16%) = √0.16 = 0.4 = **40%**. But (√16)% = **4%**. If a printed radical bar is unclear, ask the teacher.
- A ratio 4:25 is 4/25 × 100 = **16%**.

## Average, ratios, charts

- **Average = sum / number.** Five marks average 18 → sum 90. If four marks total 72, fifth = **18**.
- 12, 16, 20, 24 average = 72/4 = **18**.
- Ratio 3:5, total 96: 8 equal parts → 12 per part → quantities **36 and 60**.
- A bar rises from 20 to 30: increase is **50%**; final is **150% of** the original.
- In a graph, read **title, axes, units, scale, date and what is being compared** before looking for an answer.
- Do not infer exact values for days not shown in a graph.

## Intervals and time

- First dose at time zero, then every 6 h, 15 total doses: **14 intervals × 6 = 84 h**. This is a maths example, not medical advice.
- Five fence posts equally spaced make **four gaps**. Off-by-one errors are common.
- A journey of 60 km at 60 km/h and 60 km at 30 km/h takes 1 + 2 = 3 h. Average is **40 km/h**.

## Number patterns, geometry and counting

- Positive squares to 100: **1, 4, 9, 16, 25, 36, 49, 64, 81, 100**: 10 values.
- If M = n², next square = (n + 1)² = **M + 2√M + 1**, for a nonnegative integer n.
- For positive integers a and b, **GCD × LCM = a × b**. Given GCD 6, LCM 120 and sum 54, pair 24 and 30 → difference 6.
- A triangle's angles sum to **180°** in plane geometry.
- Adjacent angles on a straight line sum to 180°; their internal bisectors are separated by **90°**.
- If DE is parallel to BC in triangle ABC, with D on AB and E on AC, **AD/DB = AE/EC** when lengths are nonzero.
- Example AD = 9, DB = 21, AE = 6 → 9/21 = 6/EC → **EC = 14**.
- The centroid of a triangle divides it into three equal-area triangles when joined to the vertices. Total area 60 → each is **20**.
- A square with one vertical and one horizontal centre line contains **4 little squares + 1 large = 5 squares**.
- A Venn diagram: count a shared region only once. 18 play cricket, 12 volleyball, 5 both → at least one = 18 + 12 − 5 = **25**.
- **Probability:** favourable outcomes / equally likely outcomes. A fair die: even and greater than 3 = {4, 6} → 2/6 = **1/3**.
- Two independent fair coin heads: 1/2 × 1/2 = **1/4**.

## Codes and matching

- Alphabet-opposite code: A ↔ Z, B ↔ Y, C ↔ X, etc. CAT → **XZG**.
- In a matching question, write each correct pair before reading the answer codes. Watch for **swapped numbers**.
- Scientifically important pairings: Sarabhai—space; Bhabha—nuclear programme; Swaminathan—Green Revolution; J. C. Bose—plant physiology/wireless.
- Missions: Chandrayaan-3—Moon; Mangalyaan—Mars; Aditya-L1—Sun; AstroSat—astronomy.

## Thinking like a scientist

- **Observation:** what was seen/measured. **Inference:** an interpretation of what it might mean.
- **Hypothesis:** a testable proposed explanation, not automatically a proven fact.
- **Independent variable:** deliberately changed. **Dependent variable:** measured response.
- **Controlled variables:** comparable conditions such as species, water, soil and time when testing light effects on growth.
- **Control group:** comparison that helps isolate an effect; the correct control depends on the question.
- **Replicates:** multiple comparable samples help reveal natural variation; they do not fix every bias.
- If the fertilised group also gets more water, **two factors changed**. Water could explain the result.
- **Correlation ≠ causation.** Ice-cream sales and swimming can both rise in summer because of warm weather; one does not by itself prove it causes the other.
- Ask: who measured it, how many samples, what control, could another factor explain it, and can it be reproduced?
- A popular claim is not proof; a single failed trial also does not prove an effect is impossible under all conditions.
- Distinguish “**some evidence supports X**” from “**X is proven for every case**.”

## Safety, civic sense and digital information

- During earthquake shaking: **drop, cover and hold on** where appropriate; avoid lifts and windows and follow local instructions.
- Suspected prize scam: **do not pay or share OTP, PIN, password or personal bank details**. Verify using an independently obtained official contact.
- For Indian financial cyber-fraud, contact the bank promptly and report via **1930** or [cybercrime.gov.in](https://cybercrime.gov.in/).
- **Constitution Article 51A(h):** develop scientific temper, humanism and spirit of inquiry and reform. [Official Constitution site](https://legislative.gov.in/constitution-of-india/).
- **Election Commission of India** conducts elections to Parliament, state legislatures and the offices of President and Vice President; local-body election arrangements involve state election commissions.
- Menstruation is normal; include students and support access to education.
- Treat claims about solar eclipses, health cures or “magic” with evidence and empathy—not ridicule or risky experiments.

## Final decision method

1. What exactly is asked: **increase, final value, NOT, or most likely**?
2. Do a quick estimate and keep units.
3. Check assumptions and figure labels.
4. Eliminate wrong options; if none works, mark the ambiguity for discussion.
5. Don't treat a questionable printed option or our unofficial working as an official key.
