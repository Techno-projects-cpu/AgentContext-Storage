# Science and easy maths: understand the connection 🔬

Cover the explanation, say the answer, then give an everyday example. Telugu glosses are aids, not extra facts to memorise.

## Biology: the body, plants and the environment

1. **Cell → tissue → organ → organ system:** cells form tissues; tissues form organs; organs cooperate in systems. Example: muscle cells → muscle tissue → heart → circulatory system.
2. **Nucleus (కేంద్రకం):** in most human cells it contains most of the cell's DNA. Mature human red blood cells have no nucleus.
3. **DNA:** carries genetic information. A gene is a segment of DNA with a role in producing a functional product.
4. **Cell membrane:** controls movement of substances into and out of cells. Plant cells also have a cell wall outside the membrane.
5. **Mitochondria:** participate in releasing usable energy from food during respiration; plant cells have them too.
6. **Chlorophyll:** green pigment that helps plants absorb light energy for photosynthesis. Green leaves exchange gases through stomata.
7. **Xylem vs phloem:** xylem carries water and minerals mainly upward; phloem transports sugars from sources to places that use or store them.
8. **Transpiration:** water vapour escapes from plant surfaces, largely through stomata; it can help cool leaves and move water through the plant.
9. **Respiration vs breathing:** breathing moves air; cellular respiration releases energy in cells. Plants respire day and night.
10. **Lungs:** alveoli are tiny air sacs with thin walls where gases exchange between air and blood.
11. **Blood:** haemoglobin in red blood cells helps transport oxygen; white blood cells help defend against infection; platelets help clot blood.
12. **Heart:** arteries carry blood away from the heart, veins toward it. The pulmonary artery carries oxygen-poor blood to the lungs.
13. **Digestion:** the small intestine absorbs most digested nutrients. Teeth and digestive juices help break food down.
14. **Kidneys:** filter blood and help remove waste in urine. They also help regulate water and salts.
15. **Reflex:** a rapid automatic response; the knee-jerk reflex uses a spinal reflex arc and somatic motor neurons, not a simple example of an autonomic reflex.
16. **Vaccines:** train immune responses to particular infections. Antibiotics treat some bacterial infections, not viral influenza.
17. **Food chains:** producer → herbivore → carnivore. Arrows show the direction of energy transfer; energy is lost at each transfer.
18. **Decomposition:** fungi and bacteria can break down dead material and return nutrients to soil; not all microbes are harmful.
19. **Pollination vs fertilisation:** pollination transfers pollen to a flower's stigma; fertilisation is fusion of reproductive cells.
20. **Biodiversity:** protecting habitats helps protect the variety of living things. Whales are mammals that breathe air, despite living in water.

## Physics: use it in real life

21. **Inertia (జడత్వం):** a body resists a change in motion. In a suddenly braking bus, passengers tend to keep moving forward.
22. **Force:** a push or pull can change velocity or shape. Friction can help shoes grip the floor but also wear down surfaces.
23. **Mass vs weight:** mass is measured in kg; weight is gravitational force measured in newtons. Moon gravity gives an object less weight, not less mass.
24. **Pressure = force/area:** a sharp needle can make greater pressure than a blunt tip for the same applied force.
25. **Buoyancy:** water exerts an upward force on submerged objects. A floating ship displaces enough water for that force to balance its weight.
26. **Sound:** needs a material medium. Frequency relates to pitch; amplitude generally relates to loudness.
27. **Echo:** sound reflects from a surface and returns after a delay. The distance to the reflecting wall is **half** the sound's total round-trip distance.
28. **Light reflection:** incidence angle equals reflection angle, both measured from the normal (perpendicular to the reflecting surface).
29. **Refraction:** light changes speed and may bend at a boundary. A pencil in water can look bent. A convex lens converges parallel rays in air; a concave lens diverges them.
30. **Heat transfer:** conduction through a spoon, convection in circulating liquids or gases, radiation from the Sun through space.
31. **Evaporation:** higher-energy molecules escape a liquid's surface; evaporation can cool the remaining liquid and the skin underneath.
32. **Electric current:** rate of flow of charge, in amperes; voltage is energy transferred per unit charge, in volts; resistance is measured in ohms.
33. **Ohm's law:** V = IR for an ohmic conductor under constant conditions. A 6 V battery across 3 Ω gives 2 A in the idealised calculation.
34. **Power and energy:** P = VI. A device drawing 100 W for 2 h uses 200 Wh = 0.2 kWh; electricity bills use energy, not merely power.
35. **Series vs parallel:** an open switch breaks a simple series loop; in parallel branches, one bulb can continue when another branch is open.
36. **Safety:** never use household mains or short battery terminals in a practice experiment; use teacher-approved low-voltage kits only.

## Chemistry, Earth and scientific thinking

37. **Element:** one type of atom; **compound:** elements chemically combined in a fixed proportion; **mixture:** components physically mixed in variable proportions.
38. **Physical vs chemical change:** melting ice is physical; rusting iron is chemical. A burning candle shows melting wax and chemical combustion together.
39. **Rusting:** iron needs oxygen and moisture to rust; paint or a protective coating helps reduce contact.
40. **Acids and bases:** blue litmus turns red in acid; red litmus turns blue in base. Do not taste chemicals.
41. **Filtration:** separates insoluble sand from water, but does not remove dissolved salt. Evaporation can recover salt from saltwater.
42. **Water purification:** boiling kills many disease-causing organisms but does not reliably remove dissolved chemicals; follow local health guidance.
43. **Weather vs climate:** weather means conditions over a short period; climate describes patterns over a long period.
44. **Seasons:** mainly result from Earth's tilted axis and its orbit, not from a simple change in distance from the Sun.
45. **Moon phases:** we see changing portions of the Moon's sunlit half; Earth's shadow causes lunar eclipses, not ordinary monthly phases.
46. **Ozone vs greenhouse effect:** stratospheric ozone absorbs much ultraviolet light; increasing greenhouse gases contribute to warming. These are different issues.
47. **C. V. Raman:** Nobel Prize in Physics 1930 for work on scattering of light and the Raman effect. [Nobel source](https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/physics/1930/raman/facts/).
48. **Fair test:** change one factor you want to test; keep other relevant conditions similar; measure, repeat, and report uncertainty.
49. **Correlation is not proof of cause:** ice-cream sales and temperatures can rise together; hot weather can influence both.
50. **Evidence:** a repeated claim or confident speaker is not by itself proof. Prefer observations and credible sources; revise when evidence changes.

## Maths: easy steps, no Olympiad tricks

51. **Percent:** 25% of 120 = 120 ÷ 4 = 30. 10% of ₹450 = ₹45.
52. **Increase trap:** from 20 to 40 the increase is 20/20 × 100 = 100%. The final number is 200% *of* the original.
53. **Discount:** ₹500 less 20% = ₹500 − ₹100 = ₹400.
54. **Average:** (6 + 8 + 10) ÷ 3 = 8. Use all the values and the correct number of values.
55. **Speed:** 120 km in 3 h = 40 km/h. For multiple stages, average speed = total distance / total time.
56. **Ratio:** split 35 in 2:3 → 5 parts → 7 each → 14 and 21.
57. **Fraction:** 3/4 of 80 = 80 ÷ 4 × 3 = 60.
58. **Area and perimeter:** 8 cm × 5 cm rectangle: area 40 cm²; perimeter 2(8 + 5) = 26 cm.
59. **Probability:** on a fair six-sided die, chance of an even number = 3/6 = 1/2; 2, 4 and 6 are even.
60. **Graph reading:** inspect title, axes, units and scale. A truncated axis can exaggerate differences visually.
