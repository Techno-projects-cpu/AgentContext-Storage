# 1. From election announcement to oath

This describes a typical **contested direct election** to a Lok Sabha or State Assembly constituency. A by-election fills a casual vacancy. Individual notifications, court orders and ECI directions govern actual dates and exceptions. Roll work, security planning and voter education happen well before announcement.

## A. Before and at announcement

1. **Preparation / electoral roll (ఓటర్ల జాబితా):** ECI, CEOs, DEOs, EROs and BLOs prepare/revise rolls, arrange polling stations and train staff. Generally an eligible citizen must be 18 by a qualifying date, ordinarily resident and enrolled. Four qualifying dates: 1 January, 1 April, 1 July, 1 October. A voter ID alone does not substitute for a name on the roll; the voter must also meet the applicable identification requirements. Inclusion/correction deadlines around nominations are statutory and instruction-dependent; do not assume an application can be processed on poll day.
2. **Announcement (ఎన్నికల ప్రకటన):** ECI announces the programme: phases, poll days and counting day. The **Model Code of Conduct (MCC)** ordinarily applies from announcement until the election process is completed. This is not the same as the formal election notification. Police, observers, expenditure teams and accessibility arrangements are deployed under instructions.
3. **Formal notification (అధికారిక నోటిఫికేషన్):** The President (Lok Sabha) or Governor (Assembly) issues the statutory notification calling the election on ECI's recommendation. The ECI sets the statutory programme and the RO gives public notice inviting nominations. In multiphase elections separate notifications can apply to different phases.

## B. Candidates: nomination → scrutiny → withdrawal

4. **Nomination (నామినేషన్):** Candidates submit prescribed nomination papers and security deposit to the RO/authorised ARO within the notified window, along with required declarations including Form 26 affidavit on criminal cases, assets, liabilities and educational qualifications. They must meet constitutional and statutory qualifications and avoid disqualifications. A Lok Sabha or Assembly candidate must generally be at least **25**. A recognised party candidate needs **one proposer**; an independent or candidate of a registered unrecognised party needs **ten**, subject to the statutory conditions. Proposers must be electors of the constituency. Maximum **two** constituencies per person at a general election or simultaneous by-elections, subject to §33(7).
5. **Deposit:** Lok Sabha ₹25,000, Assembly ₹10,000; a candidate who is SC or ST pays half, even when contesting a general seat. A losing candidate's deposit is forfeited if votes polled **do not exceed one-sixth of the total valid votes polled by all candidates**. A winner does not lose the deposit on this ground. NOTA is not a candidate and is excluded from this denominator; apply the statutory calculation. One deposit suffices for multiple nomination papers for the same constituency.
6. **Scrutiny (పరిశీలన):** RO examines nominations against requirements, hears objections and decides validity; not a vote count or a general credibility contest. ARO generally cannot scrutinise unless RO is unavoidably prevented. Substantial defects matter; an error that is not substantial is not a ground for rejection. An affidavit does not make a candidate guilty merely because a case is pending.
7. **Withdrawal (ఉపసంహరణ):** Validly nominated candidates may withdraw through the prescribed notice by the deadline. RO publishes the final list of contesting candidates and allocates symbols under applicable rules. If only one valid candidate remains, an uncontested declaration can follow; polling and counting are unnecessary.

**Quiz timing:** Under §30, nomination deadline is normally the **seventh day after notification**, scrutiny normally the next day, and withdrawal normally the **second day after scrutiny**; public holidays and statutory exceptions alter the calculation. Poll is ordinarily at least **14 days after the last date of withdrawal**. Know the sequence rather than inventing a universal calendar.

## C. Campaign and silence

8. **Campaign (ప్రచారం):** Candidates communicate policies and seek votes. The MCC restrains misuse of official machinery, appeals to communal hatred, inducements and certain government announcements; offences and corrupt practices also attract separate legal provisions. Track candidate expenditure; parties have disclosure obligations but do not confuse party spending with the candidate's statutory ceiling.
9. **48-hour silence (నిశ్శబ్ద కాలం):** Under RPA 1951 §126, specified election-related public meetings and display of election matter by television or similar apparatus are prohibited during the **48 hours ending with the hour fixed for conclusion of the poll** in that polling area. The exact legal reach and ECI directions must be checked; don't assume all speech or every media form is governed identically. Exit-poll publication restrictions under §126A operate for the separately notified period, often across all phases.

## D. Poll day (పోలింగ్ రోజు)

10. **Mock poll:** Before actual polling, officials demonstrate zero count, conduct a mock poll in the presence of available polling agents, compare results and VVPAT slips, clear mock votes and seal as directed. ECI handbook prescribes at least **50 mock votes**, including each candidate/NOTA; follow current instructions if the field situation requires adaptation. Mock slips are kept separately as directed.
11. **Inside the booth:** Polling staff verify voter identity and roll entry, apply indelible ink, record signature/thumb impression in **Form 17A**, and enable one vote. Voter privately presses a choice on the ballot unit. The **VVPAT** displays the printed choice briefly (about seven seconds); the slip falls into its sealed box, not the voter's hand. The control unit records the electronic vote. NOTA expresses rejection of listed candidates but is not a candidate and does not automatically trigger a fresh election if it gets the most votes in these elections.
12. **Inclusion and security:** Polling staff follow instructions for accessibility and assistance while preserving secrecy. Eligible categories may use postal ballots/home voting subject to specific rules and notifications. Only eligible electors who are in the queue at the closing hour are allowed to vote after it. In a disruption, the Presiding Officer reports to the RO; ECI determines fresh poll where legally appropriate, not every machine malfunction automatically.
13. **Close and secure:** Presiding Officer closes and seals equipment, prepares **Form 17C Part I** account of votes recorded, gives authenticated copies to polling agents present, completes reports and delivers sealed machines, VVPATs and documents to the reception centre. Machines are stored in guarded **strong rooms**, with candidate/agent observation subject to instructions.

## E. Counting → declaration → oath

14. **Counting (ఓట్ల లెక్కింపు):** RO supervises at the notified centre with counting staff and authorised agents. Postal ballots are taken up first under Rule 54A; electronic counts are taken up in accordance with current ECI instructions (typically after a prescribed interval), and postal counting can continue alongside. EVM results are compared with relevant Form 17C accounts and recorded in rounds. Results from each polling station and postal ballots are consolidated in **Form 20**. Trends are not the official result.
15. **VVPAT and recount:** A mandatory random sample of **five polling stations per Assembly constituency or Assembly segment of a Parliamentary constituency** is generally verified using VVPAT slips under ECI's applicable directions. This is distinct from a candidate/agent's written request for VVPAT slip counting under **Rule 56D**, on which the RO gives a reasoned decision, and from a recount request under **Rule 63**. Resolve these procedures before finalising the result. Follow current instructions and court orders, including any special directions. The 2024 Supreme Court directions on post-result EVM memory verification are a separate process; see current affairs.
16. **Winner and declaration (ఫలితాల ప్రకటన):** Under first-past-the-post, the candidate with the **highest number of valid votes** wins, even without an absolute majority. For a tie between leading candidates, the RO resolves it by lot as prescribed by RPA 1951 §65. After required checks, observer clearance per ECI instructions and decision on recounts, RO declares the result: normally **Form 21C** for a contested general election, **21D** for a contested by-election; **21E** records the return and **Form 22** certifies election. Special and uncontested cases have different forms.
17. **After declaration:** Winning a seat and forming a government are not identical. The Governor or President appoints the Chief Minister or Prime Minister according to constitutional practice; the ministry must enjoy the confidence of the Assembly or Lok Sabha. The elected member takes the prescribed oath or affirmation before taking their seat (Articles 99/188). Election challenges ordinarily proceed through an election petition before the appropriate High Court under RPA 1951, not by restarting a count on social media.

### Micro-example

A, B, C receive 41,000, 39,000 and 20,000 valid candidate votes. A wins with 41%, not an absolute majority. Candidate votes total 100,000: C's 20,000 exceeds one-sixth, so C's deposit is not forfeited under the vote-share rule. If A and B tie for the highest count, the statutory lot decides between them. NOTA is not one of A/B/C.
