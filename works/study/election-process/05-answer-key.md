# 5. Answer key and one-line explanations

Return to [questions](04-practice-mcqs.md).

1. **A — Election Commission of India.** Article 324 gives the ECI election supervision.
2. **D — State Election Commission.** Article 243K provides for a State Election Commission.
3. **C — 324.** Article 324 covers superintendence, direction and control.
4. **B — 325.** Article 325 bars exclusion from roll solely on specified grounds.
5. **A — 326.** Article 326 establishes adult suffrage subject to law.
6. **D — 18.** The 61st Amendment lowered the age from 21 to 18.
7. **C — 61st.** The Constitution (61st Amendment) Act 1988 changed the age.
8. **B — 329.** Article 329 ordinarily channels election challenges into election petitions.
9. **A — President.** The President appoints the PM under the Constitution.
10. **D — 99.** Article 99 concerns parliamentary members' oath or affirmation.
11. **C — 188.** Article 188 concerns state legislators' oath or affirmation.
12. **B — Representation of the People Act 1950.** The 1950 Act covers electoral rolls and registration.
13. **A — Representation of the People Act 1951.** The 1951 Act covers election conduct and disputes.
14. **D — Registration of Electors Rules 1960.** The 1960 rules prescribe roll and registration procedures.
15. **C — Conduct of Elections Rules 1961.** The 1961 rules prescribe election conduct and counting procedures.
16. **B — One CEC and two Election Commissioners.** The Commission has been a three-member body since 1993.
17. **A — Like a Supreme Court judge.** Article 324 gives the CEC a Supreme Court judge-like removal safeguard.
18. **D — Prime Minister.** The PM nominates the Union Cabinet Minister.
19. **C — Six years or age 65 whichever is earlier.** Both duration and age limits apply.
20. **B — President and Vice-President of India.** ECI also conducts presidential and vice-presidential elections.
21. **A — Electoral Registration Officer.** The ERO has the statutory role in roll preparation and maintenance.
22. **D — Presiding Officer.** The Presiding Officer ensures order and a fair poll at the station.
23. **C — Returning Officer.** The RO formally declares the result under the law.
24. **B — District Election Officer.** The DEO coordinates and supervises election work in the district.
25. **A — Chief Electoral Officer.** The CEO works under the ECI's supervision.
26. **D — Local roll verification and voter assistance.** BLO is a field-level roll functionary.
27. **C — A political party at booth level.** A BLA is a party representative, not the BLO.
28. **B — Assists the Electoral Registration Officer.** AERO assists the ERO subject to the ERO's control.
29. **A — ARO assists RO; AERO assists ERO.** The abbreviations relate to separate election and registration functions.
30. **D — District Election Officer.** RPA 1951 section 26 provides for DEO appointment.
31. **C — Candidate's authorised representative at counting.** Counting agents observe and raise permissible objections.
32. **B — Monitors and supports a group of polling stations.** Sector officers coordinate booth-level field support.
33. **A — Independent oversight and reporting under ECI instructions.** Observers monitor elections and may have statutory powers under section 20B.
34. **D — Returning Officer.** The RO examines nominations; ARO scrutiny is restricted.
35. **C — ERO and electoral roll.** The ERO is responsible for constituency electoral-roll work.
36. **B — On ECI announcement of the election schedule.** The MCC ordinarily operates from announcement to completion.
37. **A — Calls the election under the relevant law.** The statutory notification formally calls the election.
38. **D — President.** The President issues the notification under the statutory framework.
39. **C — Governor.** The Governor issues the Assembly election notification.
40. **B — Nomination then scrutiny then withdrawal.** A valid nomination is scrutinised before withdrawal closes.
41. **A — 25.** A Lok Sabha candidate must generally be at least 25.
42. **D — 25.** A Legislative Assembly candidate must generally be at least 25.
43. **C — One.** Section 33 provides the one-proposer rule for recognised-party candidates.
44. **B — Ten.** The statutory rule requires ten proposers for independents and unrecognised-party candidates.
45. **A — The constituency concerned.** Proposers must be electors of the constituency.
46. **D — ₹25,000.** The statutory deposit is ₹25,000 with an SC/ST concession.
47. **C — ₹10,000.** The statutory Assembly deposit is ₹10,000 with an SC/ST concession.
48. **B — ₹12,500.** An SC/ST candidate pays half the standard deposit.
49. **A — ₹5,000.** An SC/ST candidate pays half the standard deposit.
50. **D — Not more than one-sixth.** The threshold includes exactly one-sixth; the winner exception applies.
51. **C — Two.** Section 33(7) sets a two-constituency limit in the relevant elections.
52. **B — Candidate disclosure affidavit.** Form 26 contains prescribed candidate disclosures.
53. **A — A pending case is not itself a conviction.** Disclosure helps informed choice; guilt is determined by courts.
54. **D — An uncontested declaration may follow.** A sole remaining candidate can be declared elected uncontested.
55. **C — Scrutiny.** The RO scrutinises nominations after the nomination window.
56. **B — Withdrawal.** Eligible nominated candidates may withdraw by the deadline.
57. **A — Candidate with most valid votes wins.** First-past-the-post does not require an absolute majority.
58. **D — A.** The candidate with the largest number of valid votes wins.
59. **C — RO decides by lot under law.** Section 65 prescribes a decision by lot for an equality of votes.
60. **B — Election to fill a casual vacancy.** A by-election fills a seat that falls vacant between general elections.
61. **A — Form 6.** Form 6 is used to apply for inclusion as an elector.
62. **D — Form 6A.** Form 6A is the overseas-elector application.
63. **C — Form 6B.** Form 6B concerns furnishing Aadhaar information.
64. **B — Form 7.** Form 7 is used for relevant objections and deletion requests.
65. **A — Form 8.** Form 8 covers shifting, correction and related services.
66. **D — 1 April.** The four dates are 1 January, 1 April, 1 July and 1 October.
67. **C — Electors Photo Identity Card.** EPIC is the electors' photo identity card.
68. **B — No.** Roll enrolment and applicable identification requirements both matter.
69. **A — 17A.** Electors sign or provide a thumb impression in Form 17A.
70. **D — 17C.** The Presiding Officer prepares Part I of Form 17C after the poll.
71. **C — Part II.** Part II records the result of counting for the polling station.
72. **B — Polling agents present.** Authenticated copies go to polling agents present under instructions.
73. **A — 20.** Form 20 consolidates constituency results including postal votes.
74. **D — 21C.** Form 21C applies to a contested general election.
75. **C — 21D.** Form 21D applies to a contested election to fill a casual vacancy.
76. **B — 21E.** Rule 64 provides for the return in Form 21E.
77. **A — 22.** Form 22 is the certificate issued to the returned candidate.
78. **D — Postal ballot application by eligible absentee voters.** Eligible absentee voters apply subject to election-specific rules.
79. **C — Ballot unit.** The ballot unit is placed in the voting compartment.
80. **B — Control unit.** The control unit stores the electronic votes for counting.
81. **A — Voter verifies printed choice briefly.** The printed slip is visible briefly and falls into a sealed box.
82. **D — Seven seconds.** The slip is displayed briefly, approximately seven seconds.
83. **C — It falls into a sealed box.** The paper slip is retained securely for verification.
84. **B — They must be cleared.** The mock count is cleared and the system secured before real votes.
85. **A — 50.** The handbook stipulates at least 50 mock votes, with coverage of choices.
86. **D — None of the Above.** NOTA is an option to reject listed candidates without choosing one.
87. **C — Highest-polling candidate still wins.** NOTA is not a candidate and does not itself mandate a rerun.
88. **B — First under Rule 54A.** Rule 54A provides for postal ballots to be dealt with first.
89. **A — Five polling stations per Assembly constituency or segment.** The current sampling direction is five randomly selected stations per Assembly constituency or segment.
90. **D — 56D.** Rule 56D concerns requests for counting VVPAT paper slips.
91. **C — 63.** Rule 63 sets out the recount application and RO's decision.
92. **B — Decides it with reasons under the rule.** The RO may allow or reject a written request, recording reasons.
93. **A — Secured strong room.** Strong-room storage is guarded under prescribed arrangements.
94. **D — Eligible voters already in the queue at closing.** Eligible voters already queued at closing are allowed to vote.
95. **C — Model Code of Conduct.** The MCC is a code governing behaviour during elections.
96. **B — No; related conduct may violate separate laws.** The code itself is not a single statute; other laws and ECI powers may apply.
97. **A — 48 hours ending with poll close.** Section 126 regulates specified activity during the last 48 hours before poll close.
98. **D — RPA 1951 section 126A.** Section 126A addresses exit polls during the notified period.
99. **C — Citizen reporting of election violations.** cVIGIL supports citizen complaints about election violations.
100. **B — 100 minutes.** The 100-minute figure is an administrative target, not a guaranteed outcome.
101. **A — Secrecy of voting.** Section 128 requires maintenance of voting secrecy.
102. **D — RPA 1951 section 123.** Section 123 defines corrupt practices for election law purposes.
103. **C — RPA 1951 section 77.** Section 77 requires a separate and correct candidate expense account.
104. **B — Within 30 days after election of returned candidate.** Section 78 generally requires lodging within 30 days.
105. **A — Three years.** The ECI can disqualify for three years under the statutory conditions.
106. **D — ₹40 lakh.** AP is in the group with the ₹40 lakh Assembly candidate ceiling; verify later changes.
107. **C — ₹95 lakh.** Telangana is in the group with the ₹95 lakh Lok Sabha candidate ceiling; verify updates.
108. **B — It is not a universal cap on party expenditure.** Candidate and party expenditure are not interchangeable categories.
109. **A — Section 170.** Chapter IX of the BNS covers election offences including bribery in section 170.
110. **D — Section 21(3).** Section 21(3) concerns special revision of rolls.
111. **C — Special Intensive Revision of electoral rolls.** SIR is a process of intensive electoral-roll revision.
112. **B — A provisional indication before final declaration.** Only the authorised declaration after applicable procedures settles the constituency result.
113. **A — Appropriate High Court.** RPA 1951 provides for election petitions before the High Court.
114. **D — No; government formation is a separate process.** The Governor appoints the CM and the government must command Assembly confidence.
115. **C — Oath or affirmation under Article 99.** The prescribed oath or affirmation precedes taking the seat.
116. **B — Enable voting as instructed at the polling station.** The assigned officer enables a voter to cast one vote.
117. **A — ERO.** The ERO decides claims and objections under applicable law.
118. **D — Legislate on election matters within constitutional limits.** Article 327 concerns Parliament's power to make election laws.
119. **C — People's Union for Civil Liberties v Union of India.** The 2013 PUCL judgment directed a secrecy-protecting NOTA option.
120. **B — Six months.** Section 151A provides a six-month timeframe with exceptions.
