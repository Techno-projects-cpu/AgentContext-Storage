# 1. Election stages — the whole story

**Say this five times:**

> **Announcement → Notification → Nomination → Scrutiny → Withdrawal → Campaign → Polling → Counting → Result**

| Stage | What happens? |
|---|---|
| **1. Announcement** | Election Commission announces the schedule. The Model Code of Conduct normally begins. |
| **2. Notification** | Election is formally called; nominations open according to the schedule. |
| **3. Nomination** (నామినేషన్) | People submit papers to contest. |
| **4. Scrutiny** (పరిశీలన) | Returning Officer checks whether nominations are valid. |
| **5. Withdrawal** (ఉపసంహరణ) | Valid candidates may withdraw by the deadline; the final list is published. |
| **6. Campaign** (ప్రచారం) | Candidates seek votes. Campaigning can overlap earlier stages; restrictions apply before polling ends. |
| **7. Polling** (పోలింగ్) | Eligible enrolled voters vote. Machines are sealed and stored securely afterwards. |
| **8. Counting** (ఓట్ల లెక్కింపు) | Postal and EVM votes are counted under the rules; required checks are completed. |
| **9. Result** (ఫలితం) | Returning Officer declares the candidate with the most valid votes elected. |

**Afterwards:** The elected member takes an oath or affirmation before taking their seat. Forming a government is a separate process.

### Three quiz traps

- **Announcement ≠ notification:** one announces the schedule; the other formally calls the election.
- **Scrutiny ≠ counting:** scrutiny checks nomination papers, not votes.
- **Most votes ≠ more than 50%:** in these elections, the candidate with the most valid votes wins.

**Quick test:** What comes between nomination and withdrawal? **Scrutiny.** Who declares the winner? **Returning Officer.**
