# 3. Laws and numbers — high-yield facts

## Constitution: learn these in order

| Article | Key idea |
|---|---|
| **324** | Election Commission supervises elections. |
| **325** | One general electoral roll; no exclusion solely on religion, race, caste or sex. |
| **326** | Adult suffrage for Lok Sabha and State Assemblies. |
| **329** | Election challenges ordinarily go through the legal election-petition process; not a total ban on judicial review. |

**Memory:** **324 Commission → 325 Roll → 326 Vote → 329 Challenge.**

## The two main Acts

- **Representation of the People Act, 1950:** electoral rolls and voter registration.
- **Representation of the People Act, 1951:** conduct of elections, candidates, offences and election disputes.

**Memory:** **1950 = roll; 1951 = election.** Each Act covers additional subjects too.

## Numbers worth knowing

| Fact | Answer |
|---|---|
| Minimum voting age | **18** (61st Constitutional Amendment). |
| Minimum candidate age for Lok Sabha / Assembly | Generally **25**. |
| Proposers | **1** for a recognised-party candidate; **10** for an independent or unrecognised-party candidate. |
| Nomination deposit | Lok Sabha **₹25,000**; Assembly **₹10,000**. SC/ST candidates pay half. |
| Silence period | **48 hours ending at poll close**; §126 restricts specified campaign activities. |
| Winner | Candidate with the **most valid votes**, not necessarily more than half. |

**Don't confuse:** The deposit is not the candidate's spending limit. A losing candidate may lose the deposit if their votes do not exceed one-sixth of the valid votes polled by all candidates.

**Quick test:** Which article is about adult suffrage? **326.** Which Act deals mainly with voter rolls? **1950.**
