# 1. Drug-Free India / Nasha Mukt Bharat

## ⭐ 20 facts to learn first

1. **Nasha Mukt Bharat Abhiyaan (NMBA)** = Drug-Free India Campaign (నషా ముక్త్ భారత్ అభియాన్).
2. Launched **15 August 2020** by the **Ministry of Social Justice and Empowerment**.
3. Initially covered **272 identified vulnerable districts**; expanded to **all districts** from **15 August 2023**.
4. NMBA is a component of **NAPDDR**: National Action Plan for Drug Demand Reduction.
5. Aim: prevent substance misuse through awareness, community participation and support for treatment and rehabilitation.
6. Three-pronged approach: **supply control**, **awareness/demand reduction**, **treatment and rehabilitation**.
7. **NCB** = Narcotics Control Bureau; a central agency under the **Ministry of Home Affairs** involved in drug law enforcement.
8. **NDPS Act, 1985** = Narcotic Drugs and Psychotropic Substances Act, 1985. It regulates controlled substances and addresses unlawful activities; permitted medical/scientific use is different from illicit use.
9. **14446** = national de-addiction helpline: counselling, guidance and referral.
10. **1933** = **MANAS** national narcotics helpline: report drug trafficking and related activity; also connects people with support.
11. **14416** = Tele-MANAS mental-health helpline. **MANAS 1933** and **Tele-MANAS 14416** are different services.
12. **112** = emergency response; **108** = ambulance in Telangana. In a medical emergency call immediately.
13. **26 June** = International Day against Drug Abuse and Illicit Trafficking, also called World Drug Day.
14. **UNODC** = United Nations Office on Drugs and Crime.
15. **Nicotine** is the addictive substance in tobacco; tobacco is harmful even though its legal regulation differs from NDPS drugs.
16. **Alcohol** is a depressant; it can impair judgment, coordination and health.
17. An **opioid overdose** can dangerously slow breathing. **Naloxone** can reverse opioid overdose, but emergency medical care is still essential.
18. Substance-use disorders are treatable health conditions; support, counselling and appropriate treatment matter. Avoid shame and stigma.
19. A prescribed medicine can be beneficial when used correctly but harmful if misused; never share or take someone else's prescription.
20. If pressured: **say no, leave, and tell a trusted adult**. Help a friend get support, not punishment or ridicule.

| District-level committee | Local coordinating body | Local plans and activities; typically led by the District Collector/Magistrate. |

**Three-pronged strategy:** (1) reduce illegal supply; (2) reduce demand through education and outreach; (3) provide treatment and rehabilitation. Real programmes require coordination between government, schools, families, health professionals and communities.

**Dated figure, not a permanent number:** A PIB release dated **6 August 2026** reported that NMBA outreach had reached over **29.05 crore people**, including over **11.05 crore youth** and **7.81 crore women**. These are reported awareness reach figures, **not** numbers of people diagnosed or cured. For a quiz, give the date if quoting them.

## C. Substances and effects — know the categories

| Category / example | Main idea | Major risk |
|---|---|---|
| **Opioids** — heroin; medical opioids such as morphine | Some relieve pain when medically prescribed; illicit use and misuse can be dangerous. | Respiratory depression, dependence and fatal overdose. |
| **Stimulants** — cocaine, methamphetamine | Increase activity/arousal in the nervous system. | Heart problems, anxiety, dependence and other harms. |
| **Cannabis** | Can affect perception, attention and memory. | Impaired coordination, mental-health harms and dependence risk. Legal treatment depends on specific substance and law. |
| **Alcohol** | Depressant; affects coordination and judgment. | Accidents, dependence, liver and other health problems. |
| **Tobacco / nicotine** | Nicotine causes dependence; smoking exposes the body to many toxic substances. | Cancer, heart and lung disease; smokeless tobacco also causes cancer. |
| **Inhalants** — some solvents/adhesives | Breathing in vapours for intoxication can be highly dangerous. | Poisoning, brain/organ damage and sudden death. |
| **Sedatives** | Some are prescribed for medical purposes. | Misuse can cause dangerous drowsiness and slowed breathing, especially with alcohol/opioids. |

**Important:** Effects vary by substance, amount, individual and combinations. All medicines are not illegal drugs; medicines should be used only as directed by a qualified professional. Alcohol and tobacco are regulated separately, not automatically classed as NDPS substances. Do not experiment.

## D. Health, prevention and helping someone

- **Brain and learning:** substance use can impair memory, attention, decision-making and coordination. Young people are particularly vulnerable to developing problems.
- **Physical health:** risks can include injury, poisoning, organ damage, infection from shared injecting equipment and overdose. The actual harm depends on the substance and method of use.
- **Family and society:** harm can affect relationships, school attendance, finances and safety. Don't assume every person using a substance is violent or a criminal.
- **Why people may be vulnerable:** curiosity, peer pressure, stress, trauma, easy availability and other social and personal factors. No single cause explains every case.
- **Protective steps:** reliable information, supportive adults, healthy activities, coping skills, early help and limiting access to harmful substances. A firm “No thanks, I'm leaving” is enough.
- **Help a friend:** listen without shaming; encourage them to tell a trusted adult, doctor or counsellor; call **14446** together for advice. Don't promise secrecy if someone's safety is at immediate risk.
- **Suspected overdose:** if someone is unresponsive or breathing slowly, call **112 or 108 immediately**, tell a trusted adult, and follow dispatcher instructions. If trained and available, use naloxone for a suspected opioid overdose. Stay with them; do not leave them alone or give food/drink to an unconscious person. Naloxone is not a substitute for medical care and does not reverse every drug overdose.

## E. Law and dates

- **NDPS Act 1985:** key Indian law concerning narcotic drugs and psychotropic substances; it allows controlled medical/scientific uses and prohibits certain activities without authorisation. Penalties depend on the offence, substance, quantity and law. Don't guess a single punishment for everything.
- **COTPA 2003:** Cigarettes and Other Tobacco Products Act; tobacco-control legislation. It is separate from the NDPS Act.
- **26 June:** International Day against Drug Abuse and Illicit Trafficking, established by UN General Assembly resolution **42/112 in 1987**.
- **31 May:** World No Tobacco Day; do not confuse with 26 June.
- **15 August 2020:** NMBA launch; **15 August 2023:** expanded to all districts.
- **18 July 2024:** MANAS narcotics helpline launched.

## F. One-line answers / potential quiz questions

- NMBA launched when? **15 August 2020.**
- Started in how many districts? **272.** Now covers? **All districts**, since 15 August 2023.
- Lead ministry? **Social Justice and Empowerment.**
- Agency associated with drug law enforcement? **NCB**, under Home Affairs.
- What is drug demand reduction? **Reducing the desire or need to use through prevention, awareness and support.**
- Which law? **NDPS Act 1985.** Which day? **26 June.**
- Help for de-addiction? **14446.** Report drug trafficking? **1933.** Mental health? **14416.** Immediate emergency? **112 / 108.**
- Is addiction treatable? **Yes.** Is shaming helpful? **No; supportive referral is better.**
- Is naloxone for every overdose? **No; it reverses opioid effects and emergency care is essential.**
