# Quiz day: officials, real stories & quick revision

**Read sections 1–3 first if you're in a hurry.** The poster includes **three topics**: Drug-Free India; Indian history and cultural heritage; Telangana cultural heritage. These are possible quiz points, not leaked or predicted questions. English explanations + Telugu key words.

## 1. ⭐ The 60-second anti-drug recap

- **Nasha Mukt Bharat Abhiyaan (NMBA)** = Drug-Free India Campaign; launched **15 August 2020**.
- Lead ministry: **Ministry of Social Justice and Empowerment** (MSJE).
- Started in **272 districts**; expanded to **all districts from 15 August 2023**.
- Three approaches: **stop illegal supply + raise awareness/reduce demand + treat and rehabilitate**.
- **NCB** = Narcotics Control Bureau; central drug law-enforcement and coordination agency under the **Ministry of Home Affairs**.
- Main narcotics law: **NDPS Act, 1985**. Drug-use disorders need compassionate healthcare as well as effective law enforcement.
- **26 June** = International Day against Drug Abuse and Illicit Trafficking; **31 May** = World No Tobacco Day.
- **14446** = de-addiction help; **1933** = MANAS drug reporting/support; **14416** = Tele-MANAS mental health; **112 / 108** = emergency / ambulance in Telangana.
- **WADA** = World Anti-Doping Agency; **NADA** = National Anti-Doping Agency, India. These address prohibited substances and methods **in sport**, not the same job as NCB.

## 2. Who works to make India drug-free?

**Easy memory:** **Ministry plans → district coordinates → officials enforce → doctors treat → community prevents.** It is a collaboration, not a single rigid chain of command.

| Person / organisation | తెలుగు / role | What they actually do |
|---|---|---|
| **Ministry of Social Justice and Empowerment** | సామాజిక న్యాయం మరియు సాధికారత మంత్రిత్వ శాఖ | Leads NMBA: awareness, demand reduction, community outreach and support for de-addiction and rehabilitation. |
| **NCB and its officers** | మాదక ద్రవ్యాల నియంత్రణ బ్యూరో అధికారులు | Gather intelligence, investigate and coordinate action against illicit narcotics trafficking under applicable law. NCB was established **17 March 1986**. |
| **Ministry of Home Affairs (MHA)** | కేంద్ర హోం మంత్రిత్వ శాఖ | Oversees NCB and national coordination on drug-law enforcement. |
| **Health ministry / health department** | ఆరోగ్య శాఖ | Helps provide medical treatment and healthcare for people affected by substance use. |
| **State NMBA committee** | రాష్ట్ర స్థాయి కమిటీ | Helps coordinate the campaign across the state with relevant departments. |
| **District Collector / District Magistrate** | జిల్లా కలెక్టర్ | Leads the district-level Nasha Mukt committee and coordinates local awareness plans and departments. The Collector is not the person who personally conducts every arrest or medical treatment. |
| **District Prohibition & Excise Officer / Excise Superintendent** | జిల్లా ప్రొహిబిషన్ మరియు ఎక్సైజ్ అధికారి | Heads district excise administration, supervises relevant enforcement and coordinates with other authorities as required. |
| **Prohibition & Excise Inspector / SHO** | ఎక్సైజ్ ఇన్‌స్పెక్టర్ / స్టేషన్ అధికారి | Station-level enforcement and investigations within legal powers; may also support awareness. |
| **Police / District SP or Commissioner** | పోలీసు / ఎస్పీ | Police leadership coordinates district/city law enforcement; officers investigate relevant crimes, assist enforcement and public safety. |
| **Telangana Anti-Narcotics Bureau (TGANB)** | తెలంగాణ మాదక ద్రవ్యాల నిరోధక విభాగం | Telangana specialist anti-narcotics agency; works with police and other departments on drug control. |
| **Drug-control officials / Drugs Control Administration** | ఔషధ నియంత్రణ అధికారులు | Regulate medicines and investigate unlawful manufacture or sale within their powers; medicines and illicit narcotics are related but not identical subjects. |
| **Doctors, nurses, psychiatrists and counsellors** | వైద్యులు, నర్సులు, కౌన్సెలర్లు | Assess and treat health problems, support recovery and make referrals. |
| **Master Volunteers / Nasha Mukti Mitra** | స్వచ్ఛంద సేవకులు | Conduct community awareness and outreach. They are not automatically police officers. |
| **Teachers, parents, NGOs and students** | ఉపాధ్యాయులు, తల్లిదండ్రులు, స్వచ్ఛంద సంస్థలు | Build awareness, support students, identify concerns sensitively and help people reach professional care. |

## 3. Sports, doping and real examples

**Doping (డోపింగ్)** means an anti-doping rule violation under the relevant sports rules. It can involve prohibited substances or methods, and other violations—not just a positive test. Some substances are prohibited always, others only in competition or particular sports; rules change. An anti-doping violation does **not** automatically prove addiction, intentional cheating or a criminal conviction.

| Who / what? | What happened? | Why it is useful in a quiz |
|---|---|---|
| **Ben Johnson, Canada** | At the **1988 Seoul Olympics**, the 100-metre sprinter tested positive for steroids and was disqualified; his gold went to **Carl Lewis**. | A famous **performance-enhancing steroid** case. [Olympics](https://www.olympics.com/en/olympic-games/seoul-1988) |
| **Kamalpreet Kaur, India** | The Indian discus thrower was sanctioned for the presence/use of prohibited **stanozolol**. In 2022 the Athletics Integrity Unit recorded **three years' ineligibility from 29 March 2022**, with results disqualified from 7 March 2022. | Indian example of an anti-doping sanction. This is a **historical sanction**, not a claim that she remains banned today or has an addiction. [AIU decision listing](https://www.athleticsintegrity.org/disciplinary-process/first-instance-decisions?gsid=a9b475e3-b198-44e2-be98-8b5d142c3af5) |
| **Sha’Carri Richardson, USA** | In **2021**, the sprinter accepted a **one-month** sanction after a competition sample tested positive for a THC metabolite. USADA said her use occurred outside competition and was unrelated to performance; her trial result was disqualified. | Not every violation is performance enhancement. This case does **not** establish addiction. [USADA](https://www.usada.org/sanction/shacarri-richardson-accepts-doping-sanction/) |
| **Kirti Bhoite / coach Mickey Menezes** | In a 2022 case, an Indian anti-doping appeal panel sanctioned coach **Mickey Menezes** for administering a prohibited steroid to athlete **Kirti Bhoite**. | Coaches and support personnel can also face anti-doping action; the case illustrates why athletes should question unknown injections or supplements. [PIB](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleseDetailm.aspx?PRID=1873982&reg=48&lang=2) |
| **Neeraj Chopra: positive example** | In **June 2026**, reporting said the Neeraj Chopra Foundation partnered with the Indian Olympic Association on clean-sport education. | An example of **preventing doping**, not a doping allegation. Being in a testing pool is not evidence of a violation. [Report](https://www.rediff.com/sports/report/neeraj-chopra-and-ioa-launch-anti-doping-awareness-campaign/20260619.htm) |

### The sports organisations

- **WADA:** global body that coordinates anti-doping standards and publishes the **Prohibited List**; the list is updated at least annually.
- **NADA India:** conducts and promotes India's anti-doping programme, including testing and education.
- **NDTL:** National Dope Testing Laboratory; analyses doping-control samples.
- **AIU:** Athletics Integrity Unit; independently handles integrity and anti-doping matters in international athletics.
- **National Anti-Doping Act, 2022:** Indian law supporting the anti-doping framework. This is separate from the **NDPS Act, 1985**.
- **TUE:** Therapeutic Use Exemption; in specified circumstances athletes may apply for permission to use an otherwise prohibited medicine for a genuine medical condition. Follow the applicable rules and medical advice.

**Big difference:** A prohibited steroid may be misused to enhance performance; a substance like cannabis may be prohibited by sports rules in competition even if its use was unrelated to performance. A positive test by itself is not a medical diagnosis of substance-use disorder.

## 4. Drugs and health — quick bullets

- **Nicotine** is the addictive chemical in tobacco. Smoking and smokeless tobacco can cause cancer and other serious illness.
- **Alcohol** is a depressant; it can impair coordination and judgment.
- **Heroin** is an opioid. An opioid overdose can dangerously slow breathing.
- **Cocaine and methamphetamine** are stimulants. Misuse can harm the heart and mental health.
- **Naloxone** can reverse an opioid overdose; it is **not** a cure for every overdose. Emergency treatment is still needed.
- **Dependence, tolerance and addiction are related but not identical:** don't treat these terms as automatic synonyms.
- **Addiction is treatable.** Listen without shaming, encourage a trusted adult/doctor/counsellor, and call **14446** for help.
- For someone unconscious or breathing slowly: call **112 or 108 immediately**, alert an adult and follow dispatcher instructions. If trained and naloxone is available, use it for suspected opioid overdose; remain with the person. Don't give food or drink to an unconscious person.
- **Medicines are not automatically illegal:** prescribed treatment can be beneficial; misuse, sharing or taking unknown medicines can be dangerous.

## 5. Telangana & Khammam — one-minute revision

- **2 June 2014:** Telangana formed; capital **Hyderabad**.
- **Bathukamma:** floral festival; **Bonalu:** offerings associated with goddess Mahakali.
- **Sammakka–Saralamma Jatara:** **Medaram**, Mulugu district; held every two years.
- **Kakatiyas:** Warangal heritage; famous ruler **Rudrama Devi**.
- **Ramappa / Kakatiya Rudreshwara Temple:** **Palampet, Mulugu**; UNESCO World Heritage **2021**.
- **Thousand Pillar Temple:** **Hanamkonda**. Don't confuse it with Ramappa Temple.
- **Charminar:** Hyderabad; **Muhammad Quli Qutb Shah**, **1591**.
- **Perini:** Telangana warrior-associated dance; **Oggu Katha:** folk storytelling.
- **Pochampally Ikat:** textile; **Cheriyal:** scroll painting.
- **Khammam Fort** and **Nelakondapalli Buddhist site**: Khammam heritage.
- **Bhadrachalam temple:** Godavari river, present-day **Bhadradri Kothagudem district**; associated with **Bhakta Ramadasu / Kancherla Gopanna**.

## 6. Indian history & culture — one-minute revision

- **Indus Civilisation:** planned cities; Great Bath at **Mohenjo-daro**.
- **Buddha:** enlightenment at **Bodh Gaya**; first sermon at **Sarnath**.
- **Mahavira:** 24th Jain Tirthankara; **ahimsa** is central to Jainism.
- **Ashoka:** Mauryan emperor, Kalinga war, edicts; Lion Capital at Sarnath inspired the national emblem.
- **1857:** major uprising; **1885:** Indian National Congress founded.
- **1919:** Jallianwala Bagh; **1930:** Dandi March; **1942:** Quit India.
- **15 August 1947:** independence; **26 January 1950:** Constitution came into force.
- **B. R. Ambedkar:** chair of Constitution Drafting Committee; **Rabindranath Tagore:** composed national anthem.
- Dance pairs: **Kuchipudi—Andhra Pradesh; Bharatanatyam—Tamil Nadu; Kathakali—Kerala; Odissi—Odisha; Sattriya—Assam.**
- **Sarnath**, Uttar Pradesh, was inscribed on UNESCO's World Heritage List in **2026**; **Ramappa** was inscribed in **2021**. [UNESCO Sarnath](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/927/) · [UNESCO Ramappa](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/1570/).
