# 1. NMBA: how does an entire country fight drugs? (0–15 min)

## The core story ⭐

**Nasha Mukt Bharat Abhiyaan (NMBA)** means **Drug-Free India Campaign**. Its goal is to prevent and reduce harmful substance use through **awareness, participation and access to care**. It is led by the **Ministry of Social Justice and Empowerment**.

- **15 August 2020:** launched in **272** identified vulnerable districts.
- **August 2022:** expanded to **372** districts.
- **15 August 2023:** extended to **all districts**.
- It is a component of **NAPDDR**: *National Action Plan for Drug Demand Reduction*.
- Vulnerable districts were identified using inputs including the national survey and information from the **Narcotics Control Bureau**.

**Why those three numbers?** They are stages in the same campaign. If a question asks “initially,” answer **272**; if it asks “now,” say **all districts**. Don't call 372 the current national total.

### Three approaches that work together

1. **Supply reduction:** disrupt unlawful manufacture and trafficking; police, NCB and other authorised agencies have roles.
2. **Demand reduction:** reduce harmful use through prevention, awareness, education and community support.
3. **Treatment and rehabilitation:** provide healthcare, counselling and continued recovery support.

**Reasoning question:** If police stop a supplier but someone with dependence receives no care, has the problem necessarily been solved? **No.** Enforcement and health support address different parts of the problem.

### Programme infrastructure 🔍

- **IRCA:** Integrated Rehabilitation Centre for Addicts—treatment and rehabilitation services. The official acronym uses “addicts”; person-first language such as “people with substance-use disorders” avoids stigma.
- **ODIC:** Outreach and Drop-In Centre—community outreach and links to support.
- **CPLI:** Community-based Peer-Led Intervention—peer-led prevention, particularly for vulnerable adolescents and young people.
- **DDAC:** District De-Addiction Centre—brings together relevant services, including IRCA/ODIC/CPLI functions as envisaged under the scheme.
- **Master Volunteers / Nasha Mukti Mitrs:** help run awareness activities; volunteers are not automatically police or healthcare professionals.
- PIB reported **over 29.05 crore people reached/sensitised** on **6 August 2026**. This is a dated government **outreach figure**, not a count of people treated or cured. Don't use changing totals without the date.

### Official machinery: don't mix the jobs ⭐

| Who / what | Job | Easy contrast |
|---|---|---|
| **Social Justice & Empowerment Ministry** | NMBA and demand reduction | Campaign lead, not the same as every enforcement agency. |
| **Ministry of Home Affairs** | Oversees NCB | NCB's parent ministry. |
| **NCB** | Narcotics Control Bureau: enforcement and coordination | NCB ≠ NCRB. |
| **NCRB** | National Crime Records Bureau: crime records and statistics | Not the same as NCB. |
| **Department of Revenue, Ministry of Finance** | NDPS administration / narcotics policy responsibilities | Different from NCB's parent ministry. |
| **Central Bureau of Narcotics (CBN)** | Revenue-side narcotics regulation and enforcement functions | CBN ≠ NCB. |
| **State and district authorities** | Coordinate local plans and work with departments | The district committee is normally headed by the Collector / District Magistrate. |
| **Telangana Anti-Narcotics Bureau** | State-level specialist anti-narcotics work | State agency, not WADA or NADA. |
| **Excise and police officers** | Enforce relevant laws within their powers, investigate and coordinate | Not the professionals who diagnose or provide therapy. |
| **Doctors and counsellors** | Assess, treat, counsel and help recovery | Treat a health condition; do not assume all patients are criminals. |
| **Teachers, families, NGOs, volunteers** | Education, early support and referral | Work together with government and health services. |

The poster names the **Prohibition and Excise Department, Khammam District** as the organiser. It lists **K. Raju, Inspector; G. Chiranjeevi, Inspector; and E. Kishore Reddy** as contacts. These are poster details, not proof that these people lead the national programme.

### NCORD: a genuinely good quiz trap 🔍

**NCORD = Narco Coordination Centre**, a coordination mechanism. PIB says it was introduced in **2016** and restructured into **four tiers in 2019**:

1. **Apex:** Union Home Secretary.
2. **Executive:** Special Secretary (Internal Security), Ministry of Home Affairs.
3. **State:** Chief Secretary.
4. **District:** District Magistrate.

**Why two district committees?** The **district NMBA committee** coordinates campaign activity, while **district NCORD** is part of an inter-agency coordination mechanism. They can involve overlapping officials but are not interchangeable names for the same programme.

# 2. Drugs, the brain and helping people (15–30 min)

## What is a psychoactive substance? ⭐

A **psychoactive substance** affects the central nervous system and can change mood, perception, thinking or behaviour. Some are medicines with legitimate uses. **Effect depends on the specific substance, amount, person and combination.**

| Type | Example | What to remember |
|---|---|---|
| **Depressant** | Alcohol; some sedative medicines | Can slow nervous-system activity and impair coordination. |
| **Stimulant** | Cocaine, methamphetamine | Can increase alertness/arousal; misuse can harm the heart and mental health. |
| **Opioid** | Heroin; morphine used in medical care | Can relieve pain, but overdose can dangerously slow breathing. |
| **Cannabis** | Cannabis products containing THC | Can affect perception, memory and coordination; legal rules depend on the specific substance and context. |
| **Tobacco / nicotine** | Cigarettes, smokeless tobacco | Nicotine is dependence-producing; tobacco use causes serious disease. |
| **Inhalants** | Some solvents and adhesives | Inhaling vapours to get intoxicated can cause severe poisoning, organ damage or sudden death. |

**Quiz trap:** “Drug” can mean a medicine as well as a misused psychoactive substance. Not all medicines are illegal, not all drugs cause the same harm, and tobacco/alcohol are not automatically governed by the same rules as NDPS-controlled substances.

## Why can dependence develop? 🔍

Think of a **learning loop**, not a moral failing:

1. A substance may affect brain pathways involved in reward, stress and learning.
2. With repeated use, changes can make certain cues or situations trigger a strong **craving**.
3. Some people develop **tolerance**: the same dose has a reduced effect, though tolerance does not make use safe.
4. After reducing or stopping some substances, a dependent person may experience **withdrawal**: symptoms depend on the substance.
5. **Substance-use disorder / addiction** involves a problematic pattern with impaired control and harm. It is treatable. Not everyone who uses a substance develops a disorder.

**Important distinction:** physical dependence, tolerance and addiction are related but not identical. A person may develop physical dependence during appropriate medical treatment without necessarily having an addiction.

**Safety:** Withdrawal from alcohol or certain sedatives can be dangerous. Someone who is dependent should get medical advice rather than attempting to manage severe withdrawal alone. Combining opioids, sedatives and alcohol increases overdose risk.

## Prevention, care and recovery ⭐

- **Risk factors:** peer pressure, stress, trauma, easy availability and family or social difficulties can contribute; no single factor determines anyone's future.
- **Protective factors:** supportive relationships, accurate information, healthy coping skills, school connection and timely healthcare.
- **If a friend is struggling:** listen privately without shaming, encourage a trusted adult or health professional, and use **14446** for guidance and referral.
- **Relapse:** a return to use after improvement. It can occur in a long-term condition; it calls for renewed help, not the conclusion that treatment is hopeless.
- **Opioid overdose:** unresponsiveness or very slow breathing may signal an emergency. Call **112 or 108**, alert a trusted adult, follow dispatcher instructions and stay with the person. If trained and available, give **naloxone** for suspected opioid overdose. Naloxone is temporary emergency help, not a replacement for medical care, and does not reverse every kind of drug overdose. Don't give food or drink to an unconscious person.

## The four numbers: make a story ⭐

- **1933 — MANAS:** narcotics-related reporting; can also connect callers to support. Think **“report”**.
- **14446 — national de-addiction helpline:** counselling, guidance and referral. Think **“recovery”**.
- **14416 — Tele-MANAS:** mental-health support. Think **“mind.”**
- **112 — emergency response; 108 — ambulance in Telangana.** Think **“immediate danger.”**

**Trap:** MANAS and **Tele-MANAS** are different services despite the similar names.

# 3. The law, sports doping and real stories (30–40 min)

## Law: control and care are both part of the picture

- **NDPS Act, 1985:** India's main statute controlling narcotic drugs and psychotropic substances, including restrictions on unauthorised activities and provisions permitting legitimate uses under law.
- **Section 27:** concerns punishment for consumption of specified narcotic drugs or psychotropic substances; the provision distinguishes categories. Don't invent a single punishment applicable to every case.
- **Section 64A:** contains a **conditional** immunity-from-prosecution provision for a person who meets its specific legal definition and conditions, including specified charges and voluntary treatment at an eligible institution. Immunity can be withdrawn if complete treatment is not undergone. **Not a blanket exemption:** a real case needs professional legal advice and the current law.
- **COTPA, 2003:** tobacco-control law, separate from the NDPS Act.
- **National Anti-Doping Act, 2022:** Indian sports anti-doping framework, distinct from the NDPS Act.

🔍 **International day:** **26 June** is the International Day against Drug Abuse and Illicit Trafficking; the UN General Assembly established it in **1987**, through resolution **42/112**. **31 May** is World No Tobacco Day.

## Sports doping is not the same as addiction ⭐

**Doping** means an anti-doping rule violation; it may involve a prohibited substance or method, or other violations. A positive sample is not automatically proof of addiction, deliberate cheating or a criminal conviction. Some substances are banned **at all times**, others **in competition** or in particular sports. The prohibited list is updated; check the rule applying at the time.

- **WADA:** World Anti-Doping Agency—global standards and Prohibited List.
- **NADA India:** national anti-doping programme and education.
- **NDTL:** National Dope Testing Laboratory—analyses samples.
- **AIU:** Athletics Integrity Unit—integrity matters in international athletics.
- **TUE:** Therapeutic Use Exemption, a formal process for eligible medical use of a prohibited substance or method under strict rules. A prescription alone does not automatically grant one.
- **Strict liability:** athletes are responsible for prohibited substances found in their bodies under the rules; intent and circumstances can matter when determining consequences. Supplements can be contaminated.

### Real cases: facts, not gossip

**1. Ben Johnson — a performance-related scandal.** Canadian sprinter, **Seoul 1988**: after winning the 100 metres he tested positive for steroids and was disqualified. **Carl Lewis** was awarded the gold. This is a historical case—not proof about every athlete or every substance.

**2. Kamalpreet Kaur — Indian athletics.** In **2022**, the AIU listed a decision for prohibited **stanozolol**: three years' ineligibility from 29 March 2022, with results disqualified from 7 March. That historical sanction should not be described as a current ban without checking.

**3. Sha’Carri Richardson — not every case is performance enhancement.** In **2021**, USADA recorded a one-month sanction following a positive cannabis-metabolite sample. USADA stated the use was out of competition and unrelated to performance, and qualifying results were disqualified. Do **not** claim she was addicted: that is not established by the sanction.

**4. Kirti Bhoite and coach Mickey Menezes — support staff can be responsible.** A 2022 PIB account describes a coach sanctioned for administering a prohibited steroid to an athlete. The athlete's case was also addressed through anti-doping proceedings. This demonstrates that athletes should check medicines and seek qualified guidance rather than accept unknown substances.

**Answers:** 1. National Anti-Doping Act, 2022. 2. WADA. 3. Therapeutic Use Exemption. 4. Ben Johnson. 5. No.

# 4. Indian history as a connected story (40–55 min)

## Ancient India: from cities to kingdoms

### Indus / Harappan civilisation ⭐

Imagine a city with organised streets, wells and drains: these are some features associated with the **Indus Valley or Harappan Civilisation**. Its mature urban phase is commonly dated approximately **2600–1900 BCE**.

- **Harappa** and **Mohenjo-daro** are in present-day Pakistan.
- **Great Bath → Mohenjo-daro**. Think: *Mohenjo = massive bath*.
- **Dholavira → Gujarat**, recognised for its urban features and water management.
- **Lothal → Gujarat**, associated with a structure often identified as a dockyard.
- The Indus script has **not been conclusively deciphered**; don't claim we can confidently read every inscription.

### Ideas and faiths ⭐

- Four Vedas: **Rig, Sama, Yajur, Atharva**. **Rigveda** is conventionally regarded as the oldest.
- **Buddha:** born at Lumbini; enlightened at **Bodh Gaya**; first sermon at **Sarnath**; mahaparinirvana at **Kushinagar**. Lumbini is in present-day Nepal; the others are in India.
- **Mahavira:** 24th Tirthankara of Jainism. **Ahimsa** = non-violence, central to Jain thought.
- **Stupa** = Buddhist commemorative sacred monument; **vihara** = monastic residence/complex. Sanchi in Madhya Pradesh is famous for its Buddhist monuments.

### Mauryas versus Guptas: names that trick people ⭐

| Name | Period / connection |
|---|---|
| **Chandragupta Maurya** | Founder of the **Mauryan** Empire. |
| **Ashoka** | Mauryan emperor; Kalinga war and dhamma; messages on rock and pillar edicts. |
| **Samudragupta** | Gupta ruler associated with the Allahabad/Prayagraj pillar inscription composed by Harisena. |
| **Chandragupta II** | Gupta ruler generally identified with King Chandra in Delhi's Iron Pillar inscription. |
| **Aryabhata** | Mathematician and astronomer associated with the Gupta-era intellectual world. |

**Don't mix up the Chandraguptas:** one founded the Mauryan Empire; another is a Gupta ruler linked with the Iron Pillar. The **Allahabad Pillar** has inscriptions from more than one period, so listen carefully to which inscription the question asks about.

**National emblem connection:** India's State Emblem is adapted from Ashoka's **Lion Capital at Sarnath**. **Satyameva Jayate** is from the Mundaka Upanishad. The Ashoka Chakra on the national flag has **24 spokes**.

## Medieval and early modern connections

- **Cholas → Thanjavur and Brihadisvara Temple:** South Indian temple architecture and maritime links.
- **Vijayanagara → Hampi, Karnataka:** impressive surviving monuments; **Krishnadevaraya** was a celebrated ruler and is associated with Telugu work *Amuktamalyada*.
- **Mughals → Taj Mahal, Agra:** commissioned by **Shah Jahan** in memory of **Mumtaz Mahal**.
- **Ajanta → Maharashtra:** Buddhist rock-cut caves renowned for murals and sculpture.
- **Ellora → Maharashtra:** rock-cut monuments linked with Buddhist, Hindu and Jain traditions.
- **Konark → Odisha:** Sun Temple, known for its chariot-like form.

## Freedom struggle: cause → event → consequence

| Year | Memory anchor |
|---|---|
| **1757** | Battle of Plassey; East India Company power expanded. |
| **1857** | Major uprising against Company rule; figures include Rani Lakshmibai and Bahadur Shah Zafar. |
| **1885** | Indian National Congress founded; A. O. Hume was associated with its founding. |
| **1905** | Partition of Bengal; Swadeshi and boycott gained momentum. |
| **1919** | Jallianwala Bagh massacre, Amritsar. |
| **1920** | Gandhi's Non-Cooperation Movement. |
| **1930** | Gandhi's Dandi Salt March and Civil Disobedience Movement. |
| **1942** | Quit India Movement; “Do or Die.” |
| **1947** | Independence and Partition. |
| **1949 / 1950** | Constitution adopted **26 November 1949**; came into effect **26 January 1950**. |

**Adopted ≠ effective:** This is a common quiz trap. Dr **B. R. Ambedkar** chaired the Drafting Committee; Dr **Rajendra Prasad** was President of the Constituent Assembly. **Jana Gana Mana → Rabindranath Tagore**; **Vande Mataram → Bankim Chandra Chattopadhyay**.

# 5. Telangana and Khammam: learn it as a map (55–70 min)

## A map made of memory hooks ⭐

**Palampet → Ramappa | Hanamkonda → Thousand Pillar | Hyderabad → Charminar | Medaram → Jatara | Khammam → Fort/Nelakondapalli | Bhadrachalam → Godavari.**

### Kakatiyas: dynasty, ruler, general, temple 🔍

The **Kakatiyas** shaped medieval Telangana, especially the Warangal region. Their heritage includes temples, stone gateways, sculptural art and water management.

- **Rudrama Devi:** prominent Kakatiya ruler, notable as a woman sovereign in medieval Indian history.
- **Warangal Fort:** associated with monumental gateways called **Kakatiya Kala Thoranam**.
- **Ramappa:** officially the **Kakatiya Rudreshwara Temple** at **Palampet in present-day Mulugu district**. UNESCO inscribed it in **2021**.
- Construction began around **1213 CE**; the UNESCO/ICOMOS assessment identifies **Recharla Rudra**, a Kakatiya general/chief, and his support for King **Ganapati Deva**.
- The temple's upper tower uses light porous **“floating bricks”** to reduce structural weight. UNESCO also discusses a **sandbox foundation** and sculpture depicting dance traditions.
- **Important distinction:** **Ganapati Deva = king associated with the reign; Recharla Rudra = general/chief associated with building; 1213 = construction began; 2021 = UNESCO inscription.** Different questions need different answers.
- **Thousand Pillar Temple:** **Hanamkonda**, Kakatiya-era temple associated with **Rudra Deva**. Both this and Ramappa have Rudreshwara associations: use the location to distinguish them.

### Hyderabad and the Qutb Shahis ⭐

- **Charminar:** Hyderabad landmark, built under **Muhammad Quli Qutb Shah**, **1591**. The name refers to its four minarets.
- **Golconda Fort:** associated with the Qutb Shahis and the Deccan's fortified heritage.
- **Qutb Shahi Tombs:** burial complex associated with the dynasty.
- **Asaf Jahis / Nizams:** later rulers of Hyderabad State. Telangana's heritage includes Telugu, Urdu and other linguistic traditions; **Ganga-Jamuni tehzeeb** describes a tradition of shared cultural influences.

## Festivals: don't just remember the name

| Festival | What to visualise | Key connection |
|---|---|---|
| **Bathukamma (బతుకమ్మ)** | Seasonal flowers stacked into a colourful arrangement, women singing around it | Floral festival, associated with Gauri and Telangana's cultural identity. |
| **Bonalu (బోనాలు)** | Decorated offering pots and processions | Offerings to goddess Mahakali, especially in Hyderabad/Secunderabad. |
| **Sammakka–Saralamma Jatara** | A vast gathering at Medaram | Important Adivasi/tribal festival in **Mulugu district**; generally held every two years. |
| **Ugadi** | A new calendar year | Telugu New Year; celebrated beyond Telangana too. |

**Compare:** Bathukamma = **flowers**; Bonalu = **Mahakali**; Medaram = **Sammakka and Saralamma**. Culture is diverse: festivals are living practices, not only exam answers.

## Performance, craft and literature

- **Perini Shivatandavam:** dynamic dance associated with Shiva and Kakatiya-era warrior tradition; revived in modern times. **Perini → Kakatiya.**
- **Oggu Katha:** folk storytelling, narration, song and music.
- **Gussadi:** dance associated with Gond/Adivasi communities.
- **Pochampally Ikat:** yarn is resist-dyed before weaving to create characteristic patterns.
- **Cheriyal:** narrative scroll-painting tradition associated with storytelling.
- **Pembarthi:** metal craft, especially brass work. **Nirmal:** painted art and woodcraft.
- **Kancherla Gopanna = Bhakta Ramadasu:** devotional composer associated with Bhadrachalam; connection to **Nelakondapalli**.
- **Kaloji Narayana Rao** and **Dasarathi Krishnamacharya:** literary figures associated with Telangana.
- **Komaram Bheem:** Adivasi leader associated with tribal rights; “Jal, Jangal, Zameen” is widely associated with him. **Chakali Ailamma:** peasant-resistance figure.

## Khammam and its neighbours ⭐

- **Khammam Fort:** a historic hill fort in Khammam city. Dates attributed to its earliest construction vary in local material: don't turn a contested year into a confident quiz answer.
- **Nelakondapalli:** a Khammam locality with Buddhist archaeological heritage and Bhakta Ramadasu connections.
- **Kusumanchi Sivalayam:** Kakatiya-period heritage in Khammam district.
- **Bhadrachalam:** temple town on the **Godavari**, in present-day **Bhadradri Kothagudem district**, a separate district after the 2016 reorganisation.
- **2 June 2014:** Telangana formed as a state; capital **Hyderabad**. The older 1956–2014 state context can differ from today's district boundaries, so pay attention to the date in a question.

# 6. Heritage, dance and UNESCO: think in pairs (70–80 min)

## What is cultural heritage?

- **Tangible heritage:** physical things—monuments, archaeological sites, sculpture, manuscripts, buildings.
- **Intangible heritage:** living practices—oral storytelling, performance, rituals, knowledge and skills. A dance is a practice; a sculpture depicting the dance is a physical object. The two can enrich each other.
- **UNESCO:** United Nations Educational, Scientific and Cultural Organization. Its **World Heritage List** recognises specific cultural, natural or mixed properties of outstanding universal value. A tentative-list proposal is **not yet an inscription**.

### Heritage site → place → idea

| Place | State / connection |
