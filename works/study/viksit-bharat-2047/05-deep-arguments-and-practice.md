# Viksit Bharat 2047: Deep Arguments and Independent Writing Practice

**For the essay:** Read the ★ lines first if your competition is tomorrow. This expands your previous material; it is not a list you need to squeeze into one page. Use your own voice. ## ★ The argument in one breath

A developed India in 2047 should combine **honest institutions, safe water, meaningful education, productive and cleaner industry, and dignified work**. Growth matters, but so do fairness, health and the living environment. Government, businesses, communities and citizens each have responsibilities.

### Turn an idea into an argument

Use **problem → practical action → benefit → limitation**. Example: “Factories can create jobs, but untreated wastewater can damage rivers. Enforcing treatment standards and monitoring discharge can protect water while allowing production to grow. Small firms may need affordable technology and technical support.” For a short essay you can omit the final sentence, but understanding the trade-off makes your argument stronger.

## 1. ★ What does development mean?

- **Economic growth:** more goods and services produced. It can supply jobs, incomes and tax revenue, but the benefits may be uneven.
- **Human development:** people have opportunities to live healthy lives, learn and enjoy a decent standard of living. An average can hide differences between groups.
- **Sustainability (స్థిరత్వం):** meeting present needs without undermining future generations' ability to meet theirs. Healthy rivers, soils, biodiversity and climate support both life and livelihoods.
- **Inclusion (సమావేశం):** reducing barriers to participation for people of different backgrounds and abilities. Fair opportunity is not the same as pretending every person starts with equal resources.
- **Resilience:** ability to prepare for, withstand and recover from shocks such as floods and illness.

**Think:** A district opens a new factory and incomes rise, but untreated waste damages the river. Is it progress? Yes, employment is a benefit; no, economic growth alone is not complete development. A better plan protects both livelihoods and water.

## 2. ★ Honest and capable governance

**Accountability (జవాబుదారీతనం)** means decision-makers must explain their actions and face consequences where rules are broken. Transparent budgets, independent audits, accessible records and fair complaint mechanisms can help. Transparency alone is not enough: records must be understandable and action must follow misconduct.

**Expertise and democracy complement each other.** Engineers, scientists, doctors and teachers can advise leaders; elected representatives remain accountable to the public. A degree does not automatically make someone honest or a good leader. Citizens affected by a decision should have a voice too.

**Example chain:** a village water project → publish budget and contractor details → inspect the work → allow residents to report failures → repair faults and investigate misuse. A complaint system that nobody can access is weak even if it exists on paper.

**Essay sentence to adapt:** “An honest government listens to citizens, uses expert advice and accepts scrutiny of how public money is spent.”

## 3. ★ Water: a system, not just a tap

1. **Source:** protect lakes, rivers, catchments and groundwater from contamination and overuse.
2. **Collection:** local rainwater harvesting can supplement supplies where suitable; storage and maintenance matter.
3. **Treatment:** select treatment based on tested contaminants. Boiling can kill many disease-causing organisms but does **not** remove dissolved arsenic, fluoride or most chemical pollutants.
4. **Distribution:** maintain pipes and reduce leaks; ensure equitable access and affordability.
5. **Wastewater:** treat sewage and industrial discharge, then reuse water safely where appropriate.
6. **Monitoring:** water that looks clear is not necessarily safe. Test quality and follow local public-health advice.

A river reaching the sea is not automatically wasted water: freshwater flows support estuaries, fisheries and ecosystems. Dams, withdrawals and transfers involve benefits and social/environmental trade-offs.

**Question:** Why is a household tap alone not the full solution? Because reliability, safety, source sustainability and maintenance matter too.

## 4. Education and individual talent

Foundational literacy, numeracy and scientific thinking allow students to learn further. Libraries, laboratories, sports, arts and vocational pathways give different talents room to grow. A good system supports teachers and provides accessibility for learners with disabilities; technology should complement teaching rather than replace it. Internet, device access, language and accuracy create real constraints.

**Beyond access:** being enrolled is different from understanding. A useful system checks learning, gives feedback and helps students who fall behind without treating a single exam score as the whole person.

**Practical example:** a school identifies students' interests, offers science experiments and sports alongside core subjects, and provides career guidance. Resources and trained teachers are needed to make this more than a promise.

## 5. ★ More production, less harm

Your original idea is important: **increase industrial production while improving the environment for every living organism.** Put it into practice with efficient processes, cleaner energy where feasible, pollution control, reuse and recycling, safer working conditions and enforcement. Efficiency may cut cost and pollution per unit, but total pollution can still rise if production grows faster than efficiency improves.

- **Air:** prevent emissions at source and use appropriate controls; monitor actual emissions.
- **Water:** treat effluent and reduce water use; do not assume dilution makes contaminants harmless.
- **Materials:** reduce waste, design products to last and recover materials where safe.
- **Nature:** locate projects responsibly; protect habitats and assess impacts on communities and biodiversity. Planting trees does not automatically replace a damaged river or a mature ecosystem.
- **Workers:** safety training, protective systems and fair conditions are part of development.

Self-reliance means strengthening domestic capability and resilience, not claiming that all imports or international cooperation are harmful. A country can develop local technology and also trade and learn internationally.

## 6. Dignified work and social protection

Jobs require useful skills, investment and demand for goods and services. Support small enterprises, agriculture, manufacturing, science and services; adapt training to local opportunities. Job **quality** matters: pay, safety, security and freedom from discrimination matter as well as the number of jobs.

Not everyone can work in the same way. Older people, young children, people with illness or disability, and unpaid caregivers deserve dignity and support; avoid implying that all people must be compelled to work.

## 7. Make the essay sound like you

**A4 outline:** introduction (what real development means); a short paragraph on governance and water; another on education and opportunity; another on production, environment and work; conclusion (shared responsibility and a hopeful 2047). Adjust to the organisers' actual word and time limits.

**Useful transitions:** “However,” “At the same time,” “For example,” “This will only succeed if,” “Therefore.”

**Five-minute drill:** Without looking, write five headings. Under each write one problem, one action and one benefit. Then write a two-sentence conclusion. Finally check: did you explain nature as well as economic growth?

## 8. Test your understanding

Answer in your own words before reading the hints.

1. Why can GDP growth and a polluted river happen at the same time? **Hint:** market production and environmental harm measure different things.
2. What makes an anti-corruption complaint mechanism effective? **Hint:** access, protection, investigation and action.
3. Why isn't clear water always safe? **Hint:** some dangerous microbes and chemicals cannot be seen.
4. Why does a degree alone not guarantee good governance? **Hint:** competence, integrity, accountability and public participation are distinct.
5. How can a factory produce more but cause less environmental damage? **Hint:** better technology and controls, actual monitoring and enforcement; check total as well as per-unit impacts.
6. Does self-reliance require stopping all imports? **Hint:** no; resilience and productive cooperation can coexist.
7. What is the difference between a job and decent work? **Hint:** conditions and rights matter too.
8. Why should children have opportunities beyond conventional careers? **Hint:** people have varied abilities and interests; basic education still matters.
