# Viksit Bharat 2047: Narrative Example Essays

These four **original examples** explore the same theme through a question, a journey or an imagined scene. They are written for inspiration and independent practice, not as descriptions of events that have already happened. Each is roughly suited to one A4 page in ordinary handwriting, but handwriting size and competition rules vary. Adapt the ideas in your own words, and check the actual word limit.

Your ideas run through the essays: honest and capable governance; safe water; education and individual talent; science and growing, cleaner industry; dignified work and care for the environment and living organisms. You do not need to memorise all four.

## Essay 1: What Does a Developed India Look Like?

Imagine it is the year 2047. India has completed one hundred years of independence. New industries are producing technology, cities are growing, and more people are finding opportunities. But is that enough to call India a developed nation?

To answer this question, let us begin with something simple: a glass of water.

A child opens a tap. The water is clear, but that does not necessarily mean it is safe. Its journey may have begun in a river, a lake or underground. Along the way, pollution, leaking pipes or poor treatment could make it dangerous. A developed India must protect water at every step—from its source to the homes that depend on it. Rivers must also continue to support fish, plants and the many other forms of life connected to them.

Now imagine that the same child goes to school. There, development is not just a new building or a computer. It is a good teacher, the freedom to ask questions, and the opportunity to discover one's talents. One student may become a scientist, another a sportsperson, an artist or a skilled worker. Education should help each of them build a meaningful future.

But where will these opportunities come from? Factories, farms, businesses and laboratories can create jobs and useful products. India should strengthen its industries and scientific abilities while working with the wider world. At the same time, production must become cleaner. A factory that creates jobs but poisons a river has solved one problem while creating another. Better technology, treated wastewater and responsible use of resources can help industry grow while protecting nature.

None of this happens automatically. Public money must reach the people it is meant to serve. Leaders should be honest and accountable, listen to citizens and seek advice from knowledgeable experts. Workers deserve safe conditions and fair opportunities, while people unable to work need dignity and support.

So, what does a developed India look like? It is not measured only by tall buildings or the size of its economy. It is seen in a safe glass of water, a curious student, a dignified job and a living river.

**Viksit Bharat 2047 should be a journey towards prosperity that leaves neither people nor nature behind.**

## Essay 2: The Journey of a Drop of Water

A drop of rain falls on a hillside. It joins a stream, passes fields and villages, and eventually enters a river. On its journey, it touches the lives of farmers, animals, plants and families. By the time it reaches a home, the story of that drop has become a story about India itself.

What happens if the river is polluted? A family may struggle to find safe drinking water. A farmer may worry about crops. Fish and other organisms may lose a healthy habitat. The problem cannot be solved by building a tap alone. Water sources must be protected, sewage and industrial wastewater treated, pipes maintained and water quality tested. Rainwater harvesting and careful use of groundwater can help where conditions are suitable.

But water is only one part of development. Imagine a child drinking that safe water before going to school. At school, the child should learn to read, calculate, experiment and think independently. Education must also make room for different interests, from science and technology to sports, arts and skilled work. Talent grows when it is encouraged, not when every student is forced into the same path.

After school, young people need opportunities to put their abilities to use. Farms, businesses, laboratories and factories can create dignified jobs. We should increase industrial production, develop useful technologies and strengthen India's capabilities. Yet industry must also use resources carefully, control pollution and protect workers. Economic growth and a healthy environment should support each other, not compete without limit.

Who will make sure these promises become reality? Honest leaders, knowledgeable experts and active citizens all have a part to play. Public projects should be checked, complaints heard and decisions explained. Support must also reach those who cannot work and those who face barriers to opportunity.

The little drop does not stop when it reaches a river's mouth. Freshwater flowing towards the sea helps sustain estuaries and the life around them. Just as the water cycle connects many places, development connects governance, education, work and nature.

In 2047, let us judge our progress not only by what we produce, but also by whether a child can drink safely, learn freely and look forward to a better life.

## Essay 3: A Day in India, 2047

The morning sun rises over an imagined India in 2047. In a village, a student fills a bottle with safe water before leaving for school. In a town, a worker travels to a factory. In a city, a young scientist prepares an experiment. Their lives are different, but their futures are connected.

At school, the student enters a classroom where questions are welcomed. Good teachers help children understand the world, not merely repeat answers. Some pupils practise sport; others explore science, art or a practical skill. Every child deserves a strong education and the chance to discover what they can contribute.

At the factory, machines produce useful goods for homes, hospitals and farms. More production can strengthen India's economy and create work. But the factory has another responsibility: it must protect its workers and reduce harm to the air, water and nearby living organisms. Cleaner processes, treated wastewater and careful use of materials can help. These measures must be monitored; simply claiming to be green is not enough.

In the laboratory, the scientist works on a problem that affects ordinary people. Perhaps it is a way to test water quality or use energy more efficiently. Science becomes especially valuable when its discoveries meet real needs. India can develop its own capabilities while learning and trading with other countries.

Behind these scenes is another important task: good governance. A water system, a school or a public laboratory needs money, planning and maintenance. Honest officials must use public resources responsibly. Expert advice, open information and citizens' participation can help identify mistakes and improve decisions.

Not everyone in this imagined India has the same needs. People who are ill, elderly or unable to work deserve care and support. Workers deserve dignity and safety. Development is stronger when opportunity reaches people who have been left behind.

As evening arrives, the student returns home, the worker finishes a shift and the scientist reviews the day's results. This vision is not a prediction that everything will be perfect. It is a goal we can work towards.

By 2047, India should aim to be not only more productive and technologically advanced, but also more fair, healthy and environmentally responsible. A developed nation is built one wise decision at a time.

## Essay 4: The Five Questions of Development

Suppose someone says, “India will be developed when it has more factories, more technology and more wealth.” These are important goals. But to understand the whole picture, we must ask five questions.

**First: Can people trust the system?** A nation needs leaders who are honest, capable and accountable. Public money should be spent for public benefit. Experts can help solve difficult problems, while citizens should be able to ask questions and report failures. Good governance is not merely a promise; it requires transparency and action.

**Second: Is the water safe?** A river connects villages, farms, cities and ecosystems. If it is polluted, the damage travels beyond the place where the waste was released. Protecting sources, treating wastewater, testing drinking water and maintaining pipes are all part of the solution. Clear water is not always safe water, and a healthy river supports far more than human beings alone.

**Third: Can every child learn and discover a talent?** Education should teach the foundations of reading, mathematics and scientific thinking. It should also provide opportunities in sports, arts, technology and practical skills. A student's future should not be limited by the belief that only one career is valuable.

**Fourth: Can we produce more while caring for nature?** India needs strong industries, scientific research and useful jobs. Yet if production damages soil, air and water, its costs will be paid by people and other living organisms. Efficient technology, pollution controls and responsible use of resources can help us grow more sustainably. Trees are valuable, but planting them cannot automatically repair every kind of environmental damage.

**Fifth: Does progress include everyone?** A good job should offer dignity and safe conditions. People who cannot work, and those who care for others, also deserve respect and support. The purpose of an economy is not simply to produce more; it is to improve lives.

These five questions are connected. Honest governance supports better schools and water systems. Education strengthens science and industry. Cleaner production protects the environment that sustains life. By asking these questions and acting on the answers, India can make 2047 a milestone not only of greater wealth, but of shared well-being.

## Practise writing your own

1. Choose an opening: a glass of water, a school day, a question or a journey.
2. Link that opening to the five themes. Include only the details that fit your word limit.
3. End by explaining what development means **to you**.
4. Read your essay aloud: does each paragraph connect naturally to the next?
5. Avoid invented statistics or promises that every problem will be completely solved by 2047.
