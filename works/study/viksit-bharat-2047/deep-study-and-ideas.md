# Viksit Bharat 2047: Deep Study and Ideas

A learning companion for your essay. Understand the ideas, then choose a few and express them in your own words. You do **not** need to squeeze everything into one sheet!

## Start here: the 2-minute map

Remember five connected pillars: **Honesty → Water → Education → Clean industry and innovation → Dignified jobs.**

- **Honesty:** transparent decisions, accountable officials, expert advice and fair laws.
- **Water:** conserve, treat, distribute and protect rivers and ecosystems.
- **Education:** strong foundations plus opportunities to explore interests, including sports.
- **Clean industry:** grow manufacturing and research while cutting pollution and protecting living things.
- **Jobs:** skills, fair wages, safe working conditions and support for those who cannot work.

**The big connection:** Educated people invent solutions; responsible industries create jobs; safe water and a healthy environment protect people; honest governance helps resources reach their intended purpose.

## 1. What does “developed” really mean?

India became independent in **1947**, so 2047 marks 100 years of independence. A country does not become developed merely because it has a large economy or impressive skyscrapers. Ask: **developed for whom, and at what cost?**

Consider these measures:

- **Income and living standards:** can people afford food, housing and other essentials?
- **Health:** can people access healthcare, nutrition, sanitation and safe water?
- **Education:** are children learning useful skills and able to continue studying?
- **Employment:** are jobs productive, safe and fairly paid?
- **Inclusion:** can women, persons with disabilities and disadvantaged communities share in progress?
- **Environment:** are air, water, soils and ecosystems healthy enough to support life now and in the future?
- **Governance:** can people trust institutions, obtain information and seek justice?

**Economic growth** means an increase in economic output. **Development** is broader: it asks whether people's lives and opportunities improve. Growth can help fund schools and healthcare, but it does not automatically reach everyone or protect nature.

**Sustainable development** means meeting present needs without undermining the ability of future generations to meet theirs. Think of three linked dimensions: **people, economy and environment**. [1]

### Your answer in an essay

> “For me, development means progress that reaches every person without destroying the natural systems on which life depends.”

This is an example of an argument, not a line you must memorise.

## 2. Honest government: how can corruption actually be reduced?

**Corruption** is the misuse of entrusted power for private benefit. It can include taking bribes or favouring a supplier unfairly. Imagine money allocated to repair a school roof: if part of it is diverted or poor materials are accepted in exchange for a bribe, students and taxpayers suffer.

### Tools that work together

1. **Transparency:** publish budgets, tender conditions, contract awards and progress reports so people can examine them.
2. **Accountability:** assign responsibility, conduct independent audits, investigate complaints and apply laws fairly.
3. **Access to information:** India's **Right to Information Act, 2005** allows citizens to seek information from public authorities, subject to the law's provisions and exemptions. RTI is one tool, not a guarantee that every problem will disappear. [2]
4. **Simple, accessible services:** clear rules and traceable processes can reduce some opportunities for bribery; people without internet access still need alternatives.
5. **Knowledge and participation:** decision-makers should consult experts, affected communities and independent evidence—not just people who agree with them.

### Must a minister have a PhD?

Expertise matters, but formal degrees are not the only kind. A farming policy benefits from agricultural scientists **and** farmers' practical experience. A minister also needs judgement, integrity, the ability to work with others and democratic accountability. Your idea can be expressed as: **“Choose capable leaders and ensure they receive qualified, independent advice.”** That is stronger than assuming a degree guarantees good government.

**Think deeper:** Could an honest but uninformed policy still cause harm? Could a very qualified person misuse power? What safeguards do both situations need?

## 3. Safe water: an engineering problem AND an ecological problem

### Follow a drop of water

Rain can flow into rivers and lakes, soak into the ground to recharge aquifers, evaporate, or be taken up by plants. People use surface water and groundwater for drinking, farming and industry. An **aquifer** is an underground layer of rock or sediment that can hold and transmit water.

Different places face different challenges: drought, flooding, leaking pipes, contamination or over-extraction of groundwater. A single national solution will not automatically fit every region.

### Five parts of a practical water plan

1. **Save:** collect rainwater where appropriate, fix leaks and use water efficiently.
2. **Protect sources:** prevent sewage, industrial chemicals and rubbish from entering water bodies; protect wetlands and catchments.
3. **Treat and test:** drinking water must meet applicable safety standards. Clear-looking water is not necessarily safe; it may contain microbes or dissolved contaminants.
4. **Distribute fairly:** improve pipes, storage and maintenance, including in underserved communities.
5. **Reuse safely:** treated wastewater can sometimes be reused for industry, irrigation or other purposes, depending on its treatment and safety standards. Do not assume all treated wastewater is immediately drinkable.

### Why rivers need to reach the sea

A river does more than carry water for people to use. Its flow helps support freshwater species and downstream wetlands; it also transports sediment and nutrients to deltas and estuaries. Excessive diversion can affect fisheries, habitats and communities. Therefore **“all river water entering the sea is wasted” is not scientifically sound**. Canals may be useful in some contexts, but they require environmental assessment, money, maintenance and fair sharing.

**Example to reason through:** A town needs water and a river downstream supports fishers. A proposal to divert water should examine both the town's needs and the flow needed downstream. Consider alternatives such as repairing leaks and treating wastewater, not just constructing a canal.

**Think deeper:** If a factory cleans the water it uses, should it also reduce how much water it takes in the first place? Why?

## 4. Education: the difference between access and learning

A classroom seat is important, but education should also develop **understanding, curiosity, critical thinking, character and practical skills**. A student may memorise a formula for an examination without understanding when to use it. An experiment, discussion or project can help connect theory to reality.

### Your vision: freedom with foundations

- Every child needs strong foundations in literacy, numeracy, science and other essential subjects.
- Students should also have opportunities to discover interests in sports, arts, science, technology and skilled trades.
- Access requires more than good intentions: trained teachers, safe schools, equipment, inclusion and financial support matter.
- Parents and teachers can guide students, but the student's interests and abilities deserve respect.
- Your proposal of free, high-quality sports training is a **goal to advocate**, not a claim that such training is already universally available.

**Real policy connection:** India's National Education Policy 2020 promotes experiential learning and more flexible, multidisciplinary education. A Ministry of Education framework describes learning by doing and sports- and arts-integrated approaches. A policy's goal is not proof that every school already provides it. [3]

**Think deeper:** How might a student combine physics and sports? Examples include studying projectile motion in a ball's flight or friction between shoes and the ground.

## 5. Science and technology: from curiosity to useful innovation

### Four steps

1. **Question:** What problem needs solving?
2. **Investigate:** What does evidence already show?
3. **Invent and test:** Build a prototype, measure results and identify mistakes.
4. **Improve and use responsibly:** Check cost, safety, access and environmental impact; revise based on evidence.

A laboratory discovery is not automatically a product. Moving from an idea to something people can safely use can require researchers, engineers, funding, testing, manufacturers and skilled workers.

### Examples to explore

- **Agriculture:** sensors could help farmers monitor soil conditions; farmers need affordable tools and trustworthy information.
- **Water:** monitoring and treatment systems can detect and reduce contamination; they require maintenance and testing.
- **Health:** diagnostic technology can support healthcare workers, but it must be tested for accuracy and used with care.
- **AI:** software can help analyse data or provide learning support; it can also make mistakes, reflect bias or misuse personal data. Human oversight and privacy matter.
- **Energy:** more efficient motors and equipment can reduce energy use for a given task. The overall outcome still depends on how the electricity and equipment are produced.

### Self-reliance versus isolation

**Self-reliance** can mean stronger domestic capabilities, skilled people, reliable supply chains and the ability to respond to disruptions. It is not necessarily the same as refusing to trade. Imports may provide resources or components unavailable or costly to produce locally; exports and international research partnerships can benefit India too. The challenge is to build capability while choosing trade wisely.

**Think deeper:** If a useful Indian invention depends on one imported component, is it useless? No—the goal can be to manage that dependency, develop alternatives where sensible and keep improving the product.

## 6. Industry AND nature: how to make both priorities

Factories can produce medicines, vehicles, electronics, materials and many jobs. But poorly managed production can also create polluted air, wastewater, hazardous waste and habitat loss. Your question is not “industry or environment?” but **“How can we produce more responsibly, and what limits must we respect?”**

### The factory checklist

- **Before construction:** assess location, water demand and potential effects on people, wildlife and habitats; consult affected communities.
- **During production:** use energy and water efficiently; control emissions; treat wastewater before discharge; handle hazardous waste safely.
- **Product design:** make products durable, repairable and reusable where possible. Collect and recycle materials safely at the end of their useful life.
- **Nature:** protect existing ecosystems first; restore degraded rivers, wetlands and habitats where feasible. Planting trees in the wrong place or relying on planting alone does not erase pollution or replace every lost ecosystem.
- **Enforcement:** monitor actual outcomes and enforce environmental rules, rather than trusting labels such as “green” without evidence.
- **Fair transition:** help workers and communities adapt as technologies and industries change.

**Circular economy:** Instead of simply “take → make → throw away,” reduce unnecessary resource use and keep materials in use through **repair, reuse, refurbishment and safe recycling**. Recycling itself can use energy and produce pollution, so reducing waste at the source is important too. [4]

**Biodiversity** means the variety of living organisms, including variation within species, between species and across ecosystems. It matters for living things themselves as well as for services people depend on, such as pollination and healthy soils.

### A useful trade-off

Solar panels can generate electricity without emissions during operation, but making them requires materials and energy, and projects occupy land. Responsible planning considers the **whole life cycle**—from raw materials to manufacturing, use and end-of-life management. The same principle applies to other technologies.

**Think deeper:** A company claims a factory is “green.” What evidence would you request about air, water, waste and nearby habitats?

## 7. Dignified employment: a job is more than a number

**Employment** is work that provides a livelihood; **unemployment** generally refers to people who are available for work and seeking it but cannot find it. Definitions and measurement can vary. A country's challenge is not just creating jobs, but creating **productive, safe and fairly paid** opportunities.

### Your idea, developed

- Help people gain skills through schooling, vocational education and apprenticeships.
- Support small businesses, entrepreneurship, farming, manufacturing and services.
- Improve working conditions, safety, reasonable hours and fair wages.
- Make workplaces accessible and remove discrimination. Many persons with disabilities can and want to work if barriers are removed; do not assume otherwise.
- Provide social support to people unable to work, and recognise unpaid care work as valuable even when it is not counted as paid employment.
- Treat employment as an **opportunity**, not forced labour. Training alone is not enough if suitable jobs do not exist.

**Think deeper:** A factory creates jobs but pollutes a village's drinking water. Is it successful? A stronger answer considers both livelihoods and the harm, then asks how pollution can be prevented and affected people protected.

## 9. Turn knowledge into an excellent essay

A strong paragraph can use this pattern: **Goal → Action → Benefit → Safeguard.**

Example: “I will support cleaner industries” **(goal)** “by encouraging energy efficiency and treating industrial wastewater” **(action)** “so that manufacturing can grow and create jobs” **(benefit)** “while protecting rivers and nearby communities” **(safeguard)**.

### Replace absolute claims with stronger arguments

| Overly absolute | More thoughtful |
|---|---|
| “Stop all rivers from reaching the sea.” | “Manage water efficiently while preserving river ecosystems and downstream needs.” |
| “Every minister must have a PhD.” | “Leaders should be capable, accountable and advised by qualified experts.” |
| “Import nothing.” | “Build Indian capabilities while importing and trading when useful.” |
| “A green factory causes no harm.” | “Measure and reduce pollution, resource use and habitat impacts.” |
| “Make everyone work.” | “Create dignified opportunities for those who want and are able to work, and support those who cannot.” |

### How to fit an A4 sheet

Use a brief introduction, one or two sentences for each of the five pillars and a short conclusion. Write a timed handwritten draft; margins and handwriting size vary. Choose only the knowledge that supports **your** argument.
