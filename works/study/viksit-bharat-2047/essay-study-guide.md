# Viksit Bharat 2047: Study Guide and Two Sample Essays

**Prompt:** “Five things I will do to make India the most developed country in the world by 2047.”

**Purpose:** Understand the ideas, choose your own examples, and write in your own voice. The two essays below are examples, not claims that this is the only correct answer. Follow your competition's own rules on outside help and originality.

**Want to learn more?** Read the [deep study and ideas companion](deep-study-and-ideas.md) for detailed explanations, examples, trade-offs, recall questions and sources.

## 1. The big picture

**Viksit Bharat** means *developed India*. The year **2047** marks 100 years since India became independent in 1947. Development is not only about wealth, tall buildings or technology: it also means clean water, education, honest institutions, good jobs, health and opportunities for people across the country.

Your central message could be: **India will progress when its people are educated, healthy, skilled and able to contribute.**

You have seven original ideas; combine science, self-reliance and environmentally responsible industrial production to make five:

1. Honest government and knowledgeable leadership.
2. Clean and reliable water for all.
3. Education and freedom to develop one's talents.
4. Science, self-reliance and environmentally responsible industrial growth.
5. Dignified work and support for people who cannot work.

## 2. Learn the five ideas

### 1. Honest government and capable leaders

- **Problem:** Corruption and poor decisions can waste public money and weaken trust.
- **Your solution:** Transparent decisions, accountability, fair enforcement of laws and advice from qualified experts.
- **Example:** An agriculture minister could consult agricultural scientists, experienced farmers and economists before making policy.
- **Important nuance:** A relevant degree can help, but a PhD is not proof of honesty or competence. Listening, experience and accountability matter too.
- **Useful words:** transparency, accountability, public service.

**Ask yourself:** How would honest leadership improve people's lives?

### 2. Safe water for everyone

- **Problem:** Some communities face water shortages or unsafe drinking water.
- **Your solution:** Harvest rainwater, repair leaks, treat wastewater, protect lakes and rivers, and improve safe drinking-water systems. Well-planned canals may help where appropriate.
- **Important science:** Not all river water reaching the ocean is wasted. Rivers carry sediment and nutrients and support deltas, estuaries and coastal ecosystems. Diverting too much water can harm people and nature. Canal projects need careful assessment, maintenance and fair sharing.
- **Do not promise:** that every drop can be captured or that every canal will automatically supply drinking water; water generally needs suitable treatment and testing.
- **Useful words:** conservation, treatment, sustainable management.

**Ask yourself:** How can India provide water while protecting rivers?

### 3. Education and individual talent

- **Problem:** Lack of resources or pressure from others can prevent children from developing their interests.
- **Your solution:** Strong schools and essential foundational learning, plus affordable access to science, arts, sports and vocational training.
- **Your personal point:** A student who loves sports should have a real chance to train, even if their family cannot afford coaching. Parents and teachers can guide children while respecting their interests.
- **Useful words:** equal opportunity, curiosity, skill, choice.

**Ask yourself:** What would you choose to study or practise if resources were no barrier?

### 4. Science, self-reliance and greener industry

- **Problem:** Countries need research, technology and resilient supply chains to solve problems and create opportunities.
- **Your solution:** Support scientists, labs, inventors, startups and skilled manufacturing; develop technologies for health, farming, energy and education.
- **Your personal point:** Increase Indian industrial production and make more important goods locally, while protecting and restoring the environment for people and other living organisms.
- **Ways to act:** Encourage energy-efficient factories and cleaner energy; reduce air pollution and greenhouse-gas emissions; treat industrial wastewater; reduce, reuse and safely recycle materials; protect forests, wetlands and wildlife habitats. Enforce environmental rules and monitor pollution.
- **Important trade-off:** Factories and clean-energy projects still use land, water and materials. Assess each project, prevent and reduce harm first, and restore damaged habitats where possible—planting trees alone does not undo all pollution or replace every ecosystem. Workers and local communities need a fair transition as industries change.
- **Important nuance:** Self-reliance does not require stopping all imports. Trade and international collaboration can be useful, and some materials or technologies may still need to be imported.
- **Useful words:** innovation, sustainable manufacturing, biodiversity (the variety of life), pollution prevention, self-reliance.

**Ask yourself:** How could an Indian factory produce more while using less water and energy and protecting nearby living things?

### 5. Dignified work and social support

- **Problem:** People need ways to earn a living and contribute; jobs must also be safe and fair.
- **Your solution:** Improve skills training, help small businesses grow, and create opportunities in farming, manufacturing, technology and services.
- **Your personal point:** Aim for opportunities for everyone who wants and is able to work—not forced labour or excessive working hours. Support people who cannot work and remove barriers that exclude people with disabilities.
- **Useful words:** dignity, fair wages, safe working conditions, inclusion.

**Ask yourself:** Why is a safe, fairly paid job better than a job at any cost?

## 3. Plan your own one-page essay

Handwriting size and margins vary, so **one A4 sheet has no fixed word limit**. The samples are roughly 220–250 words each; practise writing one by hand and shorten it if needed.

1. **Introduction:** Explain what a developed India means to you and why 2047 matters. (2–3 sentences)
2. **Five ideas:** Give one or two sentences per idea. Describe both the goal and one way to work towards it.
3. **Conclusion:** Bring the ideas together and end with a hopeful, personal line. (1–2 sentences)

### Helpful linking words

*First*, *second*, *another priority*, *to achieve this*, *at the same time*, *finally*, *together*.

### Before you write

- Can you explain each idea without looking at the page?
- Have you kept **five** points by combining innovation, manufacturing and environmental protection?
- Did you say **“I will”**, as the poster asks?
- Have you avoided claiming that all river water reaching the sea is wasted?
- Have you kept your promises realistic and included people's well-being?
- Does the competition allow a model or outside help? If not, use this only as study material and write independently.

## 4. Model essay — formal style

### Five Steps Towards Viksit Bharat 2047

India's hundredth year of independence in 2047 gives us a powerful goal: to build a nation where progress improves every person's life. A developed India should be educated, innovative, fair and environmentally responsible. I would work towards it in five ways.

First, I would promote honest government. Public decisions should be transparent, corruption should be addressed firmly, and leaders should listen to qualified experts as well as the people affected by their policies.

Second, I would work to ensure safe drinking water for every family. Rainwater harvesting, water treatment, repairs to leaking systems and carefully planned distribution can help. At the same time, we must protect rivers and the ecosystems that depend on their flow.

Third, I would invest in education that builds strong foundations and allows children to develop their interests. A talented child should have access to quality sports coaching, scientific learning or artistic training regardless of family income.

Fourth, I would support research and increase Indian manufacturing responsibly. Cleaner energy, efficient factories and proper waste treatment can reduce pollution, while protecting habitats and wildlife. India can also trade and cooperate with other countries when needed.

Finally, I would help create dignified jobs through skill training and support for businesses. Workers deserve fair wages, safe conditions and reasonable hours; people who cannot work deserve support.

My vision of Viksit Bharat is not just a powerful economy, but a country where opportunity, responsibility and compassion go together. Together, we can work towards that future.

## 5. Another essay — personal and direct style

### The India I Want to Build

In 2047, India will complete 100 years of independence. I dream of an India that is not only technologically advanced, but also honest, educated and caring. To help build that India, I would focus on five things.

First, I would fight corruption and demand accountable leadership. Ministers should listen to knowledgeable people in their fields. For example, decisions about farming should involve farmers and agricultural experts.

Second, I would work to bring clean drinking water to every home. We can collect rainwater, treat water properly and improve distribution. We must also protect our rivers rather than assume that water reaching the sea is wasted.

Third, I would invest in children. Every child should receive a good education and have the opportunity to explore their talents. If a child loves sports, their family's income should not decide whether they can train.

Fourth, I would invest in science and increase Indian industrial production without ignoring nature. Factories should use energy efficiently, treat their waste and protect nearby habitats. I want India to make more essential products while cooperating and importing when useful.

Fifth, I would help people find dignified work. Training, fair wages and safe workplaces can give people independence. No one should be forced to work beyond their ability, and those unable to work deserve support.

My dream is a Viksit Bharat where every person has the chance to learn, contribute and live with dignity. That is the future I want to help create.
